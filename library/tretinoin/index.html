What it isevidence librarian

Tretinoin, also called retinoic acid, is a prescription skin medicine sold as creams, gels and a liquid under names such as Retin-A. It is approved to treat acne. A cream version (Renova) was approved to lessen fine wrinkles, blotchy pigment and roughness of facial skin in people who also follow a skin-care and sun-avoidance program. Given by mouth in animal studies, tretinoin caused fetal malformations and death. So pregnancy is a key concern even with skin use.

Strong evidence

It is FDA-approved for acne. A cream version was approved for fine wrinkles and sun-related skin changes, based on two 24-week trials in 315 people under 50. The wrinkle benefits were modest.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Strong evidence

    Treats acne vulgaris (approved use; the microsphere gel is approved for people 12 and older) 13

  • Strong evidence

    Modest improvement in fine wrinkles (24% versus 8% with a plain cream), blotchy pigment (38% versus 27%) and roughness (16% versus 10%) after 24 weeks in two trials of 315 people under 50 who also used sun protection (an approved use for the Renova cream only; other tretinoin products are not approved for wrinkles); most of the effect fades after stopping 2

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Common

    Skin irritation, such as redness, dryness, peeling, burning, stinging and itching, especially early on; nearly everyone in the wrinkle trials had some, and 4% stopped because of it 12

  • Not known

    Acne can look worse in the first weeks of treatment 13

  • Not known

    Sun sensitivity: skin burns more easily, and wind or cold can add to irritation 13

  • In theory

    In hairless mice, tretinoin made ultraviolet light more likely to cause skin tumors; whether this applies to people is unknown 13

  • Not known

    Pregnancy concerns: tretinoin given by mouth caused malformations in animals, and skin application caused malformations in some rabbit studies; published studies of skin use in pregnancy have not found a link with major birth defects or miscarriage, but they have limitations and cannot rule out a risk, and the Renova label says not to use it in pregnancy 12

  • Not known

    Temporary lightening or darkening of the skin with repeated use 3

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Pregnant or trying to conceive (the Renova label says not to use it in pregnancy)
  • Eczema, or sunburned skin until it has healed (severe irritation)
  • Allergy to any ingredient
  • Breastfeeding (no data on passage into milk; a doctor can advise)

What it can clash with

  • Medicated or abrasive soaps and cleansers, and products with a lot of alcohol, astringents, spices or lime: more irritation
  • Products containing sulfur, resorcinol or salicylic acid: particular caution
  • Drugs that increase sun sensitivity, such as thiazide diuretics, tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones

Blood tests to watchmarkers agent

Before starting

None routinely needed.

While taking it

None routinely needed.

The labels reviewed call for no blood tests; their concerns are skin irritation, sun exposure and pregnancy.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. Label2000315 people under 50 with sun-damaged facial skin in two 24-week trials

    RENOVA (tretinoin cream): prescribing information

    Moderate improvement in fine wrinkling in 24% versus 8% on the plain cream base; the label says it does not eliminate wrinkles or reverse photoaging, was not shown to work in people over 50 or with darker skin, and most patients lose the effect after stopping. 2

  2. Label2025People 12 and older with acne

    Tretinoin gel (microsphere): prescribing information

    Approved for acne; skin irritation, burning, redness, peeling, dryness and itching were the most common side effects, and it can cause sun sensitivity. 1

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Which form suits my skin type, and how should I manage irritation?
  • I may become pregnant: should I use something else?
  • Can I use it with my other skin products, including acne washes and exfoliants?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

FDA-approved FDA-approved for acne; one cream for fine wrinkles. Tretinoin creams, gels and liquid are prescription drugs approved to treat acne (the microsphere gel for people 12 and older). Renova cream was approved as an add-on for fine wrinkles, blotchy pigment and roughness of facial skin; its label says it does not remove wrinkles, repair sun damage or restore younger skin. 12
