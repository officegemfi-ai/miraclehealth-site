Medication · Skin & hair

Minoxidil.

Also called Rogaine, Oral minoxidil, Loniten

Sold over the counter as a scalp treatment for hereditary hair loss, and by prescription as a tablet for severe high blood pressure. Low-dose tablets are also used for hair, which they aren't approved for.

What it isevidence librarian

Minoxidil comes in two forms. As a scalp product (Rogaine) it is sold over the counter to regrow hair on the top of the scalp in men and women with hereditary hair loss. As a tablet (Loniten) it is a powerful prescription blood pressure drug. It is reserved for severe high blood pressure that other drugs have not controlled, and it often causes extra hair growth on the body. Low-dose tablets are now prescribed off-label for hair loss.

Strong evidence

The scalp product is FDA-approved for hereditary hair loss. For low-dose tablets, one randomized trial in 90 men found them no better than the scalp form. Safety data come mainly from a large study without a comparison group.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Strong evidence

    Regrows hair on the top of the scalp in men and women with hereditary hair loss (approved over-the-counter use); results take months, and hair loss resumes when use stops 12

  • Strong evidence

    Lowers blood pressure in severe high blood pressure not controlled by a diuretic plus two other drugs (approved use of the tablets) 3

  • Moderate evidence

    Low-dose tablets (off-label for hair loss) were not better than topical minoxidil for hair density over 24 weeks in a trial of 90 men with male-pattern hair loss; photos favored the tablets at the crown 4

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Common

    Extra hair growth on the face and body: about 80% of people taking the tablets for blood pressure, 49% of men on low-dose tablets in the hair trial, and 15% in a large study of low-dose tablets for hair loss 345

  • Uncommon

    Boxed warning for the tablets: fluid around the heart (pericardial effusion) in about 3% of treated patients not on dialysis, sometimes progressing to tamponade, and new or worse chest pain (angina) 3

  • Uncommon

    Salt and water retention with swelling: 7% of patients on the tablets for blood pressure and about 1% of people on low-dose tablets for hair loss 35

  • Uncommon

    Faster heart rate and lightheadedness; with low-dose tablets for hair loss, lightheadedness affected 1.7% and a fast heartbeat 0.9% 53

  • Not known

    Scalp products: scalp irritation and unwanted facial hair; their labels list chest pain, rapid heartbeat, faintness, dizziness, sudden weight gain and swollen hands or feet as warning signs that need a doctor 12

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (scalp products may be harmful, and hair growth in newborns has been reported after exposure to the tablets in pregnancy)
  • Heart disease, including angina or a recent heart attack (needs a doctor first)
  • Pheochromocytoma, a hormone-releasing tumor (the tablets are contraindicated)
  • Under 18 (scalp products are not for children)
  • Hair loss that is sudden, patchy, linked to childbirth or of unknown cause (needs a diagnosis first)

What it can clash with

  • Guanethidine: starting minoxidil tablets can cause severe drops in blood pressure on standing
  • Other medicines used on the scalp: the scalp product labels say not to combine them

Blood tests to watchmarkers agent

Before starting

None routinely needed.

While taking it

None routinely needed.

No routine blood tests are tied to minoxidil in the sources reviewed; for the tablets, the label's concerns are heart rate, fluid retention and fluid around the heart rather than blood markers.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. RCT202490 men with male-pattern hair loss in Brazil (68 completed; 24 weeks)

    Oral Minoxidil vs Topical Minoxidil for Male Androgenetic Alopecia: A Randomized Clinical Trial

    Oral minoxidil was not superior to topical minoxidil for terminal hair density at the front or crown; photos favored oral at the crown. Extra body hair affected 49% and headache 14% of the oral group. 4

  2. Cohort20211,404 people with hair loss (67% women), retrospective, no comparison group

    Safety of low-dose oral minoxidil for hair loss: A multicenter study of 1404 patients

    Extra hair growth was the most common effect (15%); lightheadedness (1.7%), fluid retention (1.3%) and fast heartbeat (0.9%) were uncommon, and no life-threatening effects were reported in this retrospective review, which had no comparison group. 5

  3. Label2015People with severe high blood pressure

    LONITEN (minoxidil tablets): prescribing information

    Boxed warning: fluid around the heart, sometimes progressing to tamponade, and worsening angina; reserved for blood pressure not controlled by a diuretic plus two other drugs, and usually given with a beta-blocker and a diuretic. 3

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Is my hair loss hereditary, or could another cause need treatment first?
  • Would tablets or a scalp product suit me better, given my heart health and blood pressure?
  • Which side effects, such as swelling or a racing heart, should make me call you?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

Over the counter Topical OTC for hair loss; tablets Rx for blood pressure. Minoxidil scalp products are sold over the counter for hereditary hair loss on the top of the scalp, with separate products labeled for men and for women. Minoxidil tablets are prescription-only and approved only for severe, hard-to-control high blood pressure, with a boxed warning; using them for hair loss is off-label. 123
