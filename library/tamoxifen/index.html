Tamoxifen

Also called Nolvadex, Nolva, Soltamox

A prescription SERM that treats hormone-sensitive breast cancer, including in men, and lowers breast cancer risk in high-risk women; it is also used off-label for male breast enlargement and infertility.

What it is

Tamoxifen is a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM), available as tablets and as an oral solution (Soltamox). It counters estrogen's effect on estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer, but it also acts on other tissues, raising the risk of uterine cancer and blood clots. In men with low sperm counts, it raised LH, FSH, testosterone and estrogen levels.

Strong evidence

FDA-approved to treat breast cancer in adults and to lower breast cancer risk in women; for breast enlargement caused by prostate cancer drugs, a review of 4 randomized trials found it reduced breast enlargement (off-label), while trials in male infertility conflict.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Treats estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer; in men with metastatic breast cancer, about half of 132 evaluable patients responded 1

  • Strong evidence

    Lowered invasive breast cancer by 43% in 1,804 women with DCIS (ductal carcinoma in situ) after surgery and radiation 1

  • Moderate evidence

    Prevented or treated breast enlargement and breast pain caused by antiandrogen drugs for prostate cancer (risk ratio 0.10 for breast enlargement versus no treatment) and worked better than anastrozole; a 2019 European guideline says SERMs are not justified for gynecomastia in general (off-label) 34

  • Limited evidence

    Possibly more pregnancies in couples with unexplained male infertility: a 2013 meta-analysis of 11 trials of clomiphene or tamoxifen found a benefit, but a 2025 meta-analysis found the effect uncertain (off-label) 56

Risks and side effects

  • Rare

    Boxed warning: uterine cancers, stroke and pulmonary embolism, sometimes fatal, for women taking it to lower breast cancer risk (high-risk women and women with DCIS); in the prevention trial, endometrial cancer occurred at 2.20 versus 0.71 and pulmonary embolism at 0.75 versus 0.25 per 1,000 women a year. The label says that for most patients already diagnosed with breast cancer, the benefits outweigh these risks; talk to your oncologist before stopping it 1

  • Common

    Hot flashes (64% versus 48% with placebo), vaginal discharge, irregular periods and fluid retention 1

  • Uncommon

    Eye problems, including cataracts (more cataract surgery than with placebo), retinal changes and changes in color vision 1

  • Rare

    Liver problems, from enzyme changes to rare severe or fatal liver injury and liver cancer, and lower platelet counts 1

  • Not known

    In men treated for breast cancer, loss of libido and impotence led some to stop it 1

  • Not known

    Fetal harm, including birth defects and fetal death 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnancy or possible pregnancy, and breastfeeding
  • History of deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism, or taking warfarin, when the goal is lowering breast cancer risk
  • High risk of stroke or blood clots (discuss the boxed warning first)
  • Unexplained vaginal bleeding (needs prompt evaluation)
  • Past allergic reaction to tamoxifen, including angioedema

Interactions

  • Warfarin and other coumarin blood thinners: markedly stronger effect; the combination is contraindicated when tamoxifen is used to lower breast cancer risk
  • Strong CYP2D6 inhibitors such as paroxetine: may reduce tamoxifen's effect (studies are inconsistent)
  • Strong CYP3A4 inducers such as rifampin: lower tamoxifen levels and should not be combined
  • Aromatase inhibitors (anastrozole, letrozole): combining is not recommended

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Complete blood count
  • Liver function tests
  • Gynecological exam for women with a uterus
  • Cholesterol and triglycerides if already high
  • Men (off-label): testosterone, estradiol, LH and FSH

While taking it

  • Complete blood count and liver tests periodically
  • Yearly gynecological exam
  • Eye exam if vision changes
  • Men (off-label): hormones, and semen analysis if fertility is the goal

The label calls for periodic blood counts and liver tests, yearly gynecological exams and lipid checks in people who already have high lipids; for off-label use in men there is no label guidance.

Key studies

  1. Systematic review2012Men with prostate cancer taking non-steroidal antiandrogens, in 4 randomized trials

    Tamoxifen for the management of breast events induced by non-steroidal antiandrogens in patients with prostate cancer: a systematic review

    Tamoxifen sharply cut breast enlargement and breast pain compared with no treatment, and prevented them better than anastrozole or radiotherapy; long-term effects on cancer progression and survival are unclear. 3

  2. Meta-analysis2013Men with unexplained low sperm count or quality in 11 randomized trials

    Revisiting oestrogen antagonists (clomiphene or tamoxifen) as medical empiric therapy for idiopathic male infertility: a meta-analysis

    Clomiphene or tamoxifen was linked to more pregnancies (odds ratio 2.42) and better sperm concentration and motility, with no more side effects than controls. 5

  3. Meta-analysis2025Men with unexplained subfertility in 10 randomized trials

    Efficacy of clomiphene citrate and tamoxifen on pregnancy rates in idiopathic male subfertility: A systematic review and meta-analysis

    Pregnancy rates did not clearly differ between clomiphene and placebo, and tamoxifen versus placebo could not be pooled; the authors called the evidence limited and inconsistent. 6

  4. Label2020Women at high risk of breast cancer (NSABP P-1), women with DCIS (NSABP B-24) and men with metastatic breast cancer

    SOLTAMOX (tamoxifen citrate) oral solution: Prescribing Information

    In the prevention trial, uterine cancers and pulmonary emboli were more common with tamoxifen (strokes too, though not significantly); after DCIS it cut invasive cancer by 43%; about half of men with metastatic breast cancer responded. 1

Questions for your clinician

  • Given the boxed warning, how do my own risks of blood clots, stroke and uterine cancer compare with the expected benefit?
  • For men: is the cause of my breast enlargement known, and what does the evidence say for my situation?
  • Do any of my other medicines, such as antidepressants or blood thinners, interact with tamoxifen?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for breast cancer; off-label in men. Approved for metastatic breast cancer in adults (including men), adjuvant treatment of early breast cancer, lowering invasive cancer risk after DCIS, and lowering breast cancer risk in high-risk women. Use for gynecomastia or male infertility is off-label, and WADA bans SERMs, including tamoxifen, in sport. 12
