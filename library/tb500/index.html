What it is

TB-500 is a synthetic seven-amino-acid peptide (acetylated LKKTETQ) that copies amino acids 17 to 23 of thymosin beta-4, a protein found in most cells and in blood, saliva, tears and wound fluid. That stretch of thymosin beta-4 has been linked to wound repair and new blood vessel growth in lab and animal work. FDA stresses that TB-500 and thymosin beta-4 are not the same substance, and a 2024 lab study found TB-500 itself had no wound-healing effect.

Animal and lab studies

There are no human studies; the evidence is animal and lab work, mostly on the full thymosin beta-4 protein or related peptides, and a lab test of TB-500 itself found no wound-healing effect.

What it may do

  • Animal and lab studies

    Faster skin-wound closure and more collagen in aged mice treated with thymosin beta-4 or a non-acetylated peptide with the same LKKTETQ sequence 1

  • Animal and lab studies

    The LKKTETQ sequence has been reported to stimulate new blood vessel growth in lab studies 1

  • Animal and lab studies

    One TB-500 breakdown product caused a small but significant wound closure in a lab-dish test, although TB-500 itself did not 1

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    No human safety data: FDA found no clinical studies, case reports or other human exposure data by any route 1

  • In theory

    Immune reactions to the injected peptide, possibly worsened by clumping and impurities 12

  • Not known

    No animal toxicology or cancer studies to judge longer-term safety 1

  • Not known

    A report to FDA of skin and gum darkening, which returned on re-use, after injections of a product blending TB-500 with BPC-157; cause not established 5

Talk to a clinician first if you are

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (not studied in people)
  • Current or past cancer, unless a clinician advises otherwise (linked to blood vessel growth in lab work; no cancer studies)
  • Athletes subject to anti-doping rules (TB-500 is banned by WADA)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • General health check (complete blood count and metabolic panel) if a clinician is involved

While taking it

None routinely needed.

TB-500 has not been studied in people, so no evidence-based monitoring exists.

Key studies

  1. Review2026All published studies; no human studies found

    FDA Briefing Document for TB-500-Related Bulk Drug Substances (TB-500 (free base) and TB-500 acetate)

    FDA found no human studies, case reports or toxicology studies of TB-500 and proposed not adding it to the compounding list. It noted no TB-500 products are authorized in the EU, Canada, Australia or the UK. 1

  2. Cell2024Fibroblasts (connective-tissue cells) in a lab-dish scratch test, plus rats

    Simultaneous Quantification of TB-500 and Its Metabolites in In-Vitro Experiments and Rats

    TB-500 showed no wound-healing effect in the scratch test; one of its breakdown products caused a small closure. Findings as summarized by FDA. 1

  3. Animal2003Diabetic and aged mice

    Thymosin β4 and a synthetic peptide containing its actin-binding domain promote dermal wound repair in db/db diabetic mice and in aged mice

    Thymosin beta-4 and a non-acetylated LKKTETQ peptide applied to wounds increased wound closure and collagen in aged mice. Findings as summarized by FDA, which urged caution in interpreting them. 1

Status in the US

Not approved Not approved; compounding not yet allowed. FDA took TB-500 off its Category 2 safety-risk list in April 2026; in July 2026 its advisory committee voted 8-6 (1 abstention) to recommend allowing compounding for wound healing, although FDA staff had proposed not adding it. As of mid-October 2026 FDA had not added it to the compounding list or issued an interim policy, so compounding was still not permitted. 1234
