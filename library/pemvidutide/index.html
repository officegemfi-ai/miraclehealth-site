What it is

Pemvidutide is a lab-made drug that switches on glucagon and GLP-1 receptors in equal measure; its developer, Altimmune, calls it a balanced 1:1 dual agonist. Altimmune tested it for obesity, but its development now focuses on MASH (fatty-liver inflammation with scarring), alcohol use disorder and alcohol-associated liver disease. It is investigational and not approved.

Limited evidence

Company-reported phase 2 randomized trials: IMPACT (212 adults with MASH, liver biopsies at 24 weeks), MOMENTUM (391 adults with obesity, 48 weeks) and RECLAIM (about 100 adults with alcohol use disorder, 24 weeks); no phase 3 results yet. All results so far come from company announcements and filings.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    MASH cleared without worsening scarring in 52% to 58% versus 20% with placebo at 24 weeks in 212 adults with biopsy-confirmed MASH and moderate to advanced scarring; improvement in scarring itself was not statistically significant (33% to 36% versus 28%) (company-reported) 1

  • Limited evidence

    Average weight loss of 10.3% to 15.6% at 48 weeks versus 2.2% with placebo in 391 adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes (company-reported) 4

  • Limited evidence

    Fewer heavy drinking days per week than with placebo (a drop of 4.20 versus 2.75) over 24 weeks in about 100 adults with alcohol use disorder and excess weight, with 9.1% more weight loss than placebo (company-reported) 2

  • Limited evidence

    Lower liver stiffness and blood fibrosis scores than with placebo at 48 weeks in the MASH trial, without a repeat biopsy (company-reported) 51

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea and constipation: in the alcohol use disorder trial, nausea affected 44% versus 24% on placebo and constipation 26% versus 6%, and 10% stopped because of drug-related side effects versus none on placebo 2

  • Not known

    Long-term safety is not known; only phase 2 results exist 12

  • Not known

    Pemvidutide sold online as a 'research' product would be an unapproved drug of the GLP-1 type, which FDA says is illegally marketed 3

  • In theory

    Approved GLP-1 drugs carry a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents; whether this applies to pemvidutide was not reported in the sources checked 6

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Not for use outside a clinical trial: it is not an approved medicine, and "research" versions sold online are unapproved and unregulated
  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2 (approved GLP-1 drugs carry this contraindication)
  • Pregnant, breastfeeding or planning pregnancy

Interactions

  • Not well studied. Approved GLP-1 drugs raise the risk of low blood sugar with insulin or sulfonylureas and slow stomach emptying, which can change how oral medicines are absorbed

Blood markers to watch

Not an approved medicine. These are checks a clinician or a clinical trial would use, not a plan for taking it on your own.

Before starting

  • General health check: weight, blood pressure, HbA1c or fasting glucose, kidney and liver tests

While taking it

  • Same general checks at regular visits
  • Resting heart rate

No evidence-based monitoring exists outside clinical trials because it is not approved; these are general health checks, with heart rate added because the label of an approved GLP-1 drug (semaglutide) calls for it.

Key studies

  1. RCT2026212 adults with biopsy-confirmed MASH and moderate to advanced liver scarring (IMPACT phase 2b)

    Altimmune, Inc. Form 424B5 (prospectus supplement)

    At 24 weeks, MASH cleared without worsening scarring in 58.2% and 52.1% on two doses versus 19.9% on placebo; scarring improved in 32.6% and 35.7% versus 27.9% (not significant). Stopping because of side effects was under 1% to 1.2% versus 2.4%. Company-reported. 1

  2. RCT2024391 adults with obesity or overweight, without diabetes (MOMENTUM)

    Altimmune Presents Data from Phase 2 MOMENTUM Trial of Pemvidutide in Obesity during Oral Presentation at the American Diabetes Association's 84th Scientific Sessions

    Average weight loss at 48 weeks was 10.3%, 11.2% and 15.6% across three doses versus 2.2% with placebo; in an MRI substudy of 50 treated people, about 22% of the weight lost was lean mass. Company-reported. 4

  3. RCT2026About 100 adults with moderate to severe alcohol use disorder and excess weight (RECLAIM)

    Altimmune Announces Positive Topline Results from RECLAIM Phase 2 Trial of Pemvidutide in Alcohol Use Disorder

    Heavy drinking days per week fell by 4.20 versus 2.75 with placebo over 24 weeks (p=0.0014); 10% stopped because of drug-related side effects versus none. Company-reported. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • Would a clinical trial for MASH or alcohol use be an option for me?
  • How do approved options, such as semaglutide or tirzepatide, compare for me?
  • What are the risks of products sold as 'research' pemvidutide?

Status in the US

In clinical trials Investigational; phase 3 start not confirmed. Altimmune reports FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation for MASH (December 2025) and Fast Track designation for alcohol use disorder (August 2025), and planned to start a phase 3 MASH trial in the third quarter of 2026; that start was not confirmed in the sources checked. It is not approved; FDA warns that GLP-1 drugs sold 'for research purposes' are illegally marketed, though its page does not name pemvidutide. 123
