AOD-9604

Also called AOD, Advanced Obesity Drug

A lab-made fragment of human growth hormone taken for fat loss; it was developed as an obesity drug, but its main weight-loss trial failed and it is not approved.

What it is

AOD-9604 is a synthetic 16-amino-acid peptide: the tail end of human growth hormone (amino acids 177 to 191) plus an extra tyrosine. In obese mice it increased fat burning and reduced weight gain without raising blood sugar, and in lab tests it did not bind the growth hormone receptor or make cells multiply as full growth hormone does. An Australian company tested it as an obesity pill and ended that work in 2007 after a key trial failed.

Limited evidence

Fat-loss effects come from mouse and rat studies; in people, a 24-week trial of 502 adults with obesity missed its weight-loss goal, earlier small trials showed little or no effect, and FDA found no evidence that it works.

What it may do

  • Animal and lab studies

    Less weight gain and more fat burning in obese mice, without the rise in blood sugar seen with full growth hormone 6

  • Limited evidence

    Slightly more weight loss than placebo over 12 weeks in 300 adults with obesity, reported only in an abstract; FDA called the differences small and of unclear benefit, and a larger 24-week trial failed 1

  • Limited evidence

    No change in IGF-1 or glucose tolerance versus placebo in six human trials, unlike full growth hormone 5

  • Animal and lab studies

    Better knee joint healing in a rabbit study cited by FDA; FDA did not evaluate joint uses for lack of information 1

Risks and side effects

  • In theory

    Possible immune reactions to injected peptide because of clumping and unknown impurities; FDA cannot rule this out, and there are no human data for injection under the skin 13

  • Not known

    Serious adverse events that may be linked to it, identified by FDA, though cause is unclear 3

  • Common

    Headache, diarrhea and colds were common in trials of the pill form, at rates similar to placebo 5

  • Not known

    Possible signals in animal toxicity studies, including liver cell changes in monkeys and bone-marker changes in rats 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no safety data)
  • Competitive athletes (prohibited by WADA)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Key studies

  1. Review2024All published human and animal data, including a 502-person obesity trial and six developer-run trials

    Evaluation of AOD-9604-related Bulk Drug Substances (AOD-9604 (free base) and AOD-9604 acetate) for Inclusion on the 503A Bulk Drug Substances List

    FDA found a lack of evidence that AOD-9604 works for obesity: the 24-week OPTIONS trial missed its 12-week weight-loss goal, and the developer ended obesity development in 2007 without publishing it. FDA found no human data for injection under the skin and could not rule out immune reactions. 1

  2. Trial2013893 adults in six randomized, placebo-controlled trials

    Safety and Tolerability of the Hexadecapeptide AOD9604 in Humans

    Side effects were similar to placebo, with no change in IGF-1 or glucose tolerance and no antibodies detected. Weight-loss results were not reported, and the developer funded all six trials. 5

  3. Animal2001Obese and lean mice

    Increase of fat oxidation and weight loss in obese mice caused by chronic treatment with human growth hormone or a modified C-terminal fragment

    Over 14 days, both growth hormone and AOD-9604 reduced weight gain and increased fat burning in obese mice. Unlike growth hormone, AOD-9604 did not raise blood sugar or bind the growth hormone receptor. 6

Questions for your clinician

  • Which approved weight-loss options fit me, given that AOD-9604 failed its main trial?
  • What are the risks of injecting an unregulated peptide that FDA says may cause immune reactions?
  • If I compete in a tested sport, would this cause an anti-doping violation?

Status in the US

Not approved Not approved; FDA advisers voted against compounding. FDA says AOD-9604 is not part of any FDA-approved drug or official drug standard; after its compounding nomination was withdrawn, FDA reviewed it anyway, and in December 2024 FDA's advisers voted 12-0 against letting pharmacies compound it. FDA still cites possible immune reactions and serious adverse events of unclear cause, and WADA bans it in sport. 1234
