What it isevidence librarian

DHEA (dehydroepiandrosterone) is a steroid hormone made by the adrenal glands. The body turns it into androgens such as testosterone and into estrogens such as estradiol. DHEA taken by mouth is sold in the US as a dietary supplement. The same molecule, called prasterone, is FDA-approved as a vaginal insert (Intrarosa) for painful sex caused by menopause.

Limited evidence

A Cochrane review of 28 randomized trials in menopausal women found no quality-of-life benefit and at most a slight effect on sexual function. Two small trials in men found no gain in strength or muscle. Guidelines advise against routine use. Only the vaginal form is approved, and for one use.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Strong evidence

    Eases moderate to severe painful sex after menopause as a prescription vaginal insert (Intrarosa), in two 12-week placebo-controlled trials of 255 and 558 women 3

  • Limited evidence

    Slightly better sexual function than placebo in peri- and postmenopausal women (5 trials, 261 women), without better quality of life 5

  • Limited evidence

    Small improvements in quality of life and depression in people with adrenal insufficiency, in a 2009 meta-analysis of 10 studies, though the Endocrine Society still advises against routine use 6

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Common

    Acne and other male-pattern (androgenic) side effects in about 15% of women on DHEA versus under 3% on placebo; longer use in women can also cause facial hair growth 51

  • Not known

    Raises testosterone and estrogen levels; long-term effects on the breast and the lining of the uterus are unknown 6

  • Not known

    Lower HDL (good) cholesterol with oral DHEA in postmenopausal women 6

  • Uncommon

    With the vaginal insert: vaginal discharge (5.7% versus 3.7% on placebo) and abnormal Pap smears (2.1% over a year, without a placebo comparison) 3

  • Not known

    Long-term safety poorly studied: trials had a median follow-up of only about 3 months 6

  • Not known

    Some evidence suggests that even short-term use may cause harm, including liver damage; long-term safety is unknown 7

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Current or past breast cancer or another hormone-sensitive cancer (estrogen is one of its breakdown products)
  • Undiagnosed abnormal genital bleeding
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no data)
  • Competitive athletes (banned by WADA)

What it can clash with

  • Not well studied. The body converts DHEA into testosterone and estrogen, so anyone on hormone therapy or hormone-blocking treatment should check with a doctor first

Blood tests to watchmarkers agent

Before starting

  • Lipid panel
  • Breast and pelvic exams as recommended for age
  • Evaluation of any abnormal genital bleeding

While taking it

  • Lipid panel

No routine monitoring is set for supplement use; oral DHEA can lower HDL, abnormal bleeding should be checked before using the vaginal form, and guideline authors caution that DHEA-S blood tests vary by method.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. Systematic review20151,273 peri- and postmenopausal women in 28 randomized trials

    Dehydroepiandrosterone for women in the peri- or postmenopausal phase

    DHEA did not improve quality of life, had unclear effects on menopausal symptoms, may slightly improve sexual function, and caused more androgenic side effects such as acne than placebo. 5

  2. Label2018813 postmenopausal women in two 12-week trials

    INTRAROSA (prasterone) vaginal inserts prescribing information

    Prasterone inserts reduced painful sex more than placebo and improved vaginal cell measures and pH; vaginal discharge was the most common side effect. 3

  3. Guideline2014Expert panel review of evidence in women

    Androgen Therapy in Women: A Reappraisal: An Endocrine Society Clinical Practice Guideline

    Recommends against generalized use of DHEA in women and against routine use in adrenal insufficiency, citing small benefits, low-quality evidence and limited long-term safety data. 6

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Do I have a condition, such as adrenal insufficiency or painful sex after menopause, where DHEA or prasterone has evidence?
  • Should my cholesterol or hormone levels be checked before and during use?
  • Is DHEA safe with my personal or family history of breast or other hormone-sensitive cancer?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

Dietary supplement Supplement; prescription vaginal form approved. Oral DHEA is sold as a dietary supplement, which FDA does not approve for safety or effectiveness before sale. Prasterone vaginal inserts (Intrarosa) are FDA-approved for moderate to severe painful sex due to menopause, and the World Anti-Doping Agency bans DHEA in sport at all times. 1234
