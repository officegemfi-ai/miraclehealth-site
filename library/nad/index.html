What it isevidence librarian

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a molecule in the body's cells that takes part in energy metabolism. People try to raise their levels in two ways: with NAD+ itself, given by IV infusion or injection, or with precursors taken by mouth, such as nicotinamide riboside (NR) and nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN). In a small study, infused NAD+ was rapidly removed from the blood. It was probably broken down by enzymes such as CD38, with some passed in urine.

Limited evidence

The human data on NAD+ itself are a pilot infusion study of 11 men and a small clinic chart review with no health outcomes. Trials of oral precursors show higher NAD+ levels but unclear benefits.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Limited evidence

    Raised blood NAD+ during a 6-hour IV infusion in healthy men, but only after about 2 hours, because infused NAD+ was at first rapidly and completely removed from the blood (8 men on NAD+, 3 on saline) 4

  • Limited evidence

    Oral precursors such as NR and NMN can raise NAD+ in several human tissues and appear safe in trials so far, but whether this improves physical function is unclear 3

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Common

    Discomfort during IV infusion: all 6 clinic clients given IV NAD+ in a small chart review had moderate to severe cramping, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, fast heart rate or chest pressure, which stopped when the infusion ended; a 2019 pilot in 8 men reported no adverse events 54

  • Not known

    Little safety information beyond the short term: human studies are small and brief 34

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (not studied)
  • Liver or kidney disease (excluded from the infusion study; ask a doctor)

What it can clash with

  • None well documented

While taking it

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. Trial201911 healthy men aged 30 to 55 (8 NAD+, 3 saline)

    A Pilot Study Investigating Changes in the Human Plasma and Urine NAD+ Metabolome During a 6 Hour Intravenous Infusion of NAD+

    Blood NAD+ did not rise for the first 2 hours, then peaked at the end of the infusion; breakdown products rose and some NAD+ was passed in urine. No adverse events were reported, and some authors were linked to an IV NAD+ clinic. 4

  2. Case series202614 wellness-clinic clients (6 IV NAD+, 8 IV NR)

    Intravenous infusion of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) versus nicotinamide riboside (NR): a retrospective tolerability pilot study in a real-world setting

    All NAD+ clients had moderate to severe symptoms during infusions, versus mostly mild tingling or cramping with NR; blood markers stayed stable. The review was small, uncontrolled and run by the clinic company. 5

  3. Review2023Human trials of NR, NMN and related compounds

    Dietary Supplementation With NAD+-Boosting Compounds in Humans: Current Knowledge and Future Directions

    These supplements can raise NAD+ in several tissues and appear safe and tolerable, but evidence that they improve physiological function is unclear and most trials are small. 3

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Is there any evidence that NAD+ infusions would help the symptoms I want to treat?
  • What should happen if I get chest pressure, cramping or a racing heart during an infusion?
  • Would an oral precursor be a lower-risk option, and how would we judge whether it works?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

Compounded by prescription Not FDA-approved; pharmacies may compound it. FDA lists NAD in Category 1 (under evaluation) of its interim policy for substances that are not part of an FDA-approved drug and have no applicable USP monograph, so state-licensed pharmacies may compound it while FDA decides. Oral NAD+-boosting compounds such as NR and NMN are used and studied as dietary supplements. For NMN, FDA reversed its earlier position in letters dated September 29, 2025, saying NMN is not excluded from the definition of a dietary supplement (it remains a new dietary ingredient). 1236
