What it is

Taurine is a conditionally essential amino acid involved in many functions in the body, and it is a common ingredient in energy drinks. Interest in it for healthy aging grew after research found that taurine supplements improved age-related traits and extended lifespan in worms and mice. A 2025 NIH-led study, however, found that blood taurine rises or stays the same with age in people, monkeys and mice, so falling taurine is not a universal feature of aging.

Limited evidence

Lifespan benefits have been shown only in worms and mice; in people, a 2024 meta-analysis of 25 small trials found modest drops in blood pressure, blood sugar and triglycerides.

What it may do

  • Moderate evidence

    Small drops in blood pressure (about 4 mmHg systolic and 1.5 mmHg diastolic), fasting blood sugar and triglycerides, with no change in HDL cholesterol, in a 2024 meta-analysis of 25 randomized trials (1,024 people) 3

  • Animal and lab studies

    Improved age-related traits and longer lifespan in worms and mice given taurine 1

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    Long-term safety is not established: trials lasted 5 days to 1 year, though they found no significant side effects versus control 3

  • Not known

    Energy drinks that contain taurine also contain caffeine, which in large amounts may cause heart rhythm problems and raise heart rate and blood pressure; energy-drink emergency visits doubled from 2007 to 2011 2

  • In theory

    Could add to the effects of blood pressure or diabetes medicines, given its small lowering effects (not directly studied) 3

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (not studied)
  • Children and teens, when the taurine comes from caffeinated energy drinks
  • Taking blood pressure or diabetes medicines (clinician first)

Interactions

  • Caffeine and alcohol: energy drinks pair taurine with caffeine, and mixing energy drinks with alcohol is linked to binge drinking and alcohol-related injuries
  • Blood pressure and diabetes medicines: a small added lowering effect is possible (not directly studied)

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

None routinely needed.

While taking it

  • Blood pressure and blood sugar, if you take medicines for them

No routine tests are needed; because taurine slightly lowered blood pressure and blood sugar in trials, people on medicines for these may want them checked.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis20241,024 people in 25 randomized trials lasting 5 days to 1 year

    Taurine reduces the risk for metabolic syndrome: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

    Taurine modestly lowered systolic and diastolic blood pressure, fasting glucose and triglycerides, did not change HDL cholesterol, and caused no significant side effects versus control. 3

  2. Cohort2025Three human cohorts (ages 20 to 100), rhesus monkeys and mice

    Is taurine an aging biomarker?

    Blood taurine rose or stayed the same with age rather than falling, varied more between individuals than over time, and was inconsistently linked to muscle strength, body weight and energy measures. 4

Questions for your clinician

  • Is there anything about my heart, blood pressure or blood sugar that makes taurine or energy drinks a concern?
  • Would any of my medicines add to taurine's blood pressure or blood sugar effects?
  • Which proven steps should come first for my energy or healthy-aging goals?

Status in the US

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement; common energy-drink ingredient. Sold as a dietary supplement and added to energy drinks. NIH said in 2025 that despite lifespan gains in worms and mice, there is no solid clinical data showing taurine supplements benefit people; a 2024 meta-analysis found only small drops in blood pressure, blood sugar and triglycerides. 123
