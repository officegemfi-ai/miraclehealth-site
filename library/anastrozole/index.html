Anastrozole

Also called Arimidex, Adex, Aromatase inhibitor, AI

A prescription pill that blocks estrogen production, approved to treat hormone-sensitive breast cancer after menopause; it has also been studied off-label in men with low testosterone.

What it is

Anastrozole is a nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor: it blocks aromatase, the enzyme that converts androgens into estrogen. In postmenopausal women it lowers estradiol by about 70% within a day and about 80% after two weeks, without affecting cortisol or aldosterone. In men it raises testosterone and lowers estradiol.

Strong evidence

FDA-approved for hormone receptor-positive breast cancer after menopause; the evidence in men comes from a few small randomized trials that showed rises in testosterone but no bone benefit.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Treats hormone receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women, both early (after surgery) and advanced 1

  • Moderate evidence

    Lowered invasive breast cancer from 3% to 2% over 5 years in 3,864 high-risk postmenopausal women in the IBIS-II trial (off-label for prevention in the US) 23

  • Moderate evidence

    Raised testosterone over one year in 69 older men with low or borderline-low testosterone, but did not improve bone health (off-label) 4

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Hot flashes (36%), weakness, joint pain and stiffness, and depression in the main breast cancer trial 1

  • Common

    Bone thinning and fractures: osteoporosis 11% and fractures 10% in the main trial, more than with tamoxifen; in older men, spine bone density fell compared with placebo 14

  • Uncommon

    Higher cholesterol (9% versus 3.5% with tamoxifen) 1

  • Common

    More heart events than with tamoxifen in women with existing ischemic heart disease (17% versus 10%) 1

  • Not known

    In men, blocking estrogen raised body fat and contributed to lower sexual desire and function (400 healthy men, 16 weeks) 5

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnancy or possible pregnancy
  • Premenopausal women (it is approved only after menopause)
  • Osteoporosis or high fracture risk without a bone-protection plan
  • Existing ischemic heart disease (discuss the higher heart-event rate first)
  • Past allergic reaction to anastrozole

Interactions

  • Tamoxifen: lowers anastrozole levels by 27% and adds no benefit, so the two are not used together
  • Estrogen-containing products: can weaken its effect

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Bone density (DEXA) scan
  • Cholesterol (lipid panel)
  • Pregnancy test if pregnancy is possible
  • Men (off-label): testosterone and estradiol

While taking it

  • Bone density at intervals
  • Cholesterol periodically
  • Men (off-label): testosterone and estradiol

The label advises considering bone density and cholesterol monitoring because anastrozole can thin bone and raise cholesterol; there is no label guidance for men, where trials tracked testosterone, estradiol and bone density.

Key studies

  1. RCT20143,864 postmenopausal women at high risk of breast cancer

    Anastrozole for prevention of breast cancer in high-risk postmenopausal women (IBIS-II): an international, double-blind, randomised placebo-controlled trial

    Over 5 years, invasive breast cancer occurred in about 2% with anastrozole versus about 3% with placebo; death rates did not differ. 3

  2. RCT2013400 healthy men aged 20 to 50

    Gonadal steroids and body composition, strength, and sexual function in men

    With their own hormones suppressed, a cohort also given anastrozole showed that estrogen deficiency mainly accounted for increases in body fat and contributed to lower sexual function, while low testosterone drove losses in lean mass and strength. 5

  3. RCT200969 men aged 60 and older with low or borderline-low testosterone

    Effects of Aromatase Inhibition on Bone Mineral Density and Bone Turnover in Older Men with Low Testosterone Levels

    One year of anastrozole raised testosterone and modestly lowered estradiol, but spine bone density fell compared with placebo; the authors concluded it does not improve skeletal health in aging men. 4

  4. Label2025Postmenopausal women with early breast cancer (ATAC trial, compared with tamoxifen)

    ARIMIDEX (anastrozole) tablets: Prescribing Information

    Anastrozole caused more joint symptoms, osteoporosis and fractures than tamoxifen, and raised cholesterol more often (9% versus 3.5%). 1

Questions for your clinician

  • Do I need a bone density scan before starting, and how will we protect my bones?
  • For men: is there a medical reason to lower my estrogen, given its role in bone, body fat and sexual function?
  • Which joint, muscle or mood symptoms should I report?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for breast cancer; off-label in men. Approved for postmenopausal women with hormone receptor-positive early breast cancer (after primary treatment), as first-line treatment of advanced breast cancer, and for advanced disease that progressed on tamoxifen. Use for breast cancer prevention or in men is off-label. 1
