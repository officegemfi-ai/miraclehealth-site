What it is

IGF-1 LR3 (Long R3 IGF-1) is an engineered version of insulin-like growth factor 1, the hormone that carries out some of growth hormone's effects, including on muscle. It has arginine in place of glutamic acid at position 3 plus a 13-amino-acid extension at the front end; these changes make it bind poorly to the IGF-binding proteins that normally hold IGF-1 in check, so it acts more strongly. Natural IGF-1 (mecasermin) is sold as a medicine for rare conditions such as primary IGF-1 deficiency, but the LR3 analog is not approved for human use.

Animal and lab studies

Its extra potency comes from lab studies; no human studies were identified, and a review notes little evidence that IGF-1 improves performance in healthy people.

What it may do

  • Animal and lab studies

    Stronger IGF-1 activity than natural IGF-1 in lab studies, because it binds poorly to IGF-binding proteins 5

  • Anecdotal

    Taken by athletes for muscle growth, but a review found little information on whether IGF-1 improves performance in healthy people 5

Risks and side effects

  • In theory

    Low blood sugar: the approved IGF-1 drug (mecasermin) warns of severe low blood sugar, including seizures, and 42% of children in its trials had low blood sugar at least once; this form is built to be more potent 7

  • Not known

    Effects in people are unknown: no human studies of the analog were identified, and data on IGF-1 in healthy people are scarce 5

  • In theory

    Escapes the binding proteins that normally buffer IGF-1, which makes it more potent; the consequences in people are untested 5

  • Not known

    Lab-only IGF-1 products have reached athletes and black-market supplements have contained IGF-1 analogs, so identity and purity are uncertain 5

  • In theory

    Approved drugs that raise IGF-1, such as tesamorelin, carry label warnings about tumor growth in people with cancer 6

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Current or past cancer
  • Diabetes or insulin use (clinician first)
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no safety data)
  • Competitive athletes (prohibited by WADA at all times)

Interactions

  • Insulin and other blood-sugar-lowering medicines: IGF-1 lowers blood sugar, so the effects could add up (the approved IGF-1 drug's patient information asks about insulin and anti-diabetes medicines)

Status in the US

Not approved Not approved; not on FDA's compounding lists. IGF-1 LR3 is not an approved drug and does not appear on FDA's 503A or 503B lists of nominated bulk substances, so pharmacies have no recognized basis to compound it. WADA bans IGF-1 and its analogues in sport at all times. 1234
