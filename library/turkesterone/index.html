What it is

Turkesterone is a phytoecdysteroid, one of a group of steroid compounds made by plants and insects that are structurally similar to androgens (male hormones). It makes up most of the ecdysteroids isolated from Ajuga turkestanica; plants rich in these compounds have been used as tonics and to promote muscle growth. Lab and animal research on a related compound, ecdysterone, suggests these steroids act through estrogen-receptor pathways rather than as androgens.

Limited evidence

Turkesterone itself has been tested only in a 4-week trial of 31 adults and single-dose studies in 11 men, all reported as conference or student abstracts, and none found an effect.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    No benefit shown: no difference from placebo in body weight, lean mass or body fat after 4 weeks in a randomized, double-blind trial of 31 active adults (reported as a student symposium abstract) 4

  • Limited evidence

    No effect shown on IGF-1, a growth-related hormone, or on resting metabolism in the hours after single doses in 11 recreationally active men 15

  • Limited evidence

    In a strength-training study, men given ecdysterone, a related plant steroid, gained more muscle mass than those who were not; turkesterone itself has not been tested this way 63

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Products often don't match their labels: in a 2023 test of products bought online, 2 of 3 labeled as turkesterone held about 1% and 12% of the stated amount, and a 2026 study again found wide variation 62

  • Not known

    For tested athletes: some turkesterone-labeled products contained as much or more ecdysterone, which WADA monitors as a possible anabolic agent 23

  • Not known

    Safety in people is essentially unstudied beyond 4 weeks; no stomach upset was reported after single doses in 11 men 14

  • In theory

    Effects on hormones in people are unknown; related ecdysteroids are thought to act through estrogen-receptor pathways 6

  • Not known

    Hidden drugs: FDA says supplements found to contain hidden prescription drugs or controlled substances such as anabolic steroids are typically promoted for bodybuilding, weight loss, sexual enhancement or diabetes 7

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (not studied)
  • Athletes subject to drug testing (check with your anti-doping organization first)
  • Hormone-sensitive conditions (clinician first)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Key studies

  1. RCT202531 physically active adults (14 men, 17 women)

    The Effects of Turkesterone Supplementation on Body Composition

    After 4 weeks, body scans (DEXA) showed no differences from placebo in body mass, lean mass, fat mass or percent body fat. Reported as a student symposium abstract, not a peer-reviewed paper. 4

  2. Trial202311 recreationally active men (single-blind, placebo-controlled crossover)

    Influence of Acute Turkesterone Dosing on Serum Insulin-like Growth Factor 1 (IGF-1) and Subjective Digestibility Scores in Recreationally-active Males

    Single doses did not change IGF-1 at 3 or 24 hours, and no one reported stomach upset. 1

  3. RCT202311 recreationally active men (randomized, single-blind, placebo-controlled crossover)

    Influence of Acute Turkesterone Dosing on Resting Metabolic Rate and Substrate Utilization in Recreationally-active Males

    Over 3 hours after single doses, resting metabolic rate and fat and carbohydrate burning did not change significantly versus placebo. 5

Questions for your clinician

  • Which proven approaches to building muscle should I focus on first?
  • If I compete in tested sport, could a mislabeled product put me at risk?
  • Could a plant steroid affect any hormone-related condition I have?

Status in the US

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement. Sold as a dietary supplement that has grown popular with recreationally active people. As of March 2026 it was not named on WADA's Prohibited List or Monitoring Program, but WADA's list of anabolic agents is not exhaustive, and the related ecdysterone has been on its Monitoring Program since 2020. 123
