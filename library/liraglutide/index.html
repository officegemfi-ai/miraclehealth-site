What it is

Liraglutide is a lab-made version of GLP-1, a natural hormone that helps regulate blood sugar and appetite. It switches on GLP-1 receptors and slows stomach emptying. Victoza and Saxenda contain the same drug for different uses, and generic versions are approved. It is given as an injection under the skin.

Strong evidence

Large randomized trials, including SCALE (3,731 adults) for weight and LEADER (9,340 adults) for heart outcomes, plus FDA approval for each main use.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Lower blood sugar in adults and children 10 and older with type 2 diabetes; A1C fell about 0.9 to 1.4 points more than placebo when added to other diabetes drugs 1

  • Strong evidence

    Average weight loss of 8.4 kg versus 2.8 kg with placebo over 56 weeks in 3,731 adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes; 63% lost at least 5% of body weight versus 27% 72

  • Strong evidence

    Fewer heart attacks, strokes and cardiovascular deaths in adults with type 2 diabetes at high heart risk, most with heart disease: 13.0% versus 14.9% over a median of 3.5 years (hazard ratio 0.87) 12

  • Strong evidence

    Approved for weight management in children 12 and older with obesity 2

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Nausea (39% versus 14% on placebo), diarrhea, constipation and vomiting in weight-management trials 2

  • Common

    Faster resting heart rate: on average 2 to 3 beats per minute higher, and 34% versus 19% on placebo had rises of more than 10 beats per minute at two visits in a row 82

  • Uncommon

    Gallstones (2.2% versus 0.8% on placebo) and gallbladder inflammation (0.8% versus 0.4%) 82

  • Rare

    Acute pancreatitis (0.3% versus 0.1% on placebo) 82

  • In theory

    Boxed warning: thyroid C-cell tumors in rats and mice; whether this happens in humans is unknown 21

  • Rare

    Breathing stomach contents into the lungs under general anesthesia or deep sedation (a labeled warning) 21

Talk to a clinician first if you are

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • Past serious allergic reaction to liraglutide
  • Pregnant or planning pregnancy (discuss with a clinician first)

Interactions

  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Oral medicines: slower stomach emptying can change how they are absorbed
  • Other liraglutide products or other GLP-1 drugs: using them together is not recommended

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • HbA1c or fasting glucose
  • Kidney function (creatinine, eGFR)
  • Resting heart rate

While taking it

  • Resting heart rate at regular visits
  • Blood glucose if also taking insulin or a sulfonylurea
  • Kidney function if vomiting or diarrhea is severe

The labels warn about heart-rate increases, low blood sugar with insulin or sulfonylureas, and kidney injury from dehydration; they say routine calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound checks are of uncertain value.

Key studies

  1. RCT20153,731 adults with obesity or overweight, without diabetes

    A Randomized, Controlled Trial of […] Liraglutide in Weight Management

    Over 56 weeks, weight fell 8.4 kg with liraglutide versus 2.8 kg with placebo, and 63.2% versus 27.1% lost at least 5% of body weight. Mild or moderate nausea and diarrhea were the most common side effects. 7

  2. Label20259,340 adults with type 2 diabetes and heart disease or, for about 1 in 5, other heart risk factors (LEADER trial)

    VICTOZA (liraglutide) injection, for subcutaneous use: Prescribing Information

    Liraglutide lowered the risk of a first major heart event versus placebo (hazard ratio 0.87, 95% CI 0.78 to 0.97). 1

Questions for your clinician

  • Would a newer GLP-1 medicine suit me better than liraglutide?
  • Which symptoms, such as severe stomach pain or a racing heart, mean I should call you?
  • Should I tell my surgeon or anesthesia team before any procedure with sedation?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for diabetes, heart risk and weight. Approved as Victoza (type 2 diabetes from age 10, and lowering heart risk in adults with type 2 diabetes and heart disease) and Saxenda (weight management from age 12), with generics approved since December 2024; Saxenda's suicidal-thoughts warning was removed in early 2026. FDA listed liraglutide injection as in shortage in early 2025 (current status not confirmed), and in May 2026 proposed keeping it off the outsourcing-facility (503B) bulks list. 123456
