What it is

Cagrilintide is a lab-made, long-acting analog of the hormone amylin, developed by Novo Nordisk. In its main trials it is combined with semaglutide, a GLP-1 drug, as CagriSema, and Novo Nordisk asked FDA in December 2025 to approve CagriSema for weight management. No product containing cagrilintide is approved in the US, and FDA says it cannot be used in compounding.

Moderate evidence

Two large published phase 3 trials (3,417 and 1,206 adults) support cagrilintide combined with semaglutide; evidence for cagrilintide alone rests mainly on one 26-week phase 2 trial, and long-term safety is unknown.

What it may do

  • Moderate evidence

    With semaglutide (CagriSema): average weight loss of 20.4% versus 3.0% with placebo over 68 weeks in 3,417 adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes 45

  • Moderate evidence

    With semaglutide in adults with type 2 diabetes and excess weight: 13.7% versus 3.4% weight loss over 68 weeks, and 74% reached an A1C of 6.5% or lower versus 15.9% with placebo 5

  • Moderate evidence

    Alone: 6.0% to 10.8% average weight loss over 26 weeks, depending on dose, versus 3.0% with placebo, in a phase 2 trial of adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes 6

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Nausea and other digestive side effects: 79.6% with CagriSema versus 39.9% with placebo; in the phase 2 trial of cagrilintide alone, 4% stopped treatment because of side effects 46

  • In theory

    CagriSema contains semaglutide, whose label carries a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents (human relevance unknown) 7

  • Not known

    Pancreatitis, gallbladder disease and a faster heart rate occur with semaglutide, one of CagriSema's two drugs, and are warnings on its label; how often they occur with cagrilintide is not known 7

  • Not known

    Long-term safety is not known; it is not an approved medicine 23

  • Not known

    Cagrilintide sold online or as a 'research' product is unapproved, and FDA says it cannot be used in compounding 3

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Not for use outside a clinical trial: it is not an approved medicine, and "research" versions sold online are unapproved and unregulated
  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2 (a contraindication for semaglutide, which CagriSema contains)
  • Pregnant, breastfeeding or planning pregnancy

Interactions

  • Not well studied. The semaglutide in CagriSema raises the risk of low blood sugar with insulin or sulfonylureas and slows stomach emptying, which can change how oral medicines are absorbed

Blood markers to watch

Not an approved medicine. These are checks a clinician or a clinical trial would use, not a plan for taking it on your own.

Before starting

  • General health check: weight, blood pressure, HbA1c or fasting glucose, kidney function

While taking it

  • Same general checks at regular visits
  • Resting heart rate

No evidence-based monitoring exists outside clinical trials because it is not approved; these are general health checks, with heart rate added because the semaglutide label calls for it.

Key studies

  1. RCT20253,417 adults with obesity or overweight, without diabetes (REDEFINE 1)

    Coadministered Cagrilintide and Semaglutide in Adults with Overweight or Obesity

    At 68 weeks, average weight change was -20.4% with cagrilintide-semaglutide versus -3.0% with placebo; digestive side effects affected 79.6% versus 39.9%. 4

  2. RCT20251,206 adults with type 2 diabetes and overweight or obesity (REDEFINE 2)

    Cagrilintide-Semaglutide in Adults with Overweight or Obesity and Type 2 Diabetes

    At 68 weeks, weight fell 13.7% versus 3.4% with placebo, and 74% versus 15.9% reached an A1C of 6.5% or lower (as summarized by the American College of Cardiology). 5

  3. RCT2021706 adults with obesity or overweight, without diabetes

    Once-weekly cagrilintide for weight management in people with overweight and obesity: a multicentre, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled and active-controlled, dose-finding phase 2 trial

    Over 26 weeks, average weight loss was 6.0% to 10.8% across cagrilintide doses versus 3.0% with placebo; 4% stopped treatment because of adverse events. 6

Questions for your clinician

  • Would a clinical trial be an option for me?
  • How do approved options, such as semaglutide or tirzepatide, compare for me?
  • What are the risks of products sold as 'research' cagrilintide?

Status in the US

In clinical trials Investigational; CagriSema under FDA review. Novo Nordisk submitted CagriSema (cagrilintide plus semaglutide) to FDA for weight management in December 2025, and no FDA decision was found as of late October 2026. FDA states that cagrilintide 'cannot be used in compounding under federal law.' 123
