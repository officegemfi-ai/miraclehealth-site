What it is

Testagen is a short synthetic peptide developed at Vladimir Khavinson's institute in St. Petersburg and listed in Russia as a dietary supplement for men. Its Russian listing describes a peptide complex made from four amino acids: lysine, glutamic acid, aspartic acid and glycine. Its developers say it acts specifically on testicular tissue, supporting sperm production and testosterone, but this rests on their own rat and human studies.

Limited evidence

Rat studies and one small randomized study in 48 older men, all reported by the developers in a Russian drug-reference article rather than a peer-reviewed journal.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Higher blood testosterone and better sperm count, movement and shape in a small study of 48 men aged 54 to 68 with age-related symptoms, described as randomized but with few details on methods 5

  • Limited evidence

    Better well-being and sexual activity reported by men in the same study 5

  • Animal and lab studies

    Less radiation damage to sperm production and fertility in male rats 5

  • Animal and lab studies

    Stimulated growth of testicular tissue from young and old rats in lab culture, with no clear effect on brain, heart, liver, kidney or prostate tissue 5

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    The developers report no side effects, but no independent human safety data exist 54

  • Not known

    No FDA-approved or pharmacy-compounded version exists, so the identity and purity of products are unverified 13

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Prostate or breast cancer, or another hormone-sensitive condition
  • Known fertility problems, unless a fertility specialist is involved
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (never studied)
  • Under 18 (never studied)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Not an approved medicine. These are checks a clinician or a clinical trial would use, not a plan for taking it on your own.

Before starting

  • Morning total testosterone, with LH and FSH, if low testosterone is the concern
  • Semen analysis if fertility is the concern

While taking it

None routinely needed.

No evidence-based monitoring exists for Testagen; the developers' study tracked testosterone, LH, FSH and semen quality.

Key studies

  1. Trial202148 men aged 54 to 68 with age-related (climacteric) symptoms: 27 given Testagen plus standard care, 21 standard care alone

    Клинико-экспериментальное изучение пептидного биорегулятора Тестагена (Clinical and experimental study of the peptide bioregulator Testagen)

    The Testagen group had a significant rise in testosterone and better semen measures, and no side effects were reported. The developers' report gives few details on how the study was run. 5

Questions for your clinician

  • Has my testosterone been confirmed low with proper morning tests, and what is causing it?
  • Are there proven treatments for my symptoms or fertility goals?
  • Could this affect my fertility tests or any treatment I am on?

Status in the US

Not approved Not FDA-approved; not on FDA compounding lists. Testagen is not an FDA-approved drug and appears on none of FDA's compounding lists (nominated substances, updated May 2026; safety-risk and withdrawn nominations, April 2026). FDA allows pharmacy compounding only from ingredients that are in an approved drug, meet a USP standard, or are on or under review for its bulk list; in Russia it is listed as a dietary supplement. 1234
