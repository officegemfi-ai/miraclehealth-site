Empagliflozin

Also called Jardiance, SGLT2 inhibitor

A prescription pill, sold as Jardiance, that makes the kidneys pass extra sugar into the urine; it treats type 2 diabetes and lowers heart failure and kidney risks.

What it is

Empagliflozin is an SGLT2 inhibitor. It blocks SGLT2, the main protein in the kidneys that takes filtered sugar back into the blood, so more sugar leaves in the urine and blood sugar falls. Beyond type 2 diabetes, it is approved to lower risks in heart failure and chronic kidney disease, and those uses do not require diabetes. Its half-life is about 12 hours.

Strong evidence

FDA approval for each main use, based on large randomized trials including EMPA-REG OUTCOME (7,020 adults), EMPEROR-Reduced and EMPEROR-Preserved (9,718 adults with heart failure) and EMPA-KIDNEY (6,609 adults with kidney disease).

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Fewer cardiovascular deaths in adults with type 2 diabetes and heart disease: 3.7% versus 5.9% with placebo over a median of 3.1 years (hazard ratio 0.62) 21

  • Strong evidence

    Fewer cardiovascular deaths or heart failure hospitalizations in adults with heart failure: 19.4% versus 24.7% with placebo when the heart's pumping (ejection fraction) was reduced, and 13.8% versus 17.1% when it was above 40% 31

  • Strong evidence

    Lower risk of lasting kidney function decline, kidney failure, cardiovascular death and hospitalization in adults with chronic kidney disease at risk of progression (EMPA-KIDNEY, 6,609 adults) 1

  • Strong evidence

    Lower blood sugar in adults and children 10 and older with type 2 diabetes: A1C about 0.4 to 0.9 points lower than with placebo, with about 2% to 3% more weight loss (not approved for weight loss) 1

Risks and side effects

  • Uncommon

    Genital yeast infections: about 5% to 6% of women versus 1.5% on placebo, and 2% to 3% of men versus 0.4% 1

  • Uncommon

    Urinary tract infections (7.6% to 9.3% versus 7.6% on placebo), including serious kidney or blood infections that have needed hospital care 1

  • Rare

    Ketoacidosis, a dangerous buildup of blood acids that can occur even when blood sugar is not very high; risk is much higher in type 1 diabetes, and fatal cases have been reported in type 2 diabetes 1

  • Rare

    Dehydration and low blood pressure (0.3% to 0.5% versus 0.3% on placebo in pooled trials), more likely in older adults, people with kidney impairment or those taking loop diuretics; acute kidney injury, some cases needing hospital care or dialysis, has been reported after marketing 1

  • Rare

    Fournier's gangrene, a rare but serious infection of the tissue around the genitals and anus 1

  • Rare

    Lower-limb amputations: 5.0 versus 4.3 per 1,000 patient-years across four outcome trials, not a significant difference (hazard ratio 1.05); the label still advises watching for foot sores and infections 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Type 1 diabetes (not recommended for blood sugar control because of ketoacidosis risk)
  • Past serious allergic reaction to empagliflozin
  • Kidney function below an eGFR of 30 when the goal is blood sugar control (not recommended)
  • Polycystic kidney disease, or recent strong immune-suppressing treatment for kidney disease (not expected to help the kidneys)
  • Second or third trimester of pregnancy, or breastfeeding (not recommended)

Interactions

  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Diuretics, especially loop diuretics: more urine and a higher risk of dehydration
  • Lithium: levels can fall, so lithium needs closer monitoring
  • Urine glucose tests and the 1,5-AG blood test: give misleading results while taking it

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Kidney function (creatinine, eGFR)
  • Blood pressure and fluid status, especially in older adults or with diuretics
  • HbA1c or fasting glucose if you have diabetes

While taking it

  • Kidney function as needed (a small early dip in eGFR is expected and usually stabilizes)
  • Ketones if ketoacidosis is suspected, and considered in type 1 diabetes
  • Lithium levels if you take lithium
  • HbA1c to track blood sugar if you have diabetes

The label calls for checking kidney function and fluid status before starting and as needed afterward, and for considering ketone checks in people at risk of ketoacidosis; urine glucose and 1,5-AG tests are unreliable on this drug.

Key studies

  1. Label20177,020 adults with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular disease (EMPA-REG OUTCOME)

    JARDIANCE (empagliflozin tablets), for oral use: Prescribing Information

    Cardiovascular death occurred in 3.7% versus 5.9% with placebo over a median of 3.1 years (hazard ratio 0.62, 95% CI 0.49 to 0.77); heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death occurred in 10.5% versus 12.1% (hazard ratio 0.86). 2

  2. Label20229,718 adults with heart failure (EMPEROR-Reduced, 3,730; EMPEROR-Preserved, 5,988)

    JARDIANCE (empagliflozin tablets), for oral use: Prescribing Information

    Cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalization: 19.4% versus 24.7% (hazard ratio 0.75) with ejection fraction 40% or below over a median of 16 months, and 13.8% versus 17.1% (hazard ratio 0.79) with ejection fraction above 40% over a median of 26 months. 3

  3. Label2025Adults in four cardiovascular and kidney outcome trials

    JARDIANCE (empagliflozin tablets), for oral use: Prescribing Information

    Lower-limb amputations occurred at 5.0 versus 4.3 per 1,000 patient-years with placebo (hazard ratio 1.05, 95% CI 0.81 to 1.36). 1

Questions for your clinician

  • Should I pause it before surgery or a procedure that requires fasting?
  • Which symptoms of ketoacidosis or a genital infection should make me call you right away?
  • Does my diuretic or lithium need extra monitoring while I take it?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for diabetes, heart failure and kidney disease. Approved as Jardiance to lower cardiovascular death and heart failure hospitalization in adults with heart failure, to slow kidney decline and lower related risks in adults with chronic kidney disease at risk of progression, to lower cardiovascular death in adults with type 2 diabetes and heart disease, and to improve blood sugar in adults and children 10 and older with type 2 diabetes. It has no boxed warning, and it is not recommended for blood sugar control in type 1 diabetes because of ketoacidosis risk. 1
