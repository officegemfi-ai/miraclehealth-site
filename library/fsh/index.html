What it is

Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) is a pituitary hormone that drives egg development in women and sperm production in men. Follitropin alfa (Gonal-f) and follitropin beta (Follistim AQ) are recombinant human FSH made in Chinese hamster ovary cells. In men, it is approved, together with hCG, only to start sperm production when the pituitary or hypothalamus fails to drive the testes (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism). Both products' approvals were converted to biologics licenses on March 23, 2020.

Strong evidence

FDA-approved for female infertility and, with hCG, for starting sperm production in men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, though the male trials were small and open-label; for unexplained male infertility (off-label), a Cochrane review rated much of the evidence very low quality.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    With hCG, started sperm production in men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and no sperm: 46% to 80% reached the target sperm density across three Gonal-f trials, and 43% of 30 men in the Follistim AQ trial, after a median of about 5 to 12 months 12

  • Strong evidence

    Induced ovulation in anovulatory women: 58% ovulated in the first cycle, rising to 81% by the third (118 women) 1

  • Limited evidence

    Across 44 studies of gonadotropin therapy in men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, combining hCG with FSH was linked to higher success than other regimens 4

  • Limited evidence

    In men with unexplained infertility, gonadotropins raised spontaneous pregnancy rates from 7% to 16% (5 trials, 412 couples), but live-birth evidence is very low quality; the AUA/ASRM guideline says FSH may be considered, though its cost-effectiveness is questionable (off-label) 56

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    In men: acne (27%), injection-site pain (11%), fatigue (10%) and breast enlargement (6%; one case needed surgery) 1

  • Not known

    In women: ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome, which can be serious, progress rapidly and start after treatment stops 12

  • Not known

    Multiple pregnancies and births 12

  • Not known

    Blood clots in veins or arteries and lung complications, more likely in women with clotting risk factors 12

  • Not known

    Allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis 1

  • Not known

    Ovarian torsion, ectopic pregnancy and miscarriage 12

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • High FSH showing primary failure of the ovaries or testes, where it cannot work
  • Uncontrolled thyroid, adrenal or pituitary disorders
  • Sex hormone-dependent tumors, or tumors of the pituitary or hypothalamus
  • Pregnancy, or vaginal bleeding or ovarian cysts of unknown cause
  • Allergy to recombinant FSH products, or to streptomycin or neomycin (Follistim AQ)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Men: testosterone, LH and FSH, to confirm low pituitary hormones rather than testicular failure
  • Men: semen analysis
  • Women: pregnancy test and FSH
  • Thyroid, adrenal or pituitary tests if a disorder is suspected

While taking it

  • Men: semen analysis and testosterone
  • Women: ovarian response monitoring by a fertility specialist

The labels exclude primary gonadal failure (high FSH) and uncontrolled gland disorders; trials in men tracked sperm density in the ejaculate.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis2013456 men or couples with unexplained male subfertility in 6 randomized trials

    Gonadotrophins for idiopathic male factor subfertility

    Spontaneous pregnancy was more common with gonadotropins (16% versus 7%); one small trial reported live births (27% versus 0%), and the authors called the evidence insufficient for firm conclusions. 5

  2. Meta-analysis2014Men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and no sperm (44 gonadotropin studies)

    Factors affecting spermatogenesis upon gonadotropin-replacement therapy: a meta-analytic study

    About 75% achieved sperm in the ejaculate; combined hCG and FSH and onset after puberty predicted better results, and earlier testosterone therapy did not affect outcomes in these men; this says nothing about recovery after testosterone or steroid use in other men. 4

  3. Label202578 men aged 16 to 48 with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism in three open-label trials (63 treated)

    GONAL-f (follitropin alfa for injection): Prescribing Information

    With hCG, 46% to 80% reached the target sperm density after a median of 9 to 12 months; acne and breast enlargement were the notable side effects. 1

  4. Label202630 men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism

    FOLLISTIM AQ (follitropin beta injection): Prescribing Information

    With hCG, 43% reached the target sperm density, after a median of 165 days. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • Do my hormone tests show a pituitary cause, so that FSH with hCG could work for me?
  • How long might it take to see sperm, and how will we track it?
  • Which side effects, such as breast enlargement or acne, should I report?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved biologics for female and male infertility. Gonal-f and Follistim AQ are approved for ovulation induction and assisted reproduction in women and for inducing sperm production in men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism whose infertility is not due to testicular failure. Their approvals were converted to biologics licenses on March 23, 2020. 123
