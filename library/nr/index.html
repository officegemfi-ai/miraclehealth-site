What it is

Nicotinamide riboside (NR) is a form of vitamin B3 that the body can convert into NAD+, a molecule involved in energy metabolism whose levels decline with age and in many diseases. Taken by mouth, it reliably raises NAD+ in the blood, but there is no sign that it raises NAD+ in muscle; rises in its breakdown products there suggest much of the excess is simply cleared. Some trials have tested it combined with another compound, pterostilbene.

Limited evidence

About 25 mostly small human trials show NR raises blood NAD+ and appears safe in the short term, but a 2023 critical review found few clinically relevant effects.

What it may do

  • Moderate evidence

    Reliably raises NAD+ and related molecules in whole blood in human trials (a lab marker, not a health effect); NAD+ itself has generally not risen in muscle 32

  • Limited evidence

    Modest drops in some blood markers of inflammation, the most consistent effect across trials 3

  • Limited evidence

    No benefit shown for insulin sensitivity in several placebo-controlled trials, including detailed clamp studies, and generally none for muscle function, exercise performance or body composition 3

  • Limited evidence

    Raised NAD levels in the brain in a placebo-controlled trial of 30 people with newly diagnosed Parkinson's disease; reported symptom gains came only from an after-the-fact subgroup analysis 3

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    Blood fats: total and LDL cholesterol rose more than with placebo in a trial of 120 older adults taking NR combined with pterostilbene (clinical relevance uncertain), and triglycerides rose with NR in a placebo-controlled trial of men with obesity 3

  • Not known

    Small drops in potassium, hemoglobin and platelets in an uncontrolled study of 8 volunteers, not seen versus placebo in a heart-failure trial; reviewers judged them not clinically relevant 3

  • Not known

    Long-term safety is unknown: the longest trials of NR alone lasted about 4 to 5 months 32

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (not studied)
  • High cholesterol or triglycerides (clinician first, especially for products combined with pterostilbene)

Interactions

  • Pterostilbene: the NR-plus-pterostilbene combination raised LDL cholesterol versus placebo in one trial

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

None routinely needed.

While taking it

None routinely needed.

No routine tests are needed and none are evidence-based; trials found kidney markers unchanged, though blood fats rose in some trials, which may matter for people with high cholesterol.

Key studies

  1. Review202325 published human trials of oral NR

    What is really known about the effects of nicotinamide riboside supplementation in humans

    NR reliably raised blood NAD+ and appeared safe, but showed few clinically relevant effects; the most consistent was a modest drop in inflammatory markers, and the authors warn that the literature tends to exaggerate effects. 3

  2. Review2023Human trials of NR, NR with pterostilbene, and NMN

    Dietary Supplementation With NAD+-Boosting Compounds in Humans: Current Knowledge and Future Directions

    These supplements appear safe and can raise NAD+ in several tissues, but evidence that they improve physiological function is unclear, and small trials limit conclusions. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • Given how few clinical effects trials have shown, is there a measurable reason to expect NR to help me?
  • Should I avoid NR products combined with pterostilbene because of my cholesterol?
  • Do any of my conditions or medicines make an NAD+ booster a bad idea?

Status in the US

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement; GRAS notice for some foods. Used as a dietary supplement. In 2016 FDA had no questions about a company's conclusion that NR is generally recognized as safe (GRAS) as a vitamin B3 source in certain drinks, bars and other foods, though FDA made no GRAS determination of its own. 12
