What it is

Epitalon (also spelled epithalon; sequence Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly, or AEDG) is a synthetic tetrapeptide developed by Vladimir Khavinson's group in St. Petersburg, Russia. It was modeled on epithalamin, an extract of cattle pineal glands. In lab studies it switched on telomerase, the enzyme that rebuilds chromosome tips, and in older monkeys it raised evening melatonin. FDA found no approved epitalon product in Canada, Australia, the UK or the EU.

Animal and lab studies

Its anti-aging case rests on cell and animal studies, mostly from the Russian group that developed it; FDA found only three human studies, none of which assessed safety.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Restored melatonin output (measured in urine) to age-typical levels in a small 20-day placebo-controlled study of 40 women aged 40 to 50 with low melatonin; sleep was not measured 3

  • Animal and lab studies

    Raised evening melatonin more than fourfold and lowered evening cortisol in older rhesus monkeys 3

  • Animal and lab studies

    Increased telomerase activity and lengthened telomeres in human fetal lung cells grown in a dish 3

  • Animal and lab studies

    Fewer spontaneous tumors and slower signs of aging in several studies of female mice 3

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    Human safety is unstudied: FDA found no clinical studies assessing its safety and no clinical data supporting safe use 3

  • In theory

    Possible cancer risk: because it activates telomerase, FDA says it has the potential to be carcinogenic with long-term exposure, and the animal studies were too short and limited to rule this out 3

  • In theory

    Immune reactions when injected, from the peptide itself or from impurities and clumped (aggregated) peptide; there is no official quality standard, as it is not in the European or Japanese Pharmacopoeia 3

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Current or past cancer (because of the telomerase concern)
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no safety data)
  • Under 18 (no safety data)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Not an approved medicine. These are checks a clinician or a clinical trial would use, not a plan for taking it on your own.

Before starting

  • General health check with a clinician, including up-to-date age-appropriate cancer screening

While taking it

  • General health check

No evidence-based monitoring exists for epitalon because it has not been studied for safety in people.

Key studies

  1. Review2026FDA review of the human, animal and cell studies

    FDA Briefing Document for Epitalon-Related Bulk Drug Substances (Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee, July 23-24, 2026)

    FDA found three small human studies, no clinical safety data and no evidence of effectiveness for insomnia, raised immunogenicity and cancer concerns, and proposed not adding epitalon to the compounding list. 3

  2. Animal2002HER-2/neu transgenic mice

    Inhibitory Effect of the Peptide Epitalon on the Development of Spontaneous Mammary Tumors in HER-2/neu Transgenic Mice

    Reported that epitalon held back spontaneous breast tumors in mice bred to develop them. FDA notes that studies like this do not show whether epitalon itself could cause cancer. 3

Questions for your clinician

  • Given that human safety hasn't been studied, what would make epitalon worth the risk for me?
  • Does my personal or family cancer history change the picture, given the telomerase concern?
  • Are there proven ways to improve my sleep and melatonin rhythm that we should try first?

Status in the US

Under FDA review Not approved; compounding status under FDA review. FDA dropped epitalon from its Category 2 safety-risk list in April 2026 after the nominator withdrew it. FDA still reviewed it: staff recommended against adding it to the list of substances pharmacies may compound. and its advisory committee voted 7 to 4 (1 abstention) for adding it in July 2026, but as of mid-October 2026 no rule or interim policy allowing compounding had been published. 12345
