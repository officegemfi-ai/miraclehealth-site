Finasteride.

Also called Propecia, Proscar

A prescription pill that stops testosterone turning into the hormone DHT. Approved for male pattern hair loss and an enlarged prostate. Sexual and mood side effects are a known concern.

What it isevidence librarian

Finasteride is a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor. It blocks the Type II enzyme that turns testosterone into dihydrotestosterone (DHT). That lowers DHT in the blood and scalp, which is how it treats male pattern hair loss and an enlarged prostate. It also lowers PSA, a blood test used in prostate cancer screening. No topical (skin) finasteride product is FDA-approved. Topical versions are compounded.

Strong evidence

It is FDA-approved for male pattern hair loss and an enlarged prostate, based on large randomized trials. The hair-loss trials had 1,879 men, and each of two prostate trials had about 3,000 men.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Strong evidence

    Grew hair in men aged 18 to 41 with mild to moderate male pattern hair loss: on photographs, 48% improved at one year versus 7% on placebo, and 66% versus 7% at two years; gains reversed within 12 months of stopping 1

  • Strong evidence

    In men with an enlarged prostate, cut BPH surgery from 10.1% to 4.6% and acute urinary retention from 6.6% to 2.8% over 4 years 2

  • Strong evidence

    Combined with doxazosin, lowered the risk of BPH getting worse by 67% versus placebo (3,047 men) 2

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Uncommon

    Lower libido, erection problems and ejaculation problems: each in about 1% to 2% of men in the hair-loss trials (versus about 1% on placebo), and more often in older men treated for an enlarged prostate; guidelines say men should be told about these before starting 124

  • Not known

    Sexual problems that continue after stopping, and infertility or poor semen quality, reported after approval 1

  • Not known

    Depression and suicidal thoughts: reported after approval; in 2025 EU regulators confirmed suicidal thoughts as a side effect of unknown frequency, mostly in men taking it for hair loss 15

  • Uncommon

    More high-grade prostate cancer: Gleason 8 to 10 cancer in 1.8% versus 1.1% on placebo over 7 years (18,882 men aged 55 and older) 2

  • Uncommon

    Breast tenderness or enlargement (2.2% versus 0.7% in one long trial), and reports of male breast cancer 2

  • Not known

    Compounded topical versions: 32 reports to FDA (2019 to 2024) of problems including erectile dysfunction, anxiety, depression, suicidal thoughts and brain fog, mostly lasting after stopping; the product can also transfer to other people 3

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Women who are or may be pregnant: must not take it or handle crushed or broken tablets (risk to a male fetus)
  • Women and children (not approved for them)
  • History of depression or suicidal thoughts (discuss the mood risks first)
  • Past allergic reaction to finasteride

What it can clash with

  • None well documented (the label found no clinically important drug interactions)

Blood tests to watchmarkers agent

Before starting

  • PSA, for men who have prostate cancer screening

While taking it

  • PSA, read with the knowledge that finasteride lowers it by about half; a confirmed rise from the lowest value should be evaluated

Finasteride lowers PSA, so the labels say a confirmed rise from the lowest on-treatment value may signal prostate cancer even within the normal range; no other routine labs are required.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. Label20221,879 men aged 18 to 41 with mild to moderate hair loss in three 12-month trials

    PROPECIA (finasteride) tablets: Prescribing Information

    Hair counts rose versus placebo, and the difference grew through 5 years; sexual side effects affected about 1% to 2% of men and generally resolved. 1

  2. Label2026About 3,000 men with an enlarged prostate (PLESS), and 18,882 men aged 55 and older in the Prostate Cancer Prevention Trial

    PROSCAR (finasteride) tablets: Prescribing Information

    Finasteride halved BPH surgery and urinary retention over 4 years; in the prevention trial, high-grade (Gleason 8 to 10) cancer was more common (1.8% versus 1.1%). 2

  3. Review2025EU-wide safety review of 325 reported cases and an estimated 270 million patient-years of finasteride use

    Measures to minimise risk of suicidal thoughts with finasteride and dutasteride medicines

    Confirmed suicidal thoughts as a side effect of finasteride tablets, with unknown frequency, and noted that sexual side effects may contribute to mood changes; a patient card was added for the hair-loss tablets. 5

  4. Review202532 adverse event reports to FDA, 2019 to 2024

    FDA alerts health care providers, compounders and consumers of potential risks associated with compounded topical finasteride products

    Reported problems included erectile dysfunction, anxiety, depression, suicidal thoughts and brain fog, which mostly persisted after the product was stopped. 3

Questions to ask your doctor

  • What sexual or mood changes should I watch for, and what should I do if they happen?
  • Should anyone reading my PSA results know I take finasteride?
  • How does a compounded topical version compare with the approved tablet in evidence and safety?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

FDA-approved FDA-approved for hair loss and enlarged prostate. Approved as Propecia for male pattern hair loss in men and as Proscar for symptomatic benign prostatic hyperplasia; Proscar's label says it is not approved to prevent prostate cancer. No topical finasteride is FDA-approved, and in April 2025 FDA warned about compounded topical products marketed by compounders and telemedicine platforms. 123
