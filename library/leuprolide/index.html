Leuprolide

Also called Lupron, Eligard, Leuprorelin

A prescription hormone-blocking injection that switches off the body's sex-hormone production, used for prostate cancer, endometriosis, fibroids and early puberty in children.

What it is

Leuprolide is a lab-made drug that acts like gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), the signal that drives the pituitary gland's control of the testes and ovaries. Given continuously as a long-acting injection, it first causes a short rise in testosterone or estrogen, which can briefly worsen symptoms, and then shuts their production down to very low levels. The effect is meant to reverse after stopping; the label says normal function usually returns within three months.

Strong evidence

It is FDA-approved for advanced prostate cancer, endometriosis, fibroid-related anemia before surgery and central precocious puberty, based on the clinical trials reviewed for its labels.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Lowers testosterone to castration levels, usually within four weeks, in men with advanced prostate cancer 13

  • Strong evidence

    Hormone-lowering therapy for prostate cancer (not leuprolide alone) cut prostate-cancer deaths and deaths from any cause in a pooled analysis of 11 randomized trials 5

  • Strong evidence

    Relieves pain and shrinks lesions in women with endometriosis 2

  • Strong evidence

    Improves anemia before surgery in women with fibroids, used together with iron 2

  • Strong evidence

    Pauses early puberty in children aged 2 and older with central precocious puberty 4

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Hot flashes and sweats, in about half or more of users in trials 123

  • Uncommon

    Tumor flare in men with prostate cancer: testosterone first rises about 50% in the first weeks, which can worsen bone pain, nerve symptoms, blood in the urine or bladder blockage; spinal cord compression with paralysis can occur 1

  • Common

    Lower sex drive, erection problems and testicular shrinkage, expected while testosterone is suppressed (testicular shrinkage in about 20% at one strength in trials) 1

  • Common

    Bone loss: in women, spine bone density fell an average of about 6% after a year without add-back hormone, and some loss may not recover; bone loss is also reported in men 12

  • Common

    Depression or mood swings (about 1 in 5 women in endometriosis trials); crying, irritability and aggression are reported in children 24

  • Not known

    Higher blood sugar, new or worse diabetes, and higher blood fats 136

  • Not known

    Heart attack, sudden cardiac death, stroke and a longer QT interval (a heart-rhythm measure) have been linked to it in men; the label calls the risk low, and a pooled analysis of trials found no rise in heart deaths 156

  • Rare

    Rare serious reports: seizures, pituitary apoplexy (sudden headache, vomiting, vision changes), severe skin reactions, and in children pseudotumor cerebri (raised pressure inside the skull) 1247

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnancy: it can harm a developing baby
  • Unexplained abnormal vaginal bleeding
  • Known allergy to leuprolide or other GnRH drugs
  • Osteoporosis or strong risk factors for bone loss (needs a clinician's review first)
  • Heart disease, long QT syndrome, diabetes or a seizure history (needs a clinician's review first)

Interactions

  • Other drugs that prolong the QT interval: the label advises weighing the risks first
  • Bupropion and SSRI antidepressants: seizures have been reported with combined use
  • Lab tests of pituitary and sex-hormone function can be thrown off during use and for up to three months after

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Testosterone and PSA (prostate cancer)
  • Blood glucose or HbA1c and a lipid panel
  • Pregnancy test where relevant
  • Bone density before any repeat course in women
  • ECG and electrolytes if heart risk factors are present

While taking it

  • Testosterone and PSA periodically (prostate cancer)
  • Blood glucose or HbA1c and lipids
  • ECG and electrolytes periodically in people at risk
  • In children: LH or sex-hormone levels, height growth and bone age

The labels use these to confirm hormones are suppressed and to catch effects on blood sugar, blood fats, heart rhythm, bone and growth.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis20114,805 men with prostate cancer in 11 randomized trials

    Association of androgen deprivation therapy with cardiovascular death in patients with prostate cancer: a meta-analysis of randomized trials

    Hormone-lowering therapy did not raise cardiovascular deaths (11.0% vs 11.2%) and was linked to fewer prostate-cancer deaths and fewer deaths overall. 5

  2. Cohort201037,443 US veterans with prostate cancer

    Diabetes and cardiovascular disease during androgen deprivation therapy: observational study of veterans with prostate cancer

    Men treated with GnRH agonists had higher rates of diabetes (about 28% higher), coronary heart disease, heart attack, sudden cardiac death and stroke than men not on hormone therapy. 6

  3. Label2025Women with endometriosis or fibroids in clinical trials

    LUPRON DEPOT (leuprolide acetate for depot suspension) prescribing information, endometriosis and fibroids

    Hot flashes affected 73 to 84% of women; spine bone density fell an average of 6.3% after 52 weeks without add-back therapy, and some loss persisted after treatment ended. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • What will we do to protect my bones, and do I need a bone density scan or add-back hormone?
  • How will we check my blood sugar, cholesterol and heart rhythm while I'm on it?
  • Which symptoms should make me call you right away?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved prescription drug. Approved as Lupron Depot and Eligard for advanced prostate cancer, as a separate Lupron Depot product for endometriosis and for fibroid-related anemia before surgery, and as Lupron Depot-PED and Fensolvi for central precocious puberty in children. 1234
