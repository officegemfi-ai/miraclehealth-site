What it is

Thymosin alpha-1 is a 28-amino-acid peptide that acts on the immune system; a synthetic form is sold as Zadaxin. Its maker reports approvals in several countries, mainly for chronic hepatitis B and as a helper for flu vaccines in people with weak immunity, but FDA could not verify these and has approved no thymosin alpha-1 product. People take it hoping to strengthen immune defenses.

Limited evidence

Many human trials exist, but FDA judged the evidence insufficient for every use it reviewed, and the largest well-designed trial, in 1,106 adults with sepsis, found no effect on survival.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    No clear survival benefit in sepsis: an earlier trial of 361 adults reported 26% vs 35% deaths at 28 days with no difference in ICU deaths, and a later placebo-controlled trial of 1,106 adults found no survival benefit (23.4% vs 24.1%) 56

  • Limited evidence

    Mixed results in chronic hepatitis B: in one placebo-controlled trial the virus became undetectable about as often as with placebo (20% vs 21%), and FDA found the evidence insufficient 5

  • Limited evidence

    Somewhat higher tumor response rates than control (6% to 12% vs 4%) in a 488-patient melanoma trial, but the difference was not statistically significant 5

  • Limited evidence

    Fewer COVID-19 deaths in one of four meta-analyses; the other three found no reduction 5

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    Injection-site irritation, redness or discomfort, the most commonly reported side effects 5

  • In theory

    Possible immune reactions to the peptide or to impurities and clumps, which FDA could not rule out 13

  • Not known

    Liver enzyme (ALT) flares in people with chronic hepatitis B and thyroid test (TSH) changes in people with hepatitis C were reported in trials 35

  • Not known

    A fatal immune destruction of red blood cells in 1 of 6 stem-cell transplant recipients who also received donor immune cells; FDA could not tell whether thymosin alpha-1 contributed 3

  • In theory

    FDA flagged safety concerns about use in transplant patients and alongside flu vaccines 5

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Stem-cell transplant recipients (FDA flagged a fatal immune reaction and more infections after stem-cell transplant)
  • Chronic hepatitis B or C, unless a liver specialist is involved (liver flares and thyroid changes reported; approved antiviral drugs exist)
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no safety data)

Interactions

  • Flu vaccines: FDA raised safety concerns about using it as a vaccine helper and found too little evidence of benefit

Blood markers to watch

  • Liver enzymes and TSH if used with viral hepatitis

No evidence-based monitoring exists for thymosin alpha-1; liver enzyme flares and thyroid test changes were reported in hepatitis trials.

Key studies

  1. RCT20251,106 adults with sepsis in 22 hospitals in China

    The efficacy and safety of thymosin α1 for sepsis (TESTS): multicentre, double blinded, randomised, placebo controlled, phase 3 trial

    Deaths within 28 days were 23.4% with thymosin alpha-1 and 24.1% with placebo, and no secondary or safety outcome differed. Possible differences by age and diabetes were called hypothesis-generating. 6

  2. Review2024Published human and animal studies, including trials in hepatitis, sepsis, cancer and COVID-19

    FDA Evaluation of Thymosin Alpha-1 (Ta1) – Related Bulk Drug Substances (Ta1 (free base) and Ta1 acetate)

    FDA found too little nonclinical safety data, listed adverse events including liver enzyme flares, thyroid test changes and a fatal case after stem-cell transplant, and concluded the evidence weighed against allowing compounding. 3

  3. Review2024Trials in hepatitis B and C, sepsis, melanoma, liver cancer and COVID-19

    Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee Meeting: Thymosin Alpha-1 (Ta1) Related Bulk Drug Substances (FDA presentation)

    FDA judged effectiveness insufficient for every use reviewed: hepatitis B results were mixed, a 488-patient melanoma trial showed no significant difference, and three of four COVID-19 meta-analyses found no effect on deaths. 5

Questions for your clinician

  • What is behind my concern about immunity, and which proven treatments or vaccines fit?
  • If I have hepatitis B or C, how does this compare with approved antiviral drugs?
  • Which side effects or lab changes should make me stop?

Status in the US

Not approved Not approved; FDA advisers voted against compounding. No thymosin alpha-1 product is FDA-approved, though it has four US orphan-drug designations; its compounding nomination was withdrawn, but FDA reviewed it anyway, and in December 2024 FDA's advisers voted 17-4 against adding it to the list pharmacies may compound from. FDA still cites possible immune reactions, and for outsourcing facilities it sits in Category 3 (nominated without adequate support). 1234
