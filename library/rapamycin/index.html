What it isevidence librarian

Rapamycin, also called sirolimus and sold as Rapamune, is a prescription drug that suppresses the immune system. It blocks a signaling pathway called mTOR, which mouse studies have linked to lifespan. It is approved to prevent rejection of a kidney transplant and to treat lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM), a rare lung disease. It is being tested in small human trials for aging.

Limited evidence

It is an approved transplant and LAM drug. For aging, the evidence is mouse lifespan studies plus small, short human trials. Those trials measured markers of aging, not lifespan or disease outcomes.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Strong evidence

    Prevents organ rejection in kidney transplant patients aged 13 and older (approved use) 1

  • Strong evidence

    Treats lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM), a rare lung disease (approved use) 1

  • Animal and lab studies

    Longer lifespan in genetically diverse mice, even when started late in life: 14% for females and 9% for males at the age when 90% had died, at three test sites 2

  • Limited evidence

    Improved some aging-related measures of the immune system, heart and blood vessels, and skin in a review of 19 human studies, but not muscle, hormone or nervous-system measures 3

  • Limited evidence

    No benefit shown on the main outcome: no change in visceral fat in a 48-week trial of 114 healthy adults aged 50 to 85; women on the higher dose gained lean tissue and reported less pain 4

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Not known

    Boxed warning: a weakened immune system raises the risk of infections and of lymphoma and other cancers; not recommended after liver or lung transplant because of excess deaths, graft loss and other serious complications 1

  • Common

    High cholesterol and triglycerides, swelling (edema) and high blood pressure, each in 30% or more of kidney transplant patients 1

  • Common

    Mouth sores, diarrhea, nausea, acne and headache, each in 20% or more of LAM patients 1

  • Not known

    Slow wound healing, lung inflammation (interstitial lung disease, sometimes fatal), protein in the urine and skin cancer 1

  • Not known

    Angioedema (swelling of the face, lips or throat), more likely when taken with other drugs that can cause it, such as ACE inhibitors 1

  • Not known

    High blood sugar and new diabetes have been reported 1

  • Not known

    Harm to a developing baby, and low or absent sperm counts 1

  • Common

    In healthy older adults on the regimen tested, more stomach and gut complaints than placebo (about 1 in 5 versus 1 in 10), with similar rates of serious events 4

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Pregnant or planning pregnancy (it can harm a fetus; the label advises highly effective contraception during treatment and for 12 weeks after)
  • Allergy to sirolimus
  • Liver or lung transplant recipients (not recommended)
  • Active infection or a weakened immune system
  • Planning to get a live vaccine (live vaccines should be avoided during treatment)

What it can clash with

  • Strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors such as ketoconazole, voriconazole, itraconazole, erythromycin and clarithromycin: avoid, as they raise sirolimus levels
  • Strong CYP3A4/P-gp inducers such as rifampin and rifabutin: avoid, as they lower sirolimus levels
  • ACE inhibitors: raise the risk of angioedema
  • Grapefruit juice: must not be taken with it
  • Cyclosporine: interacts with sirolimus, and the label sets rules for using them together

The label recommends sirolimus level checks, lipid monitoring and periodic urine-protein and kidney tests; these were set for transplant and LAM care, and no monitoring plan has been established for use in aging.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. Systematic review202419 studies in healthy adults and people with aging-related diseases

    Targeting ageing with rapamycin and its derivatives in humans: a systematic review

    Rapamycin and related drugs improved some immune, cardiovascular and skin measures but not endocrine, muscle or nervous-system measures; healthy people had no serious drug-related events, while patients had more infections and higher cholesterol and triglycerides. 3

  2. RCT2025114 healthy adults aged 50 to 85 (48 weeks)

    Influence of rapamycin on safety and healthspan metrics after one year: PEARL trial results

    Low-dose intermittent rapamycin did not change visceral fat; women on the higher dose gained lean tissue and had less pain, and adverse events were similar to placebo. The trial was small and several authors work for the company that ran it. 4

  3. Animal2009Genetically diverse mice at three test sites

    Rapamycin fed late in life extends lifespan in genetically heterogeneous mice

    Rapamycin started at 600 days of age extended median and maximal lifespan in both sexes, possibly by delaying cancer deaths, slowing aging, or both. 2

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Given my infection history, cholesterol and any planned surgery, what are the risks of rapamycin for me?
  • Which of my medicines, supplements or foods could change rapamycin levels?
  • Is there a clinical trial of rapamycin for aging that I could join?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

FDA-approved FDA-approved transplant and LAM drug; not for aging. Rapamune is approved to prevent organ rejection in kidney transplant patients aged 13 and older and to treat LAM, with a boxed warning about immunosuppression, infections and cancer. It is not approved for aging, and the label says only physicians experienced in immunosuppressive therapy should prescribe it for transplant patients. 1
