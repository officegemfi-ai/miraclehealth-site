What it is

SS-31, or elamipretide, is a small synthetic peptide that binds cardiolipin, a fat in the inner membrane of mitochondria, the parts of cells that make energy; its label says this improves mitochondrial structure and function. FDA approved it in 2025 as Forzinity to improve muscle strength in Barth syndrome, a rare inherited mitochondrial disease. People also take it hoping to slow aging, based mainly on mouse studies.

Limited evidence

FDA's accelerated approval rests on strength gains in about 10 people with Barth syndrome during an open-label extension, after a 12-week placebo-controlled phase in 12 patients missed its goals; a 218-person trial in mitochondrial myopathy found no benefit, and anti-aging effects come from mouse studies.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Gradual gains in knee muscle strength over months to years of open-label treatment in about 10 people with Barth syndrome, the basis for accelerated approval; strength did not improve during the 12-week placebo-controlled phase 12

  • Animal and lab studies

    Substantially reversed age-related problems with heart relaxation (diastolic dysfunction) and lowered mitochondrial oxidative stress in old mice after 8 weeks 4

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Injection-site reactions in every treated patient in the Barth syndrome trial: redness, pain, hardening, itching, and sometimes bruising or hives 1

  • Not known

    Serious allergic reactions needing emergency care have been reported, along with rashes and cough; they can begin minutes to months after starting 1

  • Common

    Higher eosinophil counts (a type of white blood cell) with longer use, peaking around 90 days and later returning to baseline 1

  • Not known

    Drug levels rise when kidney function is reduced, and it has not been studied in people on dialysis 1

  • Not known

    Safety for aging or wellness use has not been studied; in a 24-week trial in 218 adults with mitochondrial myopathy, side effects were mostly mild to moderate 3

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Known serious allergy to elamipretide or its ingredients (contraindicated)
  • Newborns, especially premature babies (the product contains benzyl alcohol, which has caused fatal reactions in them)
  • Severe kidney disease or dialysis (drug levels rise; dialysis not studied)
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no human data)

Interactions

  • In lab tests it blocks MATE1, a kidney transporter that clears some drugs; clinical studies found no meaningful effect on aspirin, clopidogrel or heparin

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Kidney function (creatinine and eGFR)
  • Complete blood count with differential

While taking it

  • Complete blood count, since eosinophil counts often rose with longer use

These follow the label's notes on kidney function and eosinophils; for off-label use in healthy people, no evidence-based monitoring exists.

Key studies

  1. RCT2023218 adults with genetically confirmed primary mitochondrial myopathy

    Efficacy and Safety of Elamipretide in Individuals With Primary Mitochondrial Myopathy: The MMPOWER-3 Randomized Clinical Trial

    After 24 weeks, elamipretide did not improve 6-minute walk distance or fatigue compared with placebo. It was well tolerated, with mostly mild to moderate side effects. 3

  2. Label202512 males aged 12 and older with Barth syndrome in a crossover trial; 10 continued in an open-label extension

    FORZINITY (elamipretide) prescribing information (updated December 2025)

    The 12-week placebo-controlled phase missed its main goals (walking distance and fatigue), and strength did not rise. In the extension, median knee strength rose by week 12 and stayed higher through week 168, which FDA accepted for accelerated approval. 1

  3. Animal2020Old mice

    Late-life restoration of mitochondrial function reverses cardiac dysfunction in old mice

    Eight weeks of SS-31 substantially reversed diastolic dysfunction and lowered mitochondrial oxidative stress in the heart, similar to a gene therapy that boosts a mitochondrial antioxidant enzyme. 4

Questions for your clinician

  • What do human trials show for my goal, given that the approval covers only Barth syndrome?
  • How would we watch for allergic reactions and injection-site problems?
  • Does my kidney function affect whether this is safe for me?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved (Forzinity) for Barth syndrome only. FDA granted accelerated approval in September 2025 to improve muscle strength in people with Barth syndrome weighing at least 30 kg, after first declining the application in May 2025; continued approval may depend on a confirmatory trial. Use for aging, energy or any other condition is off-label. 12
