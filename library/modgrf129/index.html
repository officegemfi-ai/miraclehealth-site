What it is

Mod GRF 1-29 is a lab-made peptide that matches the first 29 amino acids of growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH). That is the same stretch as sermorelin, with four amino acids swapped (at positions 2, 8, 15 and 27). FDA's review calls this 29-amino-acid peptide 'CJC-1295 (free base)'. FDA treats it as a different active ingredient from CJC-1295 with DAC, which carries an extra unit that makes it last for days. Like natural GHRH, it is meant to signal the pituitary gland to release growth hormone. It is not an approved medicine.

Anecdotal

No studies of this exact peptide were identified. The only human data FDA cited for 'CJC-1295' are two small studies of the long-acting form, and they had no effectiveness endpoints.

What it may do

  • Anecdotal

    Built on the active part of GHRH, so it is expected to trigger growth hormone release like sermorelin; no study of this exact peptide was identified 56

  • Limited evidence

    GHRH-type peptides and growth hormone-releasing peptides (GHRPs) together release more growth hormone than either alone; shown with related peptides, not this one 7

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    No human safety data for this exact peptide; in FDA's review of CJC-1295, human studies of the long-acting form showed frequent injection-site and vasodilatory reactions and increased heart rate 8

  • Not known

    FDA found the free base poorly soluble in water and all CJC-1295 forms 'not well characterized', raising impurity and immune-reaction concerns 28

  • Not known

    Animal studies of CJC-1295 found DNA damage in pituitary cells, and no cancer studies exist 8

  • Not known

    Gray-market supply: a review reports GHRH analogs have entered the black market, so identity and purity are uncertain 6

  • In theory

    Fluid retention, joint pain and higher blood sugar are label warnings for tesamorelin, an approved drug that works the same way 9

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Active cancer or a history of pituitary tumors
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no safety data)
  • Diabetes or prediabetes (clinician first)
  • Competitive athletes (prohibited by WADA at all times)

What it can clash with

  • None well documented

The main studies

  1. Review2024FDA review of CJC-1295 forms: two small studies in healthy adults plus rat and dog studies

    Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee meeting transcript, December 4, 2024 (morning session: CJC-1295)

    FDA treated the non-DAC and DAC forms as distinct, judged all forms poorly characterized, and found no information on effectiveness for growth hormone deficiency. 8

  2. Case series201714 men on testosterone therapy (retrospective chart review)

    Growth Hormone Secretagogue Treatment in Hypogonadal Men Raises Serum Insulin-Like Growth Factor-1 Levels

    A combination of sermorelin (the unmodified GHRH 1-29) with GHRP-2 and GHRP-6 raised average IGF-1 from about 160 to 239 ng/mL; the authors note GHRH and GHRPs act together. 7

Where it stands in the US

Not approved Not approved; FDA advisers voted against compounding. In December 2024 FDA's compounding advisory committee voted 13-0 (free base) and 12-1 (acetate) against adding non-DAC CJC-1295 to the list of substances pharmacies may compound. FDA's 'CJC-1295' entry is on its 'nominated but withdrawn' list and not in Category 1, so there is no clear legal compounding route; anti-doping rules ban GHRH analogues in sport. 1234
