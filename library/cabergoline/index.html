Cabergoline

Also called Dostinex, Caber

A prescription pill that lowers high prolactin by acting like dopamine on the pituitary; approved for hyperprolactinemia, including prolactin-making pituitary tumors.

What it is

Cabergoline is an ergot-derived dopamine agonist that binds strongly to D2 receptors and directly blocks prolactin release from the pituitary gland. Too much prolactin can cause breast milk discharge (galactorrhea) and stop periods; in trials, cabergoline restored periods and stopped discharge in most women. It stays in the body a long time, with an estimated half-life of 63 to 69 hours.

Strong evidence

FDA-approved for hyperprolactinemia based on randomized trials, including one where 77% reached normal prolactin versus 59% with bromocriptine; a 2021 Cochrane review found it probably lowers the risk of ovarian hyperstimulation in IVF (off-label).

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Brought prolactin to normal in 77% of women with hyperprolactinemia versus 59% with bromocriptine over 8 weeks, restoring periods in 77% and stopping milk discharge in 73% 1

  • Strong evidence

    In a 4-week placebo-controlled study, 74% to 95% reached normal prolactin, versus none on placebo 1

  • Moderate evidence

    Dopamine agonists, including cabergoline, probably cut moderate or severe ovarian hyperstimulation in women at high risk during IVF, from about 27% to between 8% and 14% (22 trials, 3,171 women; off-label) 2

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Nausea (27%), headache, dizziness (15%), constipation (10%), fatigue and dizziness on standing 1

  • Not known

    Heart valve damage, reported mostly at the higher amounts once used for Parkinson's disease but also in hyperprolactinemia; in people treated for hyperprolactinemia, a meta-analysis of 13 studies found more tricuspid regurgitation (odds ratio 3.74), of unclear clinical importance 13

  • Not known

    Scarring (fibrosis) of the lungs, the lining of the lungs (pleura), the lining of the heart (pericardium) or the tissue behind the abdomen 1

  • Not known

    Impulse control problems such as compulsive gambling, spending or sexual urges; psychosis and aggression have also been reported 1

  • Not known

    Using dopamine agonists to stop breast milk after childbirth has been linked to high blood pressure, stroke, heart attack, seizures and death 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Uncontrolled high blood pressure
  • History of heart valve disease or pericardial fibrosis
  • History of lung, pleural or retroperitoneal fibrosis
  • Allergy to ergot medicines
  • Severe liver impairment, pregnancy or the weeks after childbirth (dopamine agonists are generally avoided)

Interactions

  • Dopamine blockers (phenothiazines, butyrophenones, thioxanthenes, metoclopramide): not recommended together
  • Other drugs linked to heart valve disease: only if the expected benefit outweighs the risk
  • Blood pressure-lowering drugs: higher risk of dizziness or fainting on standing

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Echocardiogram (heart ultrasound)
  • Prolactin
  • Blood pressure

While taking it

  • Echocardiogram every 6 to 12 months or as clinically indicated
  • Prolactin
  • Blood pressure

The label calls for an echocardiogram before starting and repeat scans every 6 to 12 months to check the heart valves, plus blood pressure checks; prolactin shows whether treatment is working.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis20213,171 women at high risk of ovarian hyperstimulation in 22 randomized trials

    Dopamine agonists for preventing ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome

    Dopamine agonists probably reduce moderate or severe ovarian hyperstimulation (odds ratio 0.32); effects on live birth and side effects are uncertain. 2

  2. Meta-analysis2018Patients treated for hyperprolactinemia in 13 case-control studies

    A Meta-Analysis of the Prevalence of Cardiac Valvulopathy in Patients With Hyperprolactinemia Treated With Cabergoline

    Tricuspid regurgitation was more common with cabergoline than in controls (odds ratio 3.74), but no patient had symptoms from it and no other valve problem was increased. 3

  3. Label2026Women with hyperprolactinemia (8-week comparison with bromocriptine)

    Cabergoline tablets: Prescribing Information

    77% reached normal prolactin with cabergoline versus 59% with bromocriptine, and more had periods restored and milk discharge resolved. 1

Questions for your clinician

  • Do I need an echocardiogram before starting, and how often should it be repeated?
  • What is causing my high prolactin, and does it need further tests?
  • Which mood, behavior or impulse changes should I report?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for high prolactin (hyperprolactinemia). Approved for hyperprolactinemic disorders in adults, either of unknown cause or due to pituitary adenomas; the label says not to use it to suppress breast milk after childbirth. Other uses, such as preventing ovarian hyperstimulation during IVF, are off-label. 1
