L-theanine.

Also called Theanine

An amino acid from tea leaves. Taken to feel calm without getting drowsy, to ease stress, and to improve sleep quality and focus.

What it isevidence librarian

L-theanine is an amino acid that is not used to build proteins. It is found in the tea plant (Camellia sinensis) and some mushrooms, and it adds to tea's savory taste. It resembles glutamate and glutamine and can cross into the brain. It is reported to affect GABA, dopamine, serotonin and glutamate, and to increase alpha brain waves linked to a relaxed but alert state. How it works is not fully established.

Limited evidence

Small, short randomized trials suggest modest effects on acute stress, sleep quality and attention. It was often studied together with caffeine. Larger independent trials are lacking.

Where it acts in the bodymechanism agent

L-theanine works in one place in the body: the brain.

Brain
  • ▶acts: reaches the brain, reportedly raising alpha waves; how it works isn't fully known 3
  • ▼lowers: stress and anxiety may fall under acute stress (small, industry-linked trials) 3
  • ▲raises: better self-rated sleep quality and faster sleep onset (small, industry-linked trials) 34
  • ▲raises: small gains in attention and reaction time, more with caffeine (small trials) 3
  • ▶acts: no longer total sleep; overall scores matched placebo in a 30-person trial 34
  • ▶acts: no sedation in trials; side effects few and mild, at placebo rates 3

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Limited evidence

    May reduce stress and anxiety during acute stress, based on a review of 9 randomized trials with 270 people 3

  • Limited evidence

    Better self-rated sleep quality and faster sleep onset in pooled trials, without longer total sleep 34

  • Limited evidence

    Small improvements in attention and reaction time in healthy adults, stronger when combined with caffeine 3

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Not known

    Few, mild side effects reported, at rates similar to placebo in trials 3

  • Not known

    Long-term safety is unknown because trials have been short 34

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Anxiety, depression or another mental health condition (doctor first; evidence in these groups is limited)
  • Ongoing insomnia (evidence in clinical insomnia is limited, so seek an evaluation)

What it can clash with

  • None well documented

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. RCT201930 healthy adults aged 20 to 69 (crossover)

    Effects of L-Theanine Administration on Stress-Related Symptoms and Cognitive Functions in Healthy Adults: A Randomized Controlled Trial

    Over 4 weeks, time to fall asleep, sleep disturbance and sleep-medication use improved more than with placebo, but overall stress, sleep and thinking scores did not differ significantly; the study was funded by the supplement's supplier. 4

  2. Review2026Review of randomized trials in healthy adults and patient groups

    Effects of L-theanine on cognition, mood and sleep: evidence from human trials

    Found modest support for acute stress relief, sleep quality and attention, no sedation, and a need for larger and longer trials; the authors work for a company that sells L-theanine products. 3

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Could L-theanine help my sleep or stress, or should we look for an underlying cause?
  • Is it safe with my other medicines or supplements, including caffeine products?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement; GRAS food ingredient. Sold as a dietary supplement. FDA had no questions about three notices (closed in 2007, 2010 and 2014) concluding that L-theanine is generally recognized as safe for use in foods; a fourth notice was pending as of October 2026. 12
