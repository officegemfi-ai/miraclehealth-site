Triptorelin

Also called Trelstar, Decapeptyl, Triptodur

A prescription hormone drug that, with continued use, shuts down testosterone and estrogen production; approved for advanced prostate cancer and for early puberty in children.

What it is

Triptorelin is a lab-made version of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), the brain signal that tells the pituitary gland to release LH and FSH. A first dose briefly raises LH, FSH, testosterone and estradiol; with continued use the pituitary stops responding and testosterone falls to castrate levels. In the US it is sold as Trelstar (prostate cancer) and Triptodur (early puberty); in the UK it is sold as Decapeptyl, with added uses such as endometriosis. Because the first dose stimulates testosterone, anti-doping rules ban it in male athletes.

Strong evidence

It is an approved drug: controlled trials in men with advanced prostate cancer and a single-arm trial in 44 children with early puberty support its labeled uses.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Lowers testosterone to castrate levels in men with advanced prostate cancer (91% to 98% of men by day 29 across three trials) 1

  • Strong evidence

    Halts early puberty in children 2 and older with central precocious puberty (LH suppressed in over 93% of 44 children) 24

  • Strong evidence

    Treats endometriosis in women, an approved use in the UK but not in the US 512

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Hot flashes (in 59% to 73% of men in prostate cancer trials) 1

  • Common

    Lower sex drive, erection problems and testicular shrinkage are expected while testosterone is suppressed; trials listed them as side effects in about 2% to 10% of men 1

  • Uncommon

    The early hormone surge can briefly worsen symptoms: tumor flare in prostate cancer (including urinary blockage or spinal cord compression) and vaginal bleeding in children 12

  • Not known

    Higher risk of diabetes, heart attack, sudden cardiac death and stroke with long-term testosterone suppression; FDA describes the risk as low 61

  • Not known

    Bone thinning with long-term use, which may raise fracture risk 5

  • Not known

    Mood changes, depression and seizures; in children, raised pressure inside the skull (pseudotumor cerebri) has been reported 512

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or could become pregnant (it can harm a fetus)
  • Allergic to triptorelin or other GnRH agonist drugs
  • Heart disease, diabetes, a long QT interval or a history of seizures (needs a clinician's assessment first)
  • Osteoporosis or other risk factors for weak bones
  • Male athlete in drug-tested sport (banned at all times)

Interactions

  • Drugs that prolong the QT interval, such as certain antiarrhythmics, methadone, moxifloxacin and antipsychotics
  • Drugs that raise prolactin (the US label advises against using them together)

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Testosterone (men) or LH and sex-hormone levels (children)
  • Blood glucose or HbA1c and a lipid panel
  • Blood pressure and a heart rhythm check if there are QT risk factors

While taking it

  • Testosterone, to confirm it stays suppressed (men with prostate cancer)
  • Blood glucose or HbA1c, lipids and blood pressure, periodically
  • In children: LH and sex-hormone levels, height periodically, and bone age

The labels call for confirming hormone suppression and periodic checks of blood sugar and lipids; in children, growth and bone age are tracked.

Key studies

  1. Label2026Three trials in 277, 346 and 120 men with advanced prostate cancer

    TRELSTAR (triptorelin pamoate for injectable suspension), for intramuscular use: prescribing information

    Testosterone reached castrate levels by day 29 in 91% to 98% of men and stayed suppressed in 93% to 96% over the treatment period. Hot flashes were the most common side effect. 1

  2. Trial201644 children with central precocious puberty (39 girls, 5 boys)

    Efficacy and safety of triptorelin 6-month formulation in patients with central precocious puberty

    LH fell to prepubertal levels in 93% of children at month 6 and suppression held through month 12, with no unexpected drug-related side effects. 4

Questions for your clinician

  • Is triptorelin right for my situation, or is there a better-studied option for my goal?
  • What should I expect when I stop, and how long until my own hormones recover?
  • How will we watch my blood sugar, lipids, heart rhythm and bone health while I take it?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved (Trelstar, Triptodur). Trelstar (first approved in 2000) is approved for advanced prostate cancer, and Triptodur for central precocious puberty in children 2 and older. The 2026 WADA Prohibited List bans it at all times in male athletes. 123
