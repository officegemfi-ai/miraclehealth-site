Also called FST-344, Follistatin

What it is

Follistatin is a protein the body makes. It blocks myostatin, a muscle protein that limits muscle growth, and it also binds activins. FS344 is one of its naturally occurring forms, made by alternative splicing of the follistatin gene. Researchers picked FS344 for gene-therapy trials because it has minimal effects on the hormone system that controls reproduction.

Limited evidence

Human evidence comes from two small, uncontrolled gene-therapy studies in 12 patients with muscle diseases; the rest is animal research.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Walking distance improved over a year in 6 men with inclusion body myositis given follistatin gene therapy, while matched untreated patients declined 3

  • Limited evidence

    Longer 6-minute walk in 4 of 6 patients with Becker muscular dystrophy after gene therapy into both thigh muscles 2

  • Limited evidence

    Less scarring (fibrosis) and more signs of muscle repair on biopsies after gene therapy in patients with muscle diseases 23

  • Animal and lab studies

    Larger muscles in mice when follistatin was increased, a stronger effect than switching off myostatin 3

Risks and side effects

  • In theory

    Possible effects on reproductive hormones; the FS344 form was chosen to keep these small, and none were seen in the myositis trial 34

  • Not known

    Human safety data are limited to 12 gene-therapy patients, in whom no treatment-related adverse events were reported 23

Talk to a clinician first if you are

  • Pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive (follistatin acts on the reproductive hormone system)
  • Athletes subject to anti-doping rules (follistatin is banned by WADA)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • General health check (complete blood count and metabolic panel) if a clinician is involved
  • Pituitary and sex hormone levels, which the gene-therapy trial monitored

While taking it

None routinely needed.

No evidence-based monitoring exists for injected follistatin; in the myositis gene-therapy trial, hormone, heart, liver, kidney and bone marrow checks showed no changes.

Key studies

  1. Trial20176 men with sporadic inclusion body myositis, compared with 8 matched untreated patients

    Follistatin Gene Therapy for Sporadic Inclusion Body Myositis Improves Functional Outcomes

    After gene therapy into both thigh muscles, 6-minute walk distance rose by a median 56 m per year, versus a 26 m per year decline in untreated patients. No treatment-related adverse events were reported. 3

  2. Trial20146 patients with Becker muscular dystrophy

    A phase 1/2a follistatin gene therapy trial for becker muscular dystrophy

    Four of six walked farther on the 6-minute walk test, and biopsies showed less fibrosis and some muscle growth. No adverse effects were reported. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • Is there any evidence that injected follistatin builds muscle in healthy people?
  • Could this affect my fertility or hormone levels?
  • Which proven ways to build muscle should I try first?

Status in the US

Not approved Not approved; not on FDA's compounding lists. Follistatin is not an FDA-approved drug and does not appear on FDA's list of bulk substances nominated for pharmacy compounding (updated May 2026). Follistatin gene therapy has been tested only in small phase 1/2a trials. 123
