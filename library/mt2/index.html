What it is

Melanotan II is a synthetic peptide modeled on alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (alpha-MSH), the body's signal that tells skin cells to make pigment. Besides darkening the skin, it acts on the brain and can trigger spontaneous erections, nausea and yawning. It was tested in a few small studies between 1996 and 2000 but was never approved as a medicine. A related peptide, afamelanotide, is approved for a few medical uses.

Limited evidence

Its effects on skin color and erections come from a pilot study of 3 men and a placebo-controlled crossover trial of 10 men; most safety information comes from case reports.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Darker skin on the face and upper body in 2 of 3 healthy men in a pilot study 1

  • Limited evidence

    Erections in men with erectile dysfunction: after 12 of 19 injections versus 1 of 21 placebo injections, in a trial of 10 men 2

  • Limited evidence

    Higher sexual desire than placebo in the same 10-man trial 2

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Nausea, sometimes severe, along with yawning and stretching 12

  • Not known

    New or changing moles, and case reports of melanoma during or soon after use (cause not proven) 34

  • Not known

    Priapism (a prolonged erection that needs emergency care), a brain condition called posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES), and stimulant-like toxic reactions, in case reports 4

  • Not known

    Unregulated products of uncertain content and purity; impurities may trigger immune reactions 347

Talk to a clinician first if you are

  • History of melanoma, or many or unusual moles
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Past priapism
  • Heart disease or uncontrolled high blood pressure, given reports of stimulant-like toxic reactions

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • General health check including blood pressure
  • Full-body skin and mole exam

While taking it

  • Regular skin checks for new or changing moles

No evidence-based monitoring exists for this unapproved drug; skin checks follow from case reports of mole changes and melanoma.

Key studies

  1. RCT200010 men with erectile dysfunction (crossover)

    Effect of an alpha-melanocyte stimulating hormone analog on penile erection and sexual desire in men with organic erectile dysfunction

    Erections followed 12 of 19 Melanotan II injections versus 1 of 21 placebo injections, and sexual desire rose. Nausea and yawning were more common, with severe nausea after 4 of 19 injections. 2

  2. Trial19963 healthy men

    Evaluation of melanotan-II, a superpotent cyclic melanotropic peptide in a pilot phase-I clinical study

    Two of the three men had measurably darker skin. Spontaneous erections with yawning and stretching followed doses, and mild nausea was common. 1

  3. Review2017Case reports of unregulated users

    Risks of unregulated use of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone analogues: A review

    Case reports link melanotan use to changes in existing moles and new atypical moles, plus four melanomas; the authors note conclusive proof of cause is lacking. 3

Questions for your clinician

  • Should I have a full skin exam, and how often should my moles be checked?
  • What safer options exist for the reason I am considering this, such as sun protection or treatment for low libido?
  • Which symptoms, such as an erection lasting hours or a severe headache, mean I need emergency care?

Status in the US

Under FDA review Not approved; compounding review pending. It is an unapproved drug; in 2007 FDA warned a seller and told consumers to stop using it. FDA took it off its Category 2 safety-risk list in April 2026 after the nomination was withdrawn, but it is not on the list pharmacies may compound from; an FDA advisory committee is expected to review it before the end of February 2027. 45678
