What it isevidence librarian

Humanin is a peptide of 21 or 24 amino acids. It is encoded by a short stretch of DNA inside mitochondria, the cell's energy-producing structures. It was discovered in 2001 in a part of the brain spared by Alzheimer's disease. In lab and animal studies it protects cells against several kinds of stress. Blood levels fall with age in people and mice. A more potent lab-made analog, HNG, is used in animal research.

Animal and lab studies

All the treatment data come from cells, worms and rodents. The human data are only measurements of blood levels.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Animal and lab studies

    Protected nerve cells from damage by amyloid-beta, the protein that builds up in Alzheimer's disease, in cell studies 3

  • Animal and lab studies

    Improved insulin action in rats, and reduced heart damage after an induced heart attack and artery plaque in mice 3

  • Animal and lab studies

    About 7% longer average lifespan in worms engineered to make more humanin; in middle-aged mice, the analog HNG lowered visceral fat and raised lean mass but did not extend lifespan 4

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Not known

    Never tested as a treatment in people, so human side effects and safety are unknown 34

  • In theory

    Unclear effects on cancer: studies of humanin, tumor growth and spread are inconclusive 3

  • Not known

    Smaller body size and fewer offspring in worms and mice with more humanin 4

  • Not known

    Not an approved drug and on none of FDA's compounding lists, so products sold online have no checks on identity or purity 1

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Pregnant, breastfeeding or trying to conceive (fewer offspring in animals; no human data)
  • Current or past cancer (effects on tumors are unclear)

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. Animal2020Worms and middle-aged mice, plus blood levels in 18 children of centenarians and 19 controls

    The mitochondrial derived peptide humanin is a regulator of lifespan and healthspan

    Extra humanin modestly extended worm lifespan; the analog HNG improved body composition in mice without extending lifespan; children of centenarians had higher blood humanin than controls. 4

  2. Review2014Cell, rodent and human observational studies

    Humanin and Age-Related Diseases: A New Link?

    Humanin protected cells and improved insulin action, heart injury and plaque in rodents, and circulating levels fall with age; its pharmacokinetics and long-term safety still need study. 3

  3. Review2021Studies of humanin, MOTS-c and related peptides

    Mitochondrial-derived peptides in aging and age-related diseases

    The authors propose that falling levels of these peptides contribute to aging and that raising them might have broad benefits. 5
