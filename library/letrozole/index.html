Letrozole

Also called Femara

A prescription pill that blocks estrogen production, approved for hormone-sensitive breast cancer after menopause; it is also used off-label to trigger ovulation and in men with low testosterone.

What it is

Letrozole is a nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor: it blocks aromatase, the enzyme that makes estrogens. In postmenopausal women with advanced breast cancer it lowers estradiol, estrone and estrone sulfate by 75% to 95% within two to three days, without significantly affecting cortisol, aldosterone or thyroid hormones. Because high estradiol can suppress LH and FSH, it is used off-label to raise testosterone and sperm production in some infertile men.

Strong evidence

FDA-approved for breast cancer after menopause; for ovulation induction in PCOS (off-label), a 2022 Cochrane review found high-certainty evidence of more live births than with clomiphene, while evidence in men comes from 8 studies whose reviewers called for proper randomized trials.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Treats hormone receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women, both early (after surgery or after prior tamoxifen) and advanced 1

  • Strong evidence

    Led to more live births than clomiphene in infertile women with PCOS: if 20% have a baby with clomiphene, 27% to 35% would with letrozole (11 trials, 2,060 women; off-label) 3

  • Limited evidence

    Improved hormone levels and semen measures in infertile or low-testosterone men across 8 studies of aromatase inhibitors; the AUA/ASRM guideline says aromatase inhibitors may be considered for infertile men with low testosterone (off-label) 45

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    High cholesterol in 52.3% of women versus 28.6% with tamoxifen in the main adjuvant trial 1

  • Common

    Bone loss and fractures: fractures in 14.7% versus 11.4% with tamoxifen, and osteoporosis in 5.1% versus 2.7% 1

  • Common

    Hot flashes (33.5%) and joint pain or arthritis (25.4%) 1

  • Not known

    Fatigue, dizziness and sleepiness, which can affect driving 1

  • Not known

    Harm to a fetus: miscarriages and birth defects have been reported; animal studies also suggest it may impair male fertility 1

  • Uncommon

    In men, about 3% stopped aromatase inhibitors because of side effects in a review of 8 studies; pregnancy outcomes were not reported 5

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnancy or possible pregnancy
  • Breastfeeding
  • Osteoporosis or high fracture risk without a bone-protection plan
  • Cirrhosis or severe liver impairment (drug levels are about twice as high)
  • Past allergic reaction to letrozole

Interactions

  • Tamoxifen: lowers letrozole levels by about 38% on average; using the two together is not recommended

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Bone density (DEXA) scan
  • Cholesterol (lipid panel)
  • Pregnancy test if pregnancy is possible
  • Men (off-label): testosterone, estradiol, LH, FSH and semen analysis

While taking it

  • Bone density at intervals
  • Cholesterol periodically
  • Men (off-label): testosterone, estradiol and semen analysis

The label advises considering bone density and cholesterol monitoring, and a pregnancy test before starting in women who could become pregnant; there is no label guidance for men, where studies tracked hormones and semen.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis20226,522 infertile women with PCOS in 41 randomized trials

    Aromatase inhibitors (letrozole) for ovulation induction in infertile women with polycystic ovary syndrome

    Compared with clomiphene, letrozole raised live birth and pregnancy rates (high-certainty evidence), with similar rates of ovarian hyperstimulation, miscarriage and multiple pregnancy. 3

  2. Meta-analysis2020Infertile or low-testosterone men in 8 studies of testolactone, anastrozole or letrozole

    A systematic review and meta-analysis of clinical trials implementing aromatase inhibitors to treat male infertility

    Aromatase inhibitors improved all hormone and semen measures studied and were well tolerated; the authors called for randomized, placebo-controlled trials. 4

  3. Label2024Postmenopausal women with early breast cancer (BIG 1-98 trial, compared with tamoxifen)

    FEMARA (letrozole) tablets: Prescribing Information

    Letrozole caused more high cholesterol, fractures and osteoporosis than tamoxifen, while blood clots were less common (3.2% versus 4.6%). 1

Questions for your clinician

  • Do I need a bone density scan and cholesterol test before starting, and how will we follow them?
  • For fertility treatment: what are the trade-offs of letrozole versus clomiphene for me?
  • For men: is there a medical reason to lower my estrogen, and what are the risks of doing so?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for breast cancer; other uses off-label. Approved for postmenopausal women with hormone receptor-positive early breast cancer (including extended treatment after prior tamoxifen) and for advanced breast cancer. Use to induce ovulation or in men is off-label, and WADA bans aromatase inhibitors, including letrozole, in sport. 12
