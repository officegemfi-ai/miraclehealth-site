What it is

Coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) is a substance made naturally in the body, with the highest amounts in the heart, liver, kidneys and pancreas. It is an essential cofactor for energy production in cells and also acts as an antioxidant, and low levels in heart muscle are related to how severe heart failure is. It is sold in the United States as a dietary supplement.

Limited evidence

One 420-patient heart failure trial found fewer major heart events, but NIH calls the heart evidence inconclusive, pooled trials show no help for statin muscle pain, and migraine evidence is small.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Fewer major heart events and deaths over 2 years when added to standard care in one trial of 420 people with moderate to severe heart failure 21

  • Limited evidence

    May reduce how often and how long migraines last, though not how severe they are; the amount of evidence and the effects are small 3

  • Limited evidence

    May reduce some complications of heart surgery and heart damage from one type of chemotherapy drug 1

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    Mild insomnia or digestive upset 1

  • Not known

    May interact with the blood thinner warfarin and with insulin 13

  • Not known

    May not be compatible with some types of cancer treatment 1

Talk to a clinician first if you are

  • Taking warfarin (clinician first)
  • Taking insulin (clinician first)
  • Having cancer treatment (clinician first)

Interactions

  • Warfarin: CoQ10 may interact with it
  • Insulin: CoQ10 may interact with it
  • Some cancer treatments: CoQ10 may not be compatible

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

None routinely needed.

While taking it

None routinely needed.

No routine tests are needed; people on warfarin or insulin should ask whether extra INR or blood sugar checks make sense.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis2020321 patients with statin-associated muscle pain in 7 randomized trials

    Effect of Coenzyme Q10 on statin-associated myalgia and adherence to statin therapy: A systematic review and meta-analysis

    CoQ10 did not reduce muscle pain or help people stay on statins; the trials were small and single-center, and the one clearly positive trial was industry-supported. 4

  2. RCT2014420 adults with moderate to severe chronic heart failure

    The effect of coenzyme Q10 on morbidity and mortality in chronic heart failure: results from Q-SYMBIO: a randomized double-blind trial

    Over 2 years, major cardiovascular events occurred in 15% on CoQ10 versus 26% on placebo, with fewer deaths; symptom and heart measures at 16 weeks did not change. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • Is CoQ10 worth adding to my heart failure treatment, given the limited evidence?
  • If I get muscle aches on a statin, what should we try, since CoQ10 hasn't helped in pooled trials?
  • Do I need extra INR or blood sugar checks if I take it with warfarin or insulin?

Status in the US

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement. Sold in the United States as a dietary supplement. NIH's complementary health center reports no serious side effects but notes possible interactions with warfarin, insulin and some cancer treatments. 1
