What it isevidence librarian

Pancragen is a lab-made chain of four amino acids: lysine, glutamic acid, aspartic acid and tryptophan (Lys-Glu-Asp-Trp, or KEDW). The US National Library of Medicine indexes it under that name. It comes from Vladimir Khavinson's peptide research in Russia, where it is listed as a dietary supplement. In cell studies it switched on genes that pancreatic cells need to mature and make hormones. Its developers patented it to lower blood sugar in diabetes.

Limited evidence

The evidence is cell, rat and monkey studies plus two small human studies in diabetes, one in a journal and one in a patent. All involve the developer's group.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Limited evidence

    Lower fasting blood sugar, insulin and insulin resistance in a small study of older adults with type 2 diabetes 5

  • Limited evidence

    Needed less insulin (and fewer needed more) in a small study of 36 people with diabetes described in the developer's patent; see the low-blood-sugar risk below 6

  • Animal and lab studies

    Faster clearing of sugar from the blood and normal insulin responses in old rhesus monkeys 7

  • Animal and lab studies

    Lower blood sugar, more insulin and better survival in rats with chemically induced diabetes 6

  • Animal and lab studies

    Restored markers of mature pancreatic cells in aging cell cultures 89

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • In theory

    Low blood sugar is possible if combined with insulin or other diabetes medicines, given its reported glucose-lowering effect 65

  • Not known

    Human safety has only been reported by the developers; the patent calls it non-toxic without describing the tests 6

  • Not known

    No FDA-approved or pharmacy-compounded version exists, so products may not match the peptide that was studied 1013

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Diabetes treated with insulin or other glucose-lowering medicines, unless your doctor is involved
  • Pancreatitis or other pancreas disease, unless a doctor is involved
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (never studied)
  • Under 18 (never studied)

What it can clash with

  • Insulin and other glucose-lowering medicines: possible added blood-sugar lowering (not formally studied)

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. Trial201163 older adults: 33 with type 2 diabetes and 30 healthy

    Prospects of Using Pancragen for Correction of Metabolic Disorders in Elderly People

    In people with diabetes who took pancragen, fasting glucose, glucose tolerance, insulin and insulin resistance improved, while those who did not take it showed no change. The abstract does not describe randomization. 5

  2. Animal2014Old and young female rhesus monkeys

    Impact of tetrapeptide pancragen on endocrine function of the pancreas in old monkeys

    In old monkeys, pancragen sped up clearance of sugar after a glucose load and normalized insulin and C-peptide responses; part of the effect lasted three weeks after it was stopped. 7

  3. Cell2013Young and aged pancreatic cell cultures

    Effects of Pancragen on The Differentiation of Pancreatic Cells During Their Ageing

    Aged cultures lost markers of mature pancreatic cells; pancragen raised markers such as Pdx1 and Ptf1a back toward, or above, young levels. 8

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Could this cause low blood sugar with my current diabetes medicines?
  • Which blood sugar tests should I track, and how often?
  • Is there stronger evidence for other options to manage my blood sugar?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

Not approved Not FDA-approved; not on FDA compounding lists. Pancragen is not an FDA-approved drug and appears on none of FDA's compounding lists (nominated substances, updated May 2026; safety-risk and withdrawn nominations, April 2026). FDA allows pharmacy compounding only from ingredients that are in an approved drug, meet a USP standard, or are on or under review for its bulk list; Russia's drug register lists it as a dietary supplement. 1234
