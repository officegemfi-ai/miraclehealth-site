Dulaglutide

Also called Trulicity

A prescription injection, sold as Trulicity, that activates GLP-1 receptors to lower blood sugar in type 2 diabetes and reduce the risk of heart attack and stroke.

What it is

Dulaglutide is a lab-made protein built from two identical chains, each joining a GLP-1-like peptide (90% the same as natural human GLP-1) to part of an antibody, the Fc portion of human IgG4. It switches on GLP-1 receptors, which raises insulin release when blood sugar is high, lowers glucagon and slows stomach emptying. Its half-life is about 5 days, and it is given as an injection under the skin. It is approved for type 2 diabetes, not for weight management.

Strong evidence

FDA approval for blood sugar control and heart-risk reduction, based on randomized trials including REWIND, which followed 9,901 adults with type 2 diabetes for a median of 5.4 years.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Lower blood sugar in adults with type 2 diabetes: A1C fell about 0.7 to 1.1 points more than with placebo when added to other diabetes medicines 1

  • Strong evidence

    Fewer heart attacks, strokes and cardiovascular deaths in adults with type 2 diabetes, about a third of whom had known heart disease: 12.0% versus 13.4% with placebo over a median of 5.4 years (hazard ratio 0.88) 31

  • Strong evidence

    Approved to improve blood sugar control in children and teens 10 and older with type 2 diabetes, based on a 26-week placebo-controlled trial of 154 young people 12

  • Strong evidence

    Modest weight loss in adults with type 2 diabetes: about 2 to 3 kg more than with placebo or insulin glargine in label trials (it is not approved for weight loss) 1

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting and stomach pain: nausea affected about 12% to 21% versus 5% on placebo, and 1% to 4% stopped because of digestive side effects versus 0.2% 1

  • Common

    Low blood sugar when combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea: in trials with mealtime insulin, 69% to 77% had low readings and about 3% had severe episodes 1

  • Rare

    Acute pancreatitis, including rare bleeding or tissue-destroying forms that can be fatal: confirmed cases ran about 1.4 per 1,000 patient-years versus 0.88 with comparison drugs 12

  • Rare

    Acute kidney injury, sometimes needing dialysis, mostly after dehydration from vomiting or diarrhea (reported after marketing); breathing stomach contents into the lungs under general anesthesia or deep sedation has also been reported rarely, so the label asks people to tell their care team before any planned procedure 12

  • Not known

    Gallbladder disease, such as gallstones or inflammation, and worsening diabetic eye disease in people with a history of diabetic retinopathy (both label warnings) 1

  • In theory

    Boxed warning: thyroid C-cell tumors in rats; whether this happens in humans is unknown 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2, or a past serious allergic reaction to dulaglutide (contraindicated)
  • Severe digestive disease, including severe gastroparesis (not recommended)
  • History of pancreatitis (not studied in this group)
  • History of diabetic retinopathy (needs closer eye monitoring)
  • Pregnancy or planning pregnancy (animal studies showed harm; the label says to use it only if the benefit justifies the risk)

Interactions

  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Oral medicines with a narrow safety margin, such as warfarin: slower stomach emptying can change absorption, so levels need monitoring

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • HbA1c or fasting glucose
  • Kidney function (creatinine, eGFR)
  • Eye exam if you have diabetic retinopathy

While taking it

  • HbA1c to track blood sugar
  • Blood glucose if also taking insulin or a sulfonylurea
  • Kidney function if you have kidney disease and severe vomiting or diarrhea
  • Eye checks if you have diabetic retinopathy
  • INR if taking warfarin

The label calls for watching kidney function in people with kidney impairment who have severe digestive side effects, eye disease in people with a history of diabetic retinopathy, blood sugar with insulin or sulfonylureas, and narrow-margin drugs such as warfarin; it says routine calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound checks are of uncertain value.

Key studies

  1. Label20209,901 adults with type 2 diabetes, 31.5% with established heart disease (REWIND trial)

    TRULICITY (dulaglutide) injection, for subcutaneous use: Prescribing Information

    Over a median of 5.4 years, a first heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death occurred in 12.0% with dulaglutide versus 13.4% with placebo (hazard ratio 0.88, 95% CI 0.79 to 0.99). 3

  2. Label2024Adults with type 2 diabetes in placebo-controlled add-on trials

    TRULICITY (dulaglutide) injection, for subcutaneous use: Prescribing Information

    A1C fell about 0.7 to 1.1 points more than with placebo, and nausea affected about 12% to 21% versus 5%. About 1.6% developed antibodies to the drug, with no effect on how well it worked. 1

Questions for your clinician

  • Does my heart risk make dulaglutide's heart benefit relevant to me?
  • Do my insulin or sulfonylurea doses need review to avoid low blood sugar?
  • Should I tell my surgeon or anesthesia team before any procedure with sedation?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes and heart-risk reduction. Approved as Trulicity to improve blood sugar control in adults and children 10 and older with type 2 diabetes, and to lower the risk of heart attack, stroke and cardiovascular death in adults with type 2 diabetes and heart disease or several heart risk factors. FDA updated the label in May 2025 and March 2026 with stronger pancreatitis wording (including fatal forms seen with GLP-1 drugs), revised acute kidney injury wording, a severe digestive reactions warning, and postmarketing reports of bowel blockage. 12
