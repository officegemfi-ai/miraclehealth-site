What it isevidence librarian

Iron is an essential part of hemoglobin, the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. It is also part of myoglobin, which supplies oxygen to muscles. Food contains heme iron, from meat, seafood and poultry, and nonheme iron, the only form in plants and fortified foods. Deficiency develops in stages: first depleted stores, then iron-poor production of red blood cells, and finally iron deficiency anemia.

Strong evidence

Dozens of randomized trials show that iron supplements raise hemoglobin and iron stores and reduce anemia. Oral iron is the first-line treatment for iron deficiency.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Strong evidence

    Reduces anemia and iron deficiency and raises hemoglobin in menstruating women (67 trials, 8,506 women) 2

  • Limited evidence

    Appears to reduce fatigue and improve exercise performance in menstruating women 2

  • Moderate evidence

    Lowers the risk of iron deficiency anemia in pregnancy, though US groups differ on routine supplementation 1

  • Strong evidence

    Oral iron is first-line treatment for iron deficiency with or without anemia that is not severe; IV iron is preferred when pills fail, aren't tolerated or aren't absorbed 3

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Common

    Constipation, diarrhea, nausea, indigestion and metallic taste; in trials of menstruating women, bowel side effects were about twice as common as without iron 231

  • Rare

    Accidental overdose can be fatal in young children; it caused about a third of US child poisoning deaths between 1983 and 1991 1

  • Rare

    Iron overload in people with hemochromatosis, which untreated can lead to liver cirrhosis and heart disease 1

  • Not known

    In anemia of chronic disease, supplementation is controversial because it may raise infection and cardiovascular risk 1

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Hemochromatosis or other iron overload
  • Anemia of chronic disease or inflammation (doctor first)
  • Unexplained iron deficiency, which can come from blood loss in the gut or from colon cancer (get evaluated first)

What it can clash with

  • Levodopa: iron may reduce its absorption and effectiveness
  • Levothyroxine: iron can reduce its effectiveness
  • Proton pump inhibitors: reduce iron absorption and may blunt the response to iron supplements
  • Calcium may reduce iron absorption, and iron supplements can lower zinc absorption

Blood tests to watchmarkers agent

Before starting

  • Complete blood count (hemoglobin and hematocrit)
  • Serum ferritin
  • Transferrin saturation or full iron studies if inflammation may make ferritin misleading

While taking it

  • Complete blood count and ferritin to check the response

Ferritin reflects iron stores and is the most efficient test for diagnosing iron deficiency, but inflammation raises it, and it reads misleadingly high soon after IV iron.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. Systematic review20168,506 menstruating women in 67 trials

    Daily iron supplementation for improving anaemia, iron status and health in menstruating women

    Daily iron reduced anemia and iron deficiency, raised hemoglobin and iron stores, improved exercise performance and appeared to reduce fatigue, with about twice the rate of bowel side effects. 2

  2. Review2025Females with iron deficiency (review of guidelines and studies)

    Diagnosis and management of iron deficiency in females

    Recommends diagnosing with ferritin, adding full iron studies when inflammation is present, starting with oral iron, and using IV iron when oral iron fails, is not tolerated, is not absorbed or anemia is severe. 3

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Do my ferritin and hemoglobin results show that I need iron, and what is causing my deficiency?
  • Does iron interact with my thyroid, Parkinson's or acid-reducing medicines?
  • If iron pills upset my stomach or don't work, is IV iron an option for me?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement (child-poisoning warning). Sold as a dietary supplement. FDA requires iron-containing products to warn that accidental overdose is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6. 1
