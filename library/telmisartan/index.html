What it is

Telmisartan, sold as Micardis, is an angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB). It works by stopping the hormone angiotensin II from binding to its AT1 receptor in blood vessel walls and the adrenal gland. It is approved for high blood pressure and to lower the risk of heart attack, stroke and heart-related death in people 55 and older at high risk who cannot take ACE inhibitors. In lab studies, it also partly activates PPAR-gamma, a receptor involved in metabolism.

Limited evidence

Large trials support its approved blood pressure and heart uses, but its use for healthy aging rests on metabolic findings, such as a meta-analysis of 15 small trials showing less belly fat in people with high blood pressure and metabolic problems, not on aging outcomes.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Lowers blood pressure (approved use) 1

  • Strong evidence

    Matched the ACE inhibitor ramipril for preventing heart-related death, heart attack, stroke or heart-failure hospital stays in 25,620 high-risk patients (16.7% versus 16.5%) 1

  • Strong evidence

    No benefit shown on the main outcome: in 5,926 high-risk patients unable to take ACE inhibitors, it did not significantly reduce the main combined outcome versus placebo (15.7% versus 17.0%), though heart-related death, heart attack or stroke was slightly lower (13.0% versus 14.8%) 1

  • Limited evidence

    Less belly (visceral) fat but no change in fat under the skin, compared with other treatments, in a meta-analysis of 15 small trials in people with high blood pressure plus overweight, metabolic syndrome or glucose intolerance 2

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    Boxed warning: drugs acting on this hormone system can injure or kill a developing baby 1

  • Not known

    Low blood pressure, especially in people low on salt or fluids 1

  • Not known

    High potassium and changes in kidney function, especially when combined with other drugs that act on the same hormone system 1

  • Uncommon

    Back pain, sinus infection and diarrhea were the most common side effects in blood pressure trials 1

  • Uncommon

    In high-risk heart patients, leg pain from narrowed arteries (intermittent claudication) and skin ulcers were reported at least 1% more often than with placebo 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or planning pregnancy (boxed warning)
  • Past severe allergic reaction to telmisartan, such as anaphylaxis or angioedema
  • Diabetes and taking aliskiren
  • Already taking an ACE inhibitor or another drug that blocks the same hormone system (combination not recommended)
  • Liver problems (needs a clinician first)

Interactions

  • Aliskiren: not to be combined in people with diabetes, and avoided with reduced kidney function
  • ACE inhibitors such as ramipril: no added benefit and more kidney problems in a large trial
  • Lithium: levels can rise to toxic; monitoring is needed
  • Digoxin: levels should be monitored when telmisartan is started, changed or stopped
  • NSAIDs, including COX-2 inhibitors: risk of kidney injury and weaker blood pressure lowering

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Potassium
  • Kidney function (creatinine, eGFR)

While taking it

  • Potassium and kidney function, especially with other drugs acting on the same hormone system
  • Lithium or digoxin levels if either is taken too

The label warns that telmisartan can raise potassium and change kidney function and calls for monitoring lithium and digoxin levels when combined; no monitoring plan exists for use in healthy aging.

Key studies

  1. Label202225,620 (ONTARGET) and 5,926 (TRANSCEND) adults at high cardiovascular risk

    MICARDIS (telmisartan) tablets: prescribing information

    In ONTARGET, telmisartan matched ramipril on heart-related death, heart attack, stroke or heart-failure hospitalization (hazard ratio 1.01), and combining the two gave no extra benefit but more kidney problems. In TRANSCEND, it did not significantly beat placebo on the main outcome (hazard ratio 0.92). 1

  2. Meta-analysis201615 randomized trials in adults with high blood pressure plus overweight, metabolic syndrome or glucose intolerance

    Effects of telmisartan on fat distribution: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

    Telmisartan lowered visceral fat area (by about 18 square centimeters) and total cholesterol slightly versus control, with no change in fat under the skin; the trials were few and varied. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • What would telmisartan do for me if my blood pressure is normal?
  • Which of my medicines or supplements, such as potassium, NSAIDs or lithium, could interact with it?
  • What potassium and kidney checks would I need?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for high blood pressure; aging off-label. Micardis is approved to treat high blood pressure and to lower the risk of heart attack, stroke and heart-related death in people 55 and older at high risk who cannot take ACE inhibitors. It carries a boxed warning for fetal toxicity; use for healthy aging is off-label. 1
