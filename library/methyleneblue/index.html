What it isevidence librarian

Methylene blue is a water-soluble thiazine dye. As the prescription injection ProvayBlue it treats acquired methemoglobinemia, a condition in which some hemoglobin cannot carry oxygen. It works by helping convert that hemoglobin back to its normal form. It is also sold online as drops and other wellness products for focus, mood and energy. These are not FDA-approved, and some are industrial grade rather than pharmaceutical grade.

Limited evidence

It is approved only for methemoglobinemia. For focus in healthy people, the evidence is two small single-dose brain-scan studies. A related compound failed its main analyses in a large Alzheimer's trial.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Strong evidence

    Treats acquired methemoglobinemia in children and adults (approved use, as an IV drug) 1

  • Limited evidence

    About 7% more correct answers on a short-term memory test, plus more brain activity during attention and memory tasks, after a single dose in a placebo-controlled study of 26 healthy adults 3

  • Limited evidence

    Changes in resting brain connectivity on scans after a single dose in 28 healthy adults; thinking skills were not measured 4

  • Moderate evidence

    No benefit shown: a related compound (LMTM, a stabilized reduced form of methylene blue) did not slow decline in the main planned analyses of a 15-month trial in 891 people with mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease 5

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Not known

    Boxed warning: serious or fatal serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRI or SNRI antidepressants, MAO inhibitors or opioids; the risk with oral wellness products has not been studied, so the same caution applies 12

  • Not known

    Breakdown of red blood cells (hemolytic anemia), especially in people with G6PD deficiency, in whom it must not be used 1

  • Not known

    Confusion, dizziness and vision disturbances that can affect driving 1

  • Not known

    Headache, low potassium, diarrhea, low magnesium, muscle jerks, nausea and seizure-like events, each in at least 2 in 100 patients given the injection 1

  • Common

    Blue urine, which can interfere with urine tests; it can also make pulse oximeter oxygen readings falsely low 14

  • Not known

    Unregulated products: wellness products are not FDA-approved and vary widely in quality, and some are industrial-grade dye not meant to be swallowed 2

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Taking antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs, MAO inhibitors), opioids or other serotonergic drugs (boxed warning)
  • G6PD deficiency (contraindicated)
  • Allergy to methylene blue or other thiazine dyes
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (it may harm a fetus, and the label advises pausing breastfeeding after treatment)
  • Kidney or liver disease (slower clearance may raise the risk of side effects)

What it can clash with

  • SSRIs, SNRIs and MAO inhibitors: serious or fatal serotonin syndrome (boxed warning)
  • Bupropion, buspirone, clomipramine, mirtazapine, linezolid, opioids and dextromethorphan: avoid together because of serotonin syndrome
  • St. John's wort and other serotonergic supplements: caution advised
  • Drugs broken down by liver CYP enzymes: methylene blue blocks several of these enzymes in lab studies

Blood tests to watchmarkers agent

Before starting

  • G6PD test (G6PD deficiency rules it out)

While taking it

None routinely needed.

G6PD deficiency is a contraindication because of the risk of red-cell breakdown; no monitoring plan exists for wellness use.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. RCT2016891 people with mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease (15 months)

    Efficacy and safety of tau-aggregation inhibitor therapy in patients with mild or moderate Alzheimer's disease: a randomised, controlled, double-blind, parallel-arm, phase 3 trial

    LMTM, a methylene blue-related compound, did not beat a low control dose in the planned main analyses. People taking it without other Alzheimer's drugs appeared to do better, a pattern the authors could not explain. Gut and urinary effects were the most common side effects. 5

  2. RCT201626 healthy adults aged 22 to 62

    Multimodal randomized functional MR imaging of the effects of methylene blue in the human brain

    After a single dose, brain activity rose during attention and short-term memory tasks, and correct memory answers rose 7% compared with placebo. 3

  3. RCT201728 healthy adults (average age about 30)

    Methylene blue modulates functional connectivity in the human brain

    A single dose changed blood flow and resting connectivity in brain networks; behavior was not measured, and the only side effect noted was temporary urine discoloration. 4

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Could methylene blue interact with my antidepressant, pain medicine or other prescriptions, and would stopping either one be safe?
  • Should I be tested for G6PD deficiency?
  • Is there good evidence it would help my focus or energy, and what else could I try?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

FDA-approved FDA-approved injection for methemoglobinemia only. ProvayBlue is a prescription injection approved for children and adults with acquired methemoglobinemia, with a boxed warning about serotonin syndrome. Methylene blue drops and capsules sold for wellness are not FDA-approved, and pharmacist reviewers warn that some products are industrial rather than pharmaceutical grade. 12
