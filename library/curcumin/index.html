What it is

Turmeric (Curcuma longa) is a plant in the ginger family grown mainly in India and Southeast Asia; its underground stem (rhizome) is used in cooking and was used in traditional medicine for indigestion, colds, skin infections, arthritis and liver disease. Curcumin, which gives turmeric its yellow color, belongs to a group of compounds called curcuminoids that are thought to account for its effects. Products vary in how much curcumin they contain, and many add piperine from black pepper, which raises how much curcumin the body absorbs; piperine was in 3 of 7 products tested in US liver-injury cases.

Limited evidence

Several meta-analyses of small trials suggest less knee osteoarthritis pain, but NIH says higher-quality studies are needed and there isn't enough evidence to confirm any health benefit.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Less knee osteoarthritis pain and better function than placebo, similar to NSAID pain relievers, in a 2021 review of 10 studies (1,287 people); NIH calls this initial evidence that needs higher-quality confirmation 21

  • Limited evidence

    Less muscle soreness and lower muscle-damage markers after exercise in a 2020 review of 11 studies 2

  • Limited evidence

    Some benefit in early studies of fatty liver disease, though it is unclear which measures improve consistently 1

Risks and side effects

  • Rare

    Liver injury, usually starting 1 to 4 months after beginning a turmeric supplement; of 10 US cases in a national registry, 5 people were hospitalized and 1 died of liver failure. Cases have risen since 2011 and are strongly linked to the gene variant HLA-B*35:01 31

  • Not known

    Stomach upset, nausea, vomiting, acid reflux, diarrhea or constipation 1

  • Not known

    Turmeric supplements may be unsafe in pregnancy, and a professional monograph advises avoiding them while pregnant or breastfeeding 14

  • Not known

    Long-term safety is unknown; it is usually well tolerated in short studies, but the rare liver injuries typically began 1 to 4 months after starting 13

  • Not known

    Bleeding: it may add to the effect of blood thinners, antiplatelet drugs and NSAIDs, and one case report described a sudden rise in INR on warfarin 4

  • Not known

    Hives or itching when used on the skin 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Gallstones or a blocked bile duct
  • Liver disease or past liver injury from a supplement (clinician first)
  • Bleeding disorder, upcoming surgery, or taking a blood thinner (clinician first)
  • Taking other prescription medicines (clinician first)

Interactions

  • Blood thinners, antiplatelet drugs and NSAIDs (such as warfarin, apixaban, aspirin or ibuprofen): possible added bleeding risk
  • Medicines cleared by the liver enzymes CYP3A or CYP2D6 (such as tacrolimus): turmeric may raise their levels; a case report linked it to high tacrolimus levels
  • Piperine (black pepper extract): often added to raise curcumin absorption; it was in 3 of 7 products tested in US liver-injury cases, and researchers suggest the combination may help explain rising cases

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

None routinely needed.

While taking it

None routinely needed.

No routine tests are needed, but tiredness, dark urine or yellow skin or eyes should prompt stopping it and calling a clinician, who may check liver tests.

Key studies

  1. Systematic review20211,287 people with knee osteoarthritis in 10 studies

    Therapeutic effects of turmeric or curcumin extract on pain and function for individuals with knee osteoarthritis

    Turmeric or curcumin may improve knee pain and function compared with placebo, with effects similar to NSAIDs; the best amount, frequency and form remain unclear. Summarized by NIH; the paper itself was not opened. 2

  2. Case series202310 people with liver injury in a US registry (2004 to 2022)

    Liver Injury Associated with Turmeric-A Growing Problem: Ten Cases from the Drug-Induced Liver Injury Network [DILIN]

    Injury was mostly of the liver-cell (hepatocellular) type and began 1 to 4 months after starting; 5 were hospitalized and 1 died. Seven of the 10 carried HLA-B*35:01, and the authors say cases appear to be increasing. 3

Questions for your clinician

  • Is curcumin likely to help my joint pain, and how would we judge whether it is working?
  • Which liver symptoms should make me stop it and call you?
  • Could it interact with any of my medicines?

Status in the US

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement; also a common spice. Sold as a dietary supplement and widely used as a cooking spice. Supplements are not approved by FDA before they are sold; manufacturers are responsible for their safety and labeling. 1
