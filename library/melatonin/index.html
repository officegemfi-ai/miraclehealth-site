What it is

Melatonin is a hormone the brain produces in response to darkness. It helps set the timing of the body's internal clock (circadian rhythm) and sleep, and light at night can block its production. In the US it is sold over the counter as a dietary supplement, while several other countries treat it as a prescription drug.

Moderate evidence

A meta-analysis of 19 randomized trials shows modest effects on falling asleep, and reviews support it for delayed sleep timing and possibly jet lag, but guidelines do not recommend it for chronic insomnia and long-term safety data are lacking.

What it may do

  • Moderate evidence

    Fell asleep about 7 minutes sooner and slept about 8 minutes longer than with placebo, with slightly better sleep quality, in people with primary sleep disorders (19 trials, 1,683 people) 5

  • Moderate evidence

    Fell asleep about 34 minutes earlier, with better daytime functioning, in a 2018 trial of 307 people with delayed sleep-wake phase disorder 1

  • Limited evidence

    May ease jet lag, mainly after eastward flights, though the evidence is of low quality 1

  • Limited evidence

    Children with autism fell asleep 37 minutes earlier and slept 48 minutes longer, and children with ADHD 20 minutes earlier and 33 minutes longer, in reviews of small, short trials 1

  • Moderate evidence

    Less anxiety before surgery than with placebo, according to a review of trials 1

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    Mild side effects in short-term studies, such as headache, dizziness, nausea and sleepiness 1

  • Common

    Labels are often wrong: in a 2023 study, 22 of 25 gummy products were mislabeled, holding 74% to 347% of the stated amount (another had none), and in a 2017 study of 31 supplements most did not match their labels and 26% contained serotonin 1

  • Not known

    Accidental swallowing by young children: about 11,000 emergency visits for children 5 and under in 2019 to 2022. Poison-center reports for ages 19 and under rose from 8,337 in 2012 to 52,563 in 2021; most had no symptoms, but 4,097 were hospitalized over the decade, 287 needed intensive care and two children under 2 died (the data don't show whether melatonin caused the deaths) 1

  • In theory

    Long-term safety is unknown; in children, possible effects on puberty and hormone development have been raised but not established 1

  • Not known

    Next-day drowsiness, especially in older adults, in whom melatonin may stay active longer 1

Talk to a clinician first if you are

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (safety has not been researched)
  • People with dementia (a 2015 American Academy of Sleep Medicine guideline recommends against it)
  • Children, unless a health care provider advises it
  • Epilepsy, or use of blood thinners, without medical supervision

Interactions

  • Blood thinners (anticoagulants): use only under medical supervision
  • Epilepsy: people with epilepsy should take it only under medical supervision

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

None routinely needed.

While taking it

None routinely needed.

No blood tests are needed; benefit is judged by sleep, and NCCIH advises checking with a health care provider before giving melatonin to a child.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis20131,683 people with primary sleep disorders in 19 trials

    Meta-analysis: melatonin for the treatment of primary sleep disorders

    Melatonin shortened the time to fall asleep by about 7 minutes and lengthened sleep by about 8 minutes versus placebo and slightly improved sleep quality; the effects were modest but did not fade with continued use. 5

Questions for your clinician

  • Is my sleep problem the kind melatonin may help, such as jet lag or a delayed sleep schedule, or would CBT for insomnia be better?
  • Could melatonin interact with my medicines, such as a blood thinner or seizure medicine?
  • If it is for my child, is it appropriate, and how should we store it safely?

Status in the US

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement in the US; prescription elsewhere. Sold in the US as a dietary supplement, which FDA does not approve for safety or effectiveness before sale; several other countries regulate it as a prescription drug. FDA also lists melatonin in Category 1 (under evaluation) for pharmacy compounding, which lets pharmacies compound it while FDA decides. 1234
