Exemestane

Also called Aromasin

A prescription pill that irreversibly blocks aromatase, the enzyme that makes estrogen, approved for hormone-sensitive breast cancer after menopause; studies in men are few and brief.

What it is

Exemestane is a steroidal aromatase inactivator: it acts as a false substrate, and its metabolite binds the enzyme's active site and inactivates it irreversibly. It lowers circulating estrogens by 85% to 95% at peak effect and cuts the body's estrogen production by 98%, without affecting cortisol or aldosterone. In a small 10-day study of young men, it raised testosterone while lowering estradiol.

Strong evidence

FDA-approved for breast cancer after menopause, and a trial in 4,560 women found it lowered breast cancer risk over about 3 years (off-label); in men, the main data come from a 10-day study of 12 young men.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Treats estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women, as follow-on treatment after tamoxifen and for advanced disease 1

  • Moderate evidence

    Cut invasive breast cancer by 65% (0.19% versus 0.55% a year) over about 3 years in 4,560 postmenopausal women at moderately increased risk (MAP.3; off-label for prevention) 3

  • Limited evidence

    Raised testosterone by about 60% and lowered estradiol by about 38% over 10 days in 12 healthy young men (off-label) 4

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Hot flashes (21%), fatigue (16%), joint pain (15%), headache (13%), insomnia (12%) and sweating (12%) in women with early breast cancer; in the prevention trial, more hot flashes (40% versus 32%) and joint pain, and worse menopause-related quality of life 13

  • Uncommon

    Bone loss: bone density falls over time; osteoporosis (4.6% versus 2.8%) and fractures (4.2% versus 3.1%) were more common than with tamoxifen 1

  • Uncommon

    Heart ischemic events in 1.6% versus 0.6% with tamoxifen 1

  • Not known

    Blood test changes, including low lymphocyte counts and raised liver tests, bilirubin, alkaline phosphatase and creatinine 1

  • Not known

    Harm to a fetus; it is not meant for premenopausal women 1

  • Not known

    In men, long-term effects on bone, cholesterol and sexual function have not been studied; the main data come from a 10-day study of 12 young men 4

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnancy or possible pregnancy, and breastfeeding
  • Premenopausal women (it is not indicated for them)
  • Osteoporosis or high fracture risk without a bone-protection plan
  • Vitamin D deficiency that has not been checked and treated
  • Past allergic reaction to exemestane

Interactions

  • Strong CYP3A4 inducers (rifampin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital, St. John's wort): lower exemestane levels
  • Estrogen-containing products: should not be taken with it, as they may interfere with its action

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Bone density (DEXA) scan
  • 25-hydroxy vitamin D
  • Pregnancy test if pregnancy is possible
  • Men (off-label): testosterone and estradiol

While taking it

  • Bone density at intervals
  • Men (off-label): testosterone and estradiol

The label calls for bone density assessment in women with or at risk of osteoporosis, a vitamin D level before starting and pregnancy testing in women who could become pregnant; there is no label guidance for men.

Key studies

  1. RCT20114,560 postmenopausal women at moderately increased risk of breast cancer

    Exemestane for Breast-Cancer Prevention in Postmenopausal Women

    Over a median of 35 months, invasive breast cancer fell by 65% with exemestane versus placebo; fractures, heart events and other cancers did not differ, but hot flashes and joint pain were more common. 3

  2. Trial200312 healthy young men aged 14 to 26

    Pharmacokinetics and dose finding of a potent aromatase inhibitor, aromasin (exemestane), in young males

    Over 10 days, estradiol fell by about a third and testosterone rose by more than half; lipids and IGF-I did not change. Long-term efficacy and safety were not studied. 4

  3. Label2024Postmenopausal women with early breast cancer who switched from tamoxifen, compared with continuing tamoxifen

    AROMASIN (exemestane) tablets: Prescribing Information

    Exemestane caused more joint pain, osteoporosis and fractures than continuing tamoxifen, and slightly more heart ischemic events. 1

Questions for your clinician

  • Should I have a bone density scan and vitamin D test before starting?
  • Which joint, muscle or mood symptoms should I report?
  • For men: what evidence supports it in my situation, given how little it has been studied in men?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for breast cancer; off-label in men. Approved for postmenopausal women with estrogen receptor-positive early breast cancer who switch to it after 2 to 3 years of tamoxifen, and for advanced breast cancer that progressed on tamoxifen. Use for breast cancer prevention or in men is off-label, and WADA bans aromatase inhibitors, including exemestane, in sport. 12
