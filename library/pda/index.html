What it isevidence librarian

Pentadeca arginate (PDA) is the name used for a form of BPC-157 combined with the amino acid arginine. BPC-157 is a lab-made peptide of 15 amino acids (a 'pentadecapeptide') taken from a protein first isolated from human gastric juice. No peer-reviewed study or FDA document about PDA itself was found. So its exact make-up, and whether it acts like other forms of BPC-157, cannot be confirmed. FDA notes that several salts and derivatives are sold under the BPC-157 name.

Animal and lab studies

No studies of PDA itself were found. The claims rest on animal research on BPC-157, whose few small human studies FDA judged insufficient.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Animal and lab studies

    Faster tendon healing and protection of the stomach, intestines and liver in rodent studies of BPC-157, the parent peptide; PDA itself has not been tested 2

  • Animal and lab studies

    Colon fistulas about 40% smaller than in untreated rats, and closed by day 28, with BPC-157 2

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Not known

    No human safety data for PDA, and too little for BPC-157 to describe its side effects 2

  • In theory

    Immune reactions, which FDA called a possibly significant risk for BPC-157 that clumping or impurities could make worse 2

  • In theory

    Unknown cancer risk: no studies of BPC-157's cancer-causing potential exist 2

  • Not known

    Uncertain identity: FDA says several salts and derivatives are sold under the BPC-157 name, so a patient may get a different substance than the one prescribed 2

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Current or past cancer (cancer risk has not been studied)
  • Competitive athletes (WADA bans non-approved substances such as BPC-157)

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

Status unclear Not approved; not on any FDA compounding list. No PDA product is approved, and 'pentadeca arginate' is not on FDA's May 2026 compounding nominations list or its Category 2 page; FDA's July 2026 BPC-157 review covered only the free base and acetate forms, so this salt's status is unclear.
