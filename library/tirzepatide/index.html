Tirzepatide

Also called Mounjaro, Zepbound, Tirz, Tirze, GLP-1/GIP

A prescription injection, sold as Mounjaro and Zepbound, that activates two hormone receptors (GIP and GLP-1) to lower blood sugar and body weight.

What it is

Tirzepatide is a lab-made peptide that switches on the receptors for two hormones, GIP and GLP-1, which help regulate blood sugar and appetite. It reduces appetite and calorie intake and slows stomach emptying. Its half-life is about 5 to 6 days, and it is given as an injection under the skin.

Strong evidence

Large randomized trials such as SURMOUNT-1 (2,539 adults), plus FDA approval for type 2 diabetes, weight management and obstructive sleep apnea.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    About 15% to 21% average body-weight loss over 72 weeks, depending on dose, in adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes, versus about 3% with placebo 71

  • Strong evidence

    Approved as Mounjaro to improve blood sugar control in adults with type 2 diabetes; in adults with type 2 diabetes and excess weight, about 13% to 15% weight loss over 72 weeks versus about 3% with placebo 21

  • Strong evidence

    Fewer breathing pauses during sleep in adults with obesity and moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea: the apnea-hypopnea index fell by about 51% to 59% over 52 weeks, versus about 3% with placebo 1

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation and stomach pain; nausea affected about 1 in 4 people versus 8% on placebo, and severe digestive reactions about 2% to 3% versus 1% 17

  • Common

    Most lost weight tends to return after stopping: people switched to placebo after 36 weeks of treatment regained about 14% of body weight over the next year 8

  • Uncommon

    Gallbladder problems: gallstones (1.1% versus 1% on placebo) and gallbladder inflammation (0.7% versus 0.2%) 1

  • Rare

    Acute pancreatitis: a labeled warning, though rates matched placebo (0.2%) in the weight-loss trials 1

  • Rare

    Breathing stomach contents into the lungs under general anesthesia or deep sedation: rare reports; the label asks people to tell their care team before any planned procedure 1

  • In theory

    Boxed warning: thyroid C-cell tumors in rats; whether this happens in humans is unknown 12

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • Past serious allergic reaction to tirzepatide
  • Pregnancy (the label says to stop when pregnancy is recognized)
  • Severe gastroparesis (very slow stomach emptying)
  • Type 2 diabetes with diabetic retinopathy (needs closer eye monitoring)

Interactions

  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Birth control pills: can work less well after starting tirzepatide and after each dose increase; the label advises a non-oral method or adding a barrier method during those periods
  • Oral medicines with a narrow safety margin, such as warfarin: slower stomach emptying can change absorption, so levels need monitoring
  • Other tirzepatide products or any GLP-1 drug: using them together is not recommended

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • HbA1c or fasting glucose
  • Kidney function (creatinine, eGFR)
  • Eye exam if you have diabetic retinopathy

While taking it

  • Kidney function if vomiting or diarrhea is severe
  • Blood glucose if also taking insulin or a sulfonylurea
  • Eye checks if you have diabetic retinopathy
  • INR or drug levels if taking warfarin or another narrow-margin drug

The label calls for watching kidney function when digestive side effects could cause dehydration (acute kidney injury has been reported), blood sugar with insulin or sulfonylureas, and diabetic eye disease; it says routine calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound checks are of uncertain value.

Key studies

  1. RCT20222,539 adults with obesity or overweight, without diabetes

    Tirzepatide Once Weekly for the Treatment of Obesity

    Average weight change at 72 weeks was -15.0% to -20.9% across three doses versus -3.1% with placebo. Stopping because of side effects was 4.3% to 7.1% versus 2.6% with placebo. 7

  2. RCT2024670 adults with obesity or overweight who had lost weight on tirzepatide

    Continued treatment with Tirzepatide for maintenance of weight reduction in adults with obesity: The SURMOUNT-4 randomized clinical trial

    After 36 weeks of open-label tirzepatide (average loss 20.9%), those switched to placebo regained 14.0% of body weight over the next 52 weeks, while those who continued lost a further 5.5%. 8

  3. Label2026Adults with obesity and moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea (two 52-week trials)

    ZEPBOUND (tirzepatide) injection, for subcutaneous use: Prescribing Information

    The apnea-hypopnea index fell about 51% and 59% with tirzepatide versus about 3% with placebo. 1

Questions for your clinician

  • What is our plan if I stop, given that most of the weight tends to return?
  • Do I need to change my birth control while starting or increasing tirzepatide?
  • Should I tell my surgeon or anesthesia team before any procedure with sedation?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes, weight and sleep apnea. Approved as Mounjaro (type 2 diabetes) and Zepbound (weight management, and moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity); in early 2026 FDA had the suicidal-thoughts warning removed from Zepbound's label after its review found no increased risk. FDA confirmed the shortage over in December 2024 and the grace periods for pharmacy copies ended in early 2025; in May 2026 FDA proposed keeping tirzepatide off the outsourcing-facility (503B) bulks list, and it warns about compounded, counterfeit and 'research' versions. 123456
