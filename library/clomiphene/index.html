Clomiphene.

Also called Clomid, Clomiphene citrate

A prescription fertility pill that triggers ovulation in women who don't ovulate regularly. Men also take it to raise their own testosterone, which it isn't approved for.

What it isevidence librarian

Clomiphene is a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM) taken as a tablet. It raises the pituitary hormones LH and FSH. In women that leads to ovulation, and in men it stimulates the testes to make testosterone. It is a mix of two forms, enclomiphene and zuclomiphene (30% to 50% of the mix). Zuclomiphene stays in the body for more than a month.

Strong evidence

It is FDA-approved for ovulation induction. In men, a 2025 meta-analysis of 10 randomized trials (819 men) found it raises testosterone, with mixed effects on symptoms.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Strong evidence

    Induces ovulation in women with ovulatory dysfunction; about 30% of treated patients became pregnant in the label's studies 1

  • Moderate evidence

    Raises total testosterone, LH and FSH in men with low testosterone compared with placebo (10 randomized trials, 819 men; moderate-certainty evidence) 2

  • Limited evidence

    Clomiphene or enclomiphene preserved sperm counts better than testosterone gel at similar testosterone levels: men were much less likely to have a low sperm concentration (risk ratio 0.10) 2

  • Limited evidence

    Improved symptom scores (ADAM questionnaire) in mostly observational studies of men with hypogonadism; results in randomized trials were mixed 32

  • Limited evidence

    Lowered IGF-1 by about 41% on average, and to normal in 7 of 16, when added to treatment in men with acromegaly that standard therapy hadn't controlled (small open-label study; off-label) 5

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Common

    Ovarian enlargement (13.6%), hot flashes (10.4%) and abdominal or pelvic discomfort or bloating (5.5%) 1

  • Uncommon

    Visual symptoms such as blurring, spots or flashes (1.5%), more likely with higher total dose or longer use; the label says to stop treatment if they occur, and they can be long-lasting or permanent 1

  • Uncommon

    Multiple pregnancy, in about 8% of pregnancies in trials 1

  • Not known

    Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome, which can progress within a day to several days and become serious 1

  • Not known

    Possible higher risk of borderline or invasive ovarian tumors with prolonged use 1

  • Uncommon

    In men, side effects were reported in under 10% of study participants, including headache and drowsiness; the label notes reports of testicular tumors and breast enlargement in men 321

  • Common

    IGF-1 can fall: in a small study of 20 men treated for low testosterone it fell in 15, and in 2 it dropped well below normal; the authors suggest checking IGF-1 during treatment, and it isn't known whether the drop matters 4

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Pregnancy
  • Liver disease or a history of liver problems
  • Abnormal uterine bleeding of unknown cause
  • Ovarian cysts or enlargement not caused by polycystic ovary syndrome
  • Uncontrolled thyroid or adrenal problems, or a pituitary tumor or other brain lesion

What it can clash with

  • None well documented

  • Men: testosterone, estradiol and hematocrit

For women, the label rules out pregnancy and ovarian cysts before treatment; for off-label use in men there is no label guidance, and trials tracked testosterone, LH, FSH, estradiol and hematocrit; a 2025 study found IGF-1 fell in most men treated for low testosterone, and its authors suggest checking it.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. Meta-analysis2025819 men with hypogonadism in 10 randomized trials

    Clomiphene or enclomiphene citrate for the treatment of male hypogonadism: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

    Clomiphene or enclomiphene raised total testosterone, LH and FSH versus placebo; testosterone levels were similar to testosterone gel, but sperm counts were better preserved. Symptom results were mixed. 2

  2. Meta-analysis20221,642 men with hypogonadism in 19 studies (4 randomized)

    Clomiphene citrate for men with hypogonadism: a systematic review and meta-analysis

    Testosterone, LH, FSH and estradiol rose during treatment and symptom scores improved; side effects were reported in under 10% of participants and none were serious. 3

  3. Label2025Women with ovulatory dysfunction

    Clomiphene Citrate Tablets, USP: Prescribing Information

    About 30% of treated patients became pregnant; multiple pregnancies made up about 8% of pregnancies. 1

  4. Case series202520 men treated for low testosterone (median age 47), most with pituitary disease

    Reduced IGF-1 Levels Following Clomiphene Treatment for Male Hypogonadism

    IGF-1 fell in 15 of 20 men (a median of 0.6 standard deviations), and in 2 it dropped well below the normal range for age and sex, while testosterone rose in all of them. The authors call the clinical importance unknown and suggest checking IGF-1 during treatment. 4

  5. Trial201516 men with acromegaly and low testosterone whose disease wasn't controlled

    Clomiphene citrate added to treatment in men with acromegaly not controlled by standard therapy (Duarte, Jallad and Bronstein)

    Adding clomiphene for 3 months lowered IGF-1 by about 41% on average (424 to 250 ng/mL) and brought it to normal in 7 of 16; testosterone roughly doubled. 5

Questions to ask your doctor

  • For men: would clomiphene protect my fertility better than testosterone therapy?
  • Which vision changes mean I should stop and get checked?
  • How will we watch for ovarian enlargement or hyperstimulation?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

FDA-approved FDA-approved for ovulation induction; off-label in men. Approved to treat ovulatory dysfunction in women who want to become pregnant. The label says there are no adequate or well-controlled studies in male infertility, so use in men is off-label. 1
