Clomiphene

Also called Clomid, Clomiphene citrate

A prescription fertility pill that triggers ovulation in women who don't ovulate regularly; it is also used off-label in men to raise their own testosterone.

What it is

Clomiphene is a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM) taken as a tablet. It raises the pituitary hormones LH and FSH, which in women leads to ovulation and in men stimulates the testes to make testosterone. It is a mix of two forms, enclomiphene and zuclomiphene (30% to 50% of the mix); zuclomiphene stays in the body for more than a month.

Strong evidence

FDA-approved for ovulation induction; in men, a 2025 meta-analysis of 10 randomized trials (819 men) found it raises testosterone, with mixed effects on symptoms.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Induces ovulation in women with ovulatory dysfunction; about 30% of treated patients became pregnant in the label's studies 1

  • Moderate evidence

    Raises total testosterone, LH and FSH in men with low testosterone compared with placebo (10 randomized trials, 819 men; moderate-certainty evidence) 2

  • Limited evidence

    Preserved sperm counts better than testosterone gel at similar testosterone levels: men were much less likely to have a low sperm concentration (risk ratio 0.10) 2

  • Limited evidence

    Improved symptom scores (ADAM questionnaire) in mostly observational studies of men with hypogonadism; results in randomized trials were mixed 32

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Ovarian enlargement (13.6%), hot flashes (10.4%) and abdominal or pelvic discomfort or bloating (5.5%) 1

  • Uncommon

    Visual symptoms such as blurring, spots or flashes (1.5%), more likely with higher total dose or longer use 1

  • Uncommon

    Multiple pregnancy, in about 8% of pregnancies in trials 1

  • Not known

    Ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome, which can progress within a day to several days and become serious 1

  • Not known

    Possible higher risk of borderline or invasive ovarian tumors with prolonged use 1

  • Uncommon

    In men, side effects were reported in under 10% of study participants, including headache and drowsiness; the label notes reports of testicular tumors and breast enlargement in men 321

Talk to a clinician first if you are

  • Pregnancy
  • Liver disease or a history of liver problems
  • Abnormal uterine bleeding of unknown cause
  • Ovarian cysts or enlargement not caused by polycystic ovary syndrome

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Pregnancy test (women)
  • Men: morning total testosterone, LH, FSH and estradiol
  • Semen analysis if fertility is a goal

While taking it

  • Men: testosterone, estradiol and hematocrit
  • Eye exam if vision changes occur

For women, the label rules out pregnancy and ovarian cysts before treatment; for off-label use in men there is no label guidance, and trials tracked testosterone, LH, FSH, estradiol and hematocrit.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis2025819 men with hypogonadism in 10 randomized trials

    Clomiphene or enclomiphene citrate for the treatment of male hypogonadism: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

    Clomiphene or enclomiphene raised total testosterone, LH and FSH versus placebo; testosterone levels were similar to testosterone gel, but sperm counts were better preserved. Symptom results were mixed. 2

  2. Meta-analysis20221,642 men with hypogonadism in 19 studies (4 randomized)

    Clomiphene citrate for men with hypogonadism: a systematic review and meta-analysis

    Testosterone, LH, FSH and estradiol rose during treatment and symptom scores improved; side effects were reported in under 10% of participants and none were serious. 3

  3. Label2025Women with ovulatory dysfunction

    Clomiphene Citrate Tablets, USP: Prescribing Information

    About 30% of treated patients became pregnant; multiple pregnancies made up about 8% of pregnancies. 1

Questions for your clinician

  • For men: would clomiphene protect my fertility better than testosterone therapy?
  • Which vision changes mean I should stop and get checked?
  • How will we watch for ovarian enlargement or hyperstimulation?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for ovulation induction; off-label in men. Approved to treat ovulatory dysfunction in women who want to become pregnant. The label says there are no adequate or well-controlled studies in male infertility, so use in men is off-label. 1
