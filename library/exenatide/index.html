Exenatide.

Also called Byetta, Bydureon

A prescription GLP-1 medicine, first approved as Byetta in 2005, that lowers blood sugar in type 2 diabetes. The brand-name versions were discontinued in the US in 2024.

What it isevidence librarian

Exenatide is a lab-made peptide of 39 amino acids. It was first found in a lizard, Heloderma suspectum (the Gila monster), and its sequence partly overlaps that of human GLP-1. It switches on GLP-1 receptors. That raises insulin release when blood sugar is high, lowers excess glucagon and slows stomach emptying. Byetta is a short-acting form with a half-life of about 2.4 hours. Bydureon BCise is an extended-release form. Both are injected under the skin.

Strong evidence

FDA approved it for blood sugar control in type 2 diabetes, based on randomized trials. A heart-safety trial of 14,752 adults (EXSCEL) found no increase in heart events. A phase 3 trial in 194 people found no benefit in Parkinson's disease.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Strong evidence

    Lower blood sugar in adults with type 2 diabetes: with Byetta, A1C fell about 0.5 to 1.0 points more than with placebo 1

  • Strong evidence

    Approved (as Bydureon BCise) for children and teens 10 and older with type 2 diabetes: in 82 young people aged 10 to 17, A1C fell 0.71 points more than with placebo over 24 weeks 2

  • Strong evidence

    Heart safety: no increase in heart attacks, strokes or cardiovascular deaths in 14,752 adults with type 2 diabetes; a reduction was not shown (hazard ratio 0.91) 2

  • Strong evidence

    Small weight loss in adults with type 2 diabetes: about 0.3 to 2.7 kg more than with placebo (not approved for weight loss) 1

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Common

    Nausea, vomiting and diarrhea: with Byetta, nausea affected 44% versus 18% on placebo 1

  • Common

    Lumps and other reactions at the injection site with Bydureon BCise: nodules in about 1 in 10 users, and rare serious skin infections or tissue death, some needing surgery 2

  • Common

    Antibodies against exenatide, which can weaken its effect on blood sugar: high antibody levels developed in 32% of adults on Bydureon BCise 21

  • Common

    Low blood sugar when combined with a sulfonylurea: 14% to 36% on Byetta versus 3% on placebo 1

  • Rare

    Rare serious reactions: pancreatitis, kidney injury after dehydration, and drug-induced low platelet counts that have caused serious, sometimes fatal bleeding; the labels also warn about gallbladder disease such as gallstones, and about breathing stomach contents into the lungs under general anesthesia or deep sedation 12

  • In theory

    Boxed warning (Bydureon BCise only): thyroid C-cell tumors in rats; whether this happens in humans is unknown 2

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2 (a contraindication for Bydureon BCise)
  • Past serious allergic reaction to exenatide, or low platelets caused by an exenatide product
  • Severe kidney disease or kidney failure (not recommended)
  • Severe digestive disease, including severe gastroparesis (not recommended)
  • Pregnancy (animal studies showed harm to the fetus) or breastfeeding (no human data)

What it can clash with

  • Warfarin: reports of a raised INR, sometimes with bleeding; INR needs closer monitoring when starting or changing exenatide
  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Oral medicines that need to act quickly, including birth control pills: slower stomach emptying can delay absorption; the Byetta label advises on timing
  • Other exenatide products: using two together is not recommended

Blood tests to watchmarkers agent

Before starting

  • HbA1c or fasting glucose
  • Kidney function (creatinine, eGFR)
  • INR if taking warfarin

While taking it

  • HbA1c to track blood sugar (worsening control can signal antibodies)
  • Blood glucose if also taking insulin or a sulfonylurea
  • Kidney function if vomiting or diarrhea is severe
  • INR more often if taking warfarin

The labels call for watching kidney function when digestive side effects could cause dehydration, INR in people on warfarin and blood sugar with insulin or sulfonylureas, and for considering another treatment if blood sugar control worsens, which antibodies can cause; routine calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound checks are of uncertain value.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. RCT2025194 adults aged 25 to 80 with Parkinson's disease

    Exenatide once a week versus placebo as a potential disease-modifying treatment for people with Parkinson's disease in the UK: a phase 3, multicentre, double-blind, parallel-group, randomised, placebo-controlled trial

    After 96 weeks, movement scores off medication worsened by 5.7 points with exenatide and 4.5 with placebo (adjusted difference 0.92, 95% CI -1.56 to 3.39); the authors found no evidence that exenatide slows the disease. Serious adverse events: 9% versus 11%. 5

  2. Label202414,752 adults with type 2 diabetes (EXSCEL trial)

    BYDUREON BCISE (exenatide extended-release) injectable suspension, for subcutaneous use: Prescribing Information

    Heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death: hazard ratio 0.91 (95% CI 0.832 to 1.004); the drug did not raise heart risk, and a reduction was not shown. 2

  3. Label2025Adults with type 2 diabetes in placebo-controlled trials

    BYETTA (exenatide) injection, for subcutaneous use: Prescribing Information

    A1C fell about 0.5 to 1.0 points and weight about 0.3 to 2.7 kg more than with placebo; nausea affected 44% versus 18%. 1

Questions to ask your doctor

  • If exenatide is no longer available to me, what alternatives suit me?
  • Do I need more frequent INR checks if I take warfarin?
  • How would we tell if antibodies are making it work less well for me?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

FDA-approved FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes; brands discontinued. Byetta (adults) and Bydureon BCise (adults and children 10 and older) are approved to improve blood sugar control in type 2 diabetes; AstraZeneca discontinued both in October 2024, according to an insurer notice, and FDA approved a generic version of Byetta in November 2024. Exenatide is not approved for weight loss or Parkinson's disease. 1234
