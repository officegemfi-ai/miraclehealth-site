What it is

Metformin is a biguanide, a type of oral diabetes medicine. It lowers blood sugar by reducing the sugar the liver makes, reducing sugar absorbed from the gut and helping the body respond better to insulin. Taken alone it rarely causes low blood sugar.

Strong evidence

FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes, and a large randomized trial (3,234 adults) showed it delays diabetes in people at high risk; evidence that it slows aging comes mainly from observational comparisons in people with diabetes.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Lowers blood sugar in adults and children 10 and older with type 2 diabetes 1

  • Moderate evidence

    Cut new cases of type 2 diabetes by 31% versus placebo over about 3 years in 3,234 adults with raised blood sugar (intensive lifestyle change cut them by 58%); off-label in the US 2

  • Limited evidence

    Lower death rates in people with diabetes taking metformin than in people with diabetes on other drugs, and slightly lower than in people without diabetes, in a meta-analysis of 53 observational studies, a kind of comparison that can reflect differences between the people compared; these were comparisons in people with diabetes, not trials in healthy people 3

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, gas and stomach upset: diarrhea in 53% in an immediate-release trial, and in 10% versus 3% on placebo with extended-release tablets 1

  • Uncommon

    Low vitamin B12: about 7% developed below-normal levels in 29-week trials; this can cause anemia, and B12 levels are worth checking (any change to metformin is for the prescriber) 1

  • Common

    On average, smaller gains in lean mass and thigh muscle from strength training in healthy adults 65 and older (14-week trial of 94 people) 4

  • Rare

    Boxed warning: lactic acidosis, a rare but serious and sometimes fatal acid buildup in the blood; risk is higher with kidney impairment, age 65 or older, heavy drinking, liver problems, contrast dye, surgery and low-oxygen states 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Severe kidney disease (eGFR below 30)
  • Metabolic acidosis, including diabetic ketoacidosis
  • Liver disease
  • Heavy alcohol use
  • Past allergic reaction to metformin

Interactions

  • Topiramate, zonisamide, acetazolamide and other carbonic anhydrase inhibitors: higher risk of lactic acidosis
  • Ranolazine, vandetanib, dolutegravir and cimetidine: can raise metformin levels
  • Alcohol: adds to metformin's effect on lactate; heavy drinking should be avoided
  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Kidney function (eGFR)
  • HbA1c or fasting glucose
  • Complete blood count

While taking it

  • Kidney function (eGFR) at least once a year, more often in older adults
  • Complete blood count once a year
  • Vitamin B12 every 2 to 3 years
  • HbA1c to track blood sugar

The label calls for checking kidney function before starting and at least yearly, because metformin builds up when the kidneys are weak, plus yearly blood counts and vitamin B12 every 2 to 3 years.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis201753 studies comparing people with diabetes taking metformin with other groups

    Metformin reduces all-cause mortality and diseases of ageing independent of its effect on diabetes control: A systematic review and meta-analysis

    People with diabetes taking metformin had lower all-cause death rates than people without diabetes (hazard ratio 0.93) and than people with diabetes on other drugs (0.72). The authors noted baseline differences between groups that could bias the results. 3

  2. RCT20023,234 adults with raised blood sugar but no diabetes (average age 51)

    Reduction in the Incidence of Type 2 Diabetes with Lifestyle Intervention or Metformin

    Over an average of 2.8 years, diabetes developed at 7.8 cases per 100 person-years with metformin versus 11.0 with placebo, a 31% reduction; intensive lifestyle change reduced it by 58%. 2

  3. RCT201994 healthy adults aged 65 and older

    Metformin blunts muscle hypertrophy in response to progressive resistance exercise training in older adults: A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter trial: The MASTERS trial

    During 14 weeks of supervised strength training, the metformin group gained less lean mass and thigh muscle than the placebo group; the strength difference was not statistically significant. 4

Questions for your clinician

  • Is there a reason for me to take metformin if I don't have diabetes or prediabetes?
  • Should I pause it before a scan with contrast dye or before surgery?
  • If I do strength training, could metformin reduce my gains?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes; aging use off-label. Prescription-only and approved to improve blood sugar control in adults and children 10 and older with type 2 diabetes. Using it to prevent diabetes or to slow aging is off-label. 1
