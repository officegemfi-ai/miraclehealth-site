What it is

CJC-1295 with DAC is a synthetic, 29-amino-acid analog of growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH), the brain signal that tells the pituitary gland to release growth hormone. It carries an extra chemical unit at one end (MPA-lysine, the 'DAC'), part of a design meant to latch onto albumin, a blood protein, so its half-life stretches to about 6 to 8 days instead of minutes. It reached early human studies but is not an approved medicine.

Limited evidence

Two small 2006 studies in healthy adults showed higher growth hormone and IGF-1 for days after injection; FDA found no data on effectiveness for any health outcome.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Raised growth hormone and IGF-1 above baseline for days after a single injection in healthy adults 56

  • Limited evidence

    Raised baseline growth hormone between pulses (not peak levels) and lifted IGF-1 about 40% a week after one dose in healthy adults 6

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Side effects in 94% of volunteers after one injection in the first human study, mostly injection-site reactions (about 70%) 5

  • Not known

    Flushing and body-wide vasodilatory reactions, headache, nausea, dizziness and low blood pressure after repeat injections; increased heart rate in a second study 25

  • Rare

    One death from a heart attack was reported in a halted, unpublished HIV trial; the treating physician blamed existing heart disease, and causality was not established 5

  • Not known

    Animal studies found lower hemoglobin, higher cholesterol, injection-site tissue damage and DNA damage in pituitary cells; no cancer studies exist 5

  • Not known

    Possible immune reactions from impurities; FDA judged all CJC-1295 forms 'not well characterized' for compounding 25

  • In theory

    Long-term safety unknown; FDA says the known risks of raised growth hormone and IGF-1 should be assumed, such as the fluid retention and higher blood sugar on the label of tesamorelin, which works the same way 57

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Active cancer or a history of pituitary tumors
  • Heart disease or heart rhythm problems
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no full reproductive studies)
  • Diabetes or prediabetes (clinician first)
  • Competitive athletes (prohibited by WADA at all times)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Not an approved medicine. These are checks a clinician or a clinical trial would use, not a plan for taking it on your own.

Before starting

  • General health check with a clinician
  • IGF-1
  • Fasting glucose or HbA1c

While taking it

  • IGF-1 (one dose group in a study rose above the normal range)
  • Fasting glucose or HbA1c

No evidence-based monitoring exists for CJC-1295; FDA says growth hormone and IGF-1 risks should be assumed, and the label of tesamorelin (same mechanism) monitors IGF-1 and blood sugar.

Key studies

  1. Review2024FDA review of two small studies in healthy adults (one with 12 men) plus rat and dog studies

    Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee meeting transcript, December 4, 2024 (morning session: CJC-1295)

    Growth hormone and IGF-1 rose for days after injection and side effects were frequent, mostly injection-site and vasodilatory reactions. FDA found no information on effectiveness for growth hormone deficiency. 5

  2. Review2012Review of published research, including CJC-1295 studies in healthy adults

    Growth Hormone Doping in Sports: A Critical Review of Use and Detection Strategies

    Reports a half-life of 6 to 8 days, higher baseline growth hormone and about 40% higher IGF-1 a week after one dose; calls the effect moderate and notes long-acting GHRH analogs have reached the black market. 6

Questions for your clinician

  • Is an approved option better for my goal?
  • Given my heart health, blood pressure and blood sugar, is a long-acting growth hormone booster safe for me?
  • If I compete in a tested sport, would this cause an anti-doping violation?

Status in the US

Not approved Not approved; FDA advisers voted against compounding. In December 2024 FDA's compounding advisory committee voted 13-0 against adding CJC-1295 DAC to the list of substances pharmacies may compound. FDA has moved it off its Category 2 'significant safety risks' list only because the nomination was withdrawn, and it is not in Category 1, so there is no clear legal compounding route; WADA bans it in sport. 1234
