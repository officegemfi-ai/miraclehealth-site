What it isevidence librarian

Oxytocin is a hormone that makes the muscle of the uterus contract. It also has an antidiuretic effect, which makes the body hold on to water. Pitocin is synthetic oxytocin given in hospitals by IV infusion or muscle injection, to start or strengthen labor and to control bleeding after birth. Nasal oxytocin has been tested for anxiety, depression and autism, but trials have not shown clear benefits.

Limited evidence

It is FDA-approved for labor and delivery. For mood, anxiety and social goals, trials of nasal oxytocin have been small or negative. One of them, a 290-child autism trial, found no benefit.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Strong evidence

    Starts or strengthens labor contractions when there is a medical reason (approved use) 1

  • Strong evidence

    Helps control bleeding after delivery (approved use) 1

  • Limited evidence

    No clear effect on core anxiety or depression symptoms across 15 small trials; one small imaging study found reduced activity in the amygdala, a fear-processing brain area 2

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Not known

    Water intoxication from its antidiuretic effect, which in severe cases has caused seizures and coma 1

  • Not known

    Overstimulation of the uterus, uterine rupture, and slow or irregular heartbeat or other harm in the baby 1

  • Not known

    Dangerous spikes in blood pressure, bleeding in the brain, irregular heartbeat, nausea and vomiting; maternal deaths have been reported with drugs of this type 1

  • Not known

    Severe allergic (anaphylactic) reactions 1

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Pregnancy outside supervised obstetric care (it causes uterine contractions)
  • In labor: a major mismatch between the baby's head and the pelvis, fetal distress when delivery is not imminent, or an already overactive uterus
  • Allergy to oxytocin

What it can clash with

  • Vasoconstrictor drugs given with caudal block anesthesia: severe high blood pressure has been reported
  • Cyclopropane anesthesia: can change its heart effects, causing low blood pressure and abnormal heart rhythms

Blood tests to watchmarkers agent

Before starting

None routinely needed.

While taking it

  • Contractions and the baby's heart rate (in hospital)
  • Fluid balance and blood sodium during long infusions

During labor, staff watch contractions, the baby's heart rate and fluid status because of overstimulation and water-intoxication risks; no routine blood tests have been established for nasal use.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. RCT2021290 children and teens aged 3 to 17 with autism (24 weeks)

    Intranasal oxytocin in children and adolescents with autism spectrum disorder

    Nasal oxytocin did not improve social withdrawal scores or other social and cognitive measures more than placebo; side effects were similar in both groups. 3

  2. Systematic review201915 randomized trials in social anxiety, phobia, major depression and postnatal depression

    The role of intranasal oxytocin in anxiety and depressive disorders: A systematic review of randomized controlled trials

    Mostly single-dose nasal oxytocin had no significant effect on core symptoms; a few imaging studies showed changes in amygdala activity, and the evidence was judged inconclusive. 2

  3. Label2026Patients in labor and after delivery

    Pitocin (oxytocin injection, USP) prescribing information

    Approved for medically indicated labor induction or augmentation and for bleeding after delivery; warns of water intoxication, uterine overstimulation and rupture, and states it is not for elective induction. 1

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Is there evidence that oxytocin would help my mood or relationship concerns, compared with standard treatments?
  • Could oxytocin affect a pregnancy, or any heart or blood pressure condition I have?
  • Is there an approved oxytocin product for what I'm hoping it will do?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

FDA-approved FDA-approved injection for labor and delivery. Pitocin is approved for medically indicated induction or strengthening of labor and to control bleeding after delivery, given in a hospital with medical supervision; a boxed notice says it is not for elective induction. Mood, anxiety and social uses, including nasal sprays, are not among its approved uses. 1
