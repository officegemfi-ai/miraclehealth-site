Omega-3 fish oil

Also called Fish oil, Omega-3, EPA, DHA, Krill oil, Algae oil

Oils from fish, krill or algae that supply the omega-3 fats EPA and DHA, taken mainly for heart health and to lower triglycerides.

What it is

Omega-3s are polyunsaturated fats. The plant form, ALA, comes from flaxseed, soybean and canola oils, chia seeds and walnuts, while the long-chain forms EPA and DHA come mainly from fish and are first made by microalgae. Supplements include fish oil, krill oil, cod liver oil and algal oil, and separate prescription products such as icosapent ethyl (Vascepa, a purified form of EPA) are FDA-approved drugs.

Strong evidence

Large randomized trials show omega-3s lower triglycerides and that a prescription EPA drug cut heart events, but trials of standard fish oil gave mixed heart results and found no benefit for dry eye, memory in healthy older adults or macular degeneration.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Lowers blood triglycerides by about 15%, according to a 2020 Cochrane review 1

  • Strong evidence

    Prescription EPA (icosapent ethyl) cut major heart events by 25% in REDUCE-IT, a trial of 8,179 adults with high triglycerides taking statins 12

  • Moderate evidence

    Mixed heart results for lower-dose EPA plus DHA: no drop in major heart events in VITAL (25,871 adults) or ASCEND (15,480 adults with diabetes), but 28% fewer heart attacks in VITAL and 19% fewer cardiovascular deaths in ASCEND 1

  • Moderate evidence

    In rheumatoid arthritis, may reduce the need for anti-inflammatory drugs, though effects on joint symptoms are inconsistent; any change to prescribed drugs is for the treating clinician 1

  • Moderate evidence

    Taken in pregnancy, lowers the risk of preterm birth 1

Risks and side effects

  • Uncommon

    Atrial fibrillation: 25% higher risk across 7 trials of 81,210 people, and higher still in trials using higher doses; with prescription EPA, 3% versus 2% on placebo were hospitalized for it 32

  • Uncommon

    Bleeding, especially with aspirin, clopidogrel or warfarin; with prescription EPA, 12% had bleeding events versus 10% on placebo 21

  • Common

    Commonly reported mild effects: unpleasant taste, bad breath, heartburn, nausea, stomach discomfort, diarrhea, headache and smelly sweat 1

  • Not known

    Unknown whether people with fish or shellfish allergy are at higher risk of allergic reactions 2

  • Not known

    In one study (SELECT), men with the highest blood omega-3 levels had more high-grade prostate cancer; the finding is debated and overall evidence shows no consistent link 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Taking blood thinners or antiplatelet drugs such as warfarin, aspirin or clopidogrel (clinician first)
  • History of atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter (clinician first; don't stop a prescribed omega-3 on your own)
  • Fish or shellfish allergy (clinician first)

Interactions

  • Warfarin and other anticoagulants: fish oil might prolong clotting time, and NIH advises periodic INR checks
  • Aspirin, clopidogrel and other antithrombotic drugs: bleeding risk was higher with prescription EPA

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

None routinely needed.

While taking it

  • INR at intervals for people taking warfarin

No routine tests are needed for supplement use; NIH advises periodic INR checks for people on warfarin.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis202181,210 people in 7 randomized cardiovascular outcome trials

    Effect of Long-Term Marine Omega-3 Fatty Acids Supplementation on the Risk of Atrial Fibrillation in Randomized Controlled Trials of Cardiovascular Outcomes: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

    Omega-3 supplements raised the risk of atrial fibrillation by 25% over about 5 years, with a larger increase in trials that used higher doses. 3

  2. Label2026Adults in the REDUCE-IT outcome trial and in trials of very high triglycerides

    VASCEPA (icosapent ethyl) capsules prescribing information

    Approved as an add-on to statins to cut heart attack, stroke and related events in high-risk adults with high triglycerides, and to lower very high triglycerides; atrial fibrillation hospitalization (3% vs 2%) and bleeding (12% vs 10%) were more common than with placebo. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • Would a prescription omega-3 suit my triglycerides or heart risk better than a supplement?
  • With my heart rhythm history and any blood thinners I take, is omega-3 safe for me?
  • Should I get omega-3s from eating fish instead of a supplement?

Status in the US

Dietary supplement Supplement; prescription forms FDA-approved. Fish, krill and algal oils are sold as dietary supplements. Prescription icosapent ethyl (Vascepa) is FDA-approved to lower very high triglycerides and, added to a statin, to cut heart attack, stroke and related risks in adults with high triglycerides and heart disease, or diabetes plus other risk factors. 12
