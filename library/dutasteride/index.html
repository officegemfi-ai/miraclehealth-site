Dutasteride

Also called Avodart

A prescription capsule that blocks DHT production, approved in the US for an enlarged prostate; it is used off-label for male pattern hair loss.

What it is

Dutasteride is a 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor that blocks both the type 1 and type 2 enzymes that convert testosterone into DHT, cutting blood DHT by about 85% to 95%. It lasts a long time in the body: its half-life is about 5 weeks, and it can be detected for 4 to 6 months after stopping. It lowers PSA by about half within 3 to 6 months.

Strong evidence

FDA-approved for a symptomatic enlarged prostate; for hair loss (off-label in the US), a meta-analysis of 3 short randomized trials in 576 men found more hair growth than with finasteride.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Improves symptoms of an enlarged prostate and lowers the risk of acute urinary retention and BPH surgery 12

  • Moderate evidence

    Increased hair counts more than finasteride, with better photo ratings, in men with male pattern hair loss (3 randomized trials, 576 men, 24 weeks; off-label) 3

Risks and side effects

  • Uncommon

    Sexual side effects in the first 6 months: impotence (4.7% versus 1.7% on placebo), lower libido (3.0% versus 1.4%) and ejaculation problems (1.4% versus 0.5%); rates generally fell after the first year, some may persist after stopping, and guidelines say men should be told about them before starting 12

  • Not known

    Breast enlargement or tenderness; reports after approval of male breast cancer, depressed mood, and testicular pain or swelling 1

  • Uncommon

    More high-grade prostate cancer: Gleason 8 to 10 cancer in 1.0% versus 0.5% on placebo in the REDUCE trial 1

  • Not known

    Lower semen volume (26%), total sperm count (23%) and sperm motility (18%) after a year; total sperm count had not recovered after 24 weeks of follow-up, and the effect on fertility is unknown 1

  • Not known

    Possible suicidal thoughts: in 2025 EU regulators found insufficient evidence of a causal link but added the potential risk to labeling as a precaution, given a possible class effect 4

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Women who are or may be pregnant: must not take it or handle leaking capsules, since it is absorbed through the skin and can harm a male fetus
  • Women and children (not approved for them)
  • Past serious allergic reaction to dutasteride or another 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor
  • Blood donors: no donating until at least 6 months after the last dose

Interactions

  • Strong, long-term CYP3A4 inhibitors such as ritonavir or ketoconazole: can raise dutasteride levels

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • PSA (baseline)

While taking it

  • PSA: a new baseline after at least 3 months; any confirmed rise from the lowest value should be evaluated

Dutasteride lowers PSA by about half, so the label calls for a new PSA baseline after at least 3 months of treatment and evaluation of any confirmed rise; no other routine labs are required.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis2019576 men with male pattern hair loss in 3 randomized trials (24 weeks)

    The efficacy and safety of dutasteride compared with finasteride in treating men with androgenetic alopecia: a systematic review and meta-analysis

    Dutasteride increased hair counts and photo ratings more than finasteride, with similar rates of sexual side effects; the authors noted the trials were few and short. 3

  2. Label2023Men with an enlarged prostate, and men in the REDUCE prostate cancer prevention trial

    AVODART (dutasteride) capsules: Prescribing Information

    Sexual side effects were more common than with placebo early on and declined over time; in REDUCE, high-grade prostate cancer was more common (1.0% versus 0.5%). 1

  3. Guideline2021Men with urinary symptoms from an enlarged prostate

    Management of Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms Attributed to Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia: AUA Guideline

    Recommends 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors for men with an enlarged prostate, and says clinicians should tell patients about sexual side effects before starting. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • Given how long dutasteride stays in the body, what should I know about side effects, PSA tests and blood donation?
  • Which sexual or mood changes should I report?
  • If my goal is hair loss, how does off-label dutasteride compare with approved options?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for enlarged prostate; off-label for hair. Approved for symptomatic benign prostatic hyperplasia in men with an enlarged prostate, alone or with tamsulosin; the label says it is not approved to prevent prostate cancer. Use for hair loss is off-label in the US. 1
