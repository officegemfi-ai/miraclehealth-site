What it isevidence librarian

Semaglutide is a lab-made, long-lasting version of GLP-1, a natural hormone that helps regulate appetite and food intake. It shares 94% of its sequence with human GLP-1. It switches on GLP-1 receptors. That lowers calorie intake and slows stomach emptying. When blood sugar is high, it also raises insulin and lowers glucagon. It binds to the blood protein albumin, which makes it long-acting, with a half-life of about a week. It comes as an injection and as a tablet.

Strong evidence

It has been tested in large randomized trials such as STEP 1 (1,961 adults) and SELECT (17,604 adults). FDA has approved it for each of its main uses.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Strong evidence

    About 15% average body-weight loss over 68 weeks in adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes, versus about 2% with placebo 72

  • Strong evidence

    Lower blood sugar in adults with type 2 diabetes 13

  • Strong evidence

    20% fewer heart attacks, strokes and cardiovascular deaths over about 40 months in adults with heart disease and overweight or obesity but no diabetes 82

  • Strong evidence

    Lower risk of kidney function decline, kidney failure and cardiovascular death in adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease 1

  • Strong evidence

    Approved under FDA accelerated approval to treat noncirrhotic MASH (fatty-liver inflammation) with moderate to advanced liver scarring in adults; continued approval depends on a confirmatory trial 2

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Common

    Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation and stomach pain; in STEP 1, 74% had digestive side effects versus 48% on placebo 27

  • Common

    Most lost weight tends to come back after stopping: about two-thirds was regained within a year in STEP 1 9

  • Uncommon

    Gallstones and gallbladder inflammation (gallstones in 1.6% versus 0.7% on placebo) 2

  • Rare

    Acute pancreatitis 2

  • Rare

    Sudden vision loss from optic nerve damage (NAION), judged a very rare side effect by the European Medicines Agency 10

  • In theory

    Boxed warning: thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents; whether this happens in humans is unknown 21

  • Rare

    Breathing stomach contents into the lungs (pulmonary aspiration) under general anesthesia or deep sedation: rare reports with GLP-1 drugs; the label asks people to tell their doctors before any planned surgery or procedure 2

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • Past serious allergic reaction to semaglutide
  • Pregnancy, or planning a pregnancy within about 2 months
  • Severe gastroparesis (very slow stomach emptying)
  • History of diabetic retinopathy (needs closer eye monitoring)

What it can clash with

  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Oral medicines with a narrow safety margin, such as levothyroxine: slower stomach emptying can change how much is absorbed
  • Other semaglutide products or other GLP-1 drugs: using them together is not recommended

Blood tests to watchmarkers agent

Before starting

  • HbA1c or fasting glucose
  • Kidney function (creatinine, eGFR)
  • Resting heart rate
  • Eye exam if you have diabetic retinopathy

While taking it

  • Blood glucose if also taking insulin or a sulfonylurea
  • Kidney function if vomiting or diarrhea is severe
  • Resting heart rate at regular visits
  • Eye checks if you have diabetic retinopathy

The labels call for watching kidney function when vomiting or diarrhea could cause dehydration, blood sugar with insulin or sulfonylureas, heart rate and diabetic eye disease; they say routine calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound checks are of uncertain value.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. RCT20211,961 adults with obesity or overweight, without diabetes

    Once-Weekly Semaglutide in Adults with Overweight or Obesity

    Average weight change at 68 weeks was -14.9% with semaglutide versus -2.4% with placebo. Digestive side effects were more common (74% versus 48%). 7

  2. RCT202317,604 adults aged 45 or older with heart disease and overweight or obesity, without diabetes

    Semaglutide and Cardiovascular Outcomes in Obesity without Diabetes

    Heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death occurred in 6.5% versus 8.0% with placebo over about 40 months (hazard ratio 0.80). More people stopped because of side effects (16.6% versus 8.2%). 8

  3. Trial2022327 STEP 1 participants followed for a year after treatment stopped

    Weight regain and cardiometabolic effects after withdrawal of semaglutide: The STEP 1 trial extension

    People who had taken semaglutide regained about two-thirds of the weight they had lost, and most cardiometabolic improvements drifted back toward baseline. 9

Questions to ask your doctor

  • What is our plan if I stop, given that most of the weight tends to return?
  • Should I tell my surgeon or anesthesia team before any procedure with sedation?
  • Which symptoms, such as severe stomach pain or sudden vision changes, mean I should call you right away?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

FDA-approved FDA-approved for diabetes, weight, heart, kidney, MASH. Approved as Ozempic (type 2 diabetes, plus heart and kidney risk), Rybelsus (type 2 diabetes, plus heart risk) and Wegovy injection and tablets (weight and heart risk; the injection also for MASH). The shortage ended in February 2025, so pharmacies may no longer routinely copy it; in May 2026 FDA proposed keeping it off the outsourcing-facility (503B) bulks list, and it warns against compounded and 'research-use' versions. 123456
