What it is

Survodutide (BI 456906) is a lab-made drug that switches on the receptors for two hormones, glucagon and GLP-1. Boehringer Ingelheim licensed it from Zealand Pharma and is testing it for obesity, type 2 diabetes and MASH (fatty-liver inflammation) with liver scarring. It is investigational and not approved.

Moderate evidence

Two phase 3 randomized trials (725 and 755 adults) reported by Boehringer Ingelheim, which says both were published in NEJM, plus a published phase 2 trial of 387 adults; results on heart outcomes and long-term safety are not yet available.

What it may do

  • Moderate evidence

    Average weight loss of up to 16.6% over 76 weeks versus 3.2% with placebo, counted as if everyone stayed on treatment, in 725 adults with obesity or overweight without type 2 diabetes (company-reported) 1

  • Moderate evidence

    In 755 adults with obesity or overweight and type 2 diabetes: up to 13.1% weight loss versus 3.1% with placebo (counted as if everyone stayed on treatment), and A1C down by up to 1.21 points versus 0.03, over 76 weeks (company-reported) 2

  • Moderate evidence

    Less liver fat: in a 48-week trial of adults with fatty liver disease and excess weight, 61.0% reached normal liver fat versus 5.7% with placebo (company-reported) 1

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea and constipation, mostly while the dose was being raised: 18% to 19% stopped because of digestive side effects versus 1.2% to 2.9% with placebo 12

  • Common

    In the phase 2 trial, 91% had a side effect versus 75% with placebo, and 75% had digestive side effects versus 42% 4

  • Not known

    Long-term safety and effects on heart events are not yet known; a heart-outcomes trial had not reported in the sources checked 2

  • Not known

    Survodutide sold online as a 'research' product is unapproved, and FDA has warned sellers 3

  • In theory

    Approved GLP-1 drugs carry a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents; whether this applies to survodutide was not reported in the sources checked 5

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Not for use outside a clinical trial: it is not an approved medicine, and "research" versions sold online are unapproved and unregulated
  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2 (approved GLP-1 drugs carry this contraindication)
  • Pregnant, breastfeeding or planning pregnancy

Interactions

  • Not well studied. Approved GLP-1 drugs raise the risk of low blood sugar with insulin or sulfonylureas and slow stomach emptying, which can change how oral medicines are absorbed

Blood markers to watch

Not an approved medicine. These are checks a clinician or a clinical trial would use, not a plan for taking it on your own.

Before starting

  • General health check: weight, blood pressure, HbA1c or fasting glucose, kidney and liver tests

While taking it

  • Same general checks at regular visits
  • Resting heart rate

No evidence-based monitoring exists outside clinical trials because it is not approved; these are general health checks, with heart rate added because the label of an approved GLP-1 drug (semaglutide) calls for it.

Key studies

  1. RCT2024387 adults with overweight or obesity, without diabetes

    Glucagon and GLP-1 receptor dual agonist survodutide for obesity: a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-finding phase 2 trial

    Over 46 weeks, weight fell 6.2% to 14.9% across doses versus 2.8% with placebo; side effects affected 91% versus 75%, mostly digestive. 4

  2. RCT2026725 adults with obesity or overweight, without type 2 diabetes (SYNCHRONIZE-1)

    Positive data from two Phase III SYNCHRONIZE obesity trials

    Up to 16.6% average weight loss at 76 weeks versus 3.2% with placebo, counted as if everyone stayed on treatment; 19% stopped because of digestive side effects versus 2.9%. Company-reported. 1

  3. RCT2026755 adults with obesity or overweight and type 2 diabetes (SYNCHRONIZE-2)

    Boehringer Ingelheim's survodutide achieves up to 13.1% weight loss in new Phase III trial, alongside significant improvements in glycemic control in people with obesity and type 2 diabetes

    Up to 13.1% weight loss versus 3.1% (counted as if everyone stayed on treatment) and A1C reductions of up to 1.21 versus 0.03 points at 76 weeks; 18% stopped because of digestive side effects versus 1.2%. Company-reported. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • How do approved options, such as semaglutide or tirzepatide, compare for me?

Status in the US

In clinical trials Investigational; not approved. Survodutide is in phase 3 trials and holds FDA Fast Track (2021) and Breakthrough Therapy (2024) designations for MASH; no FDA filing was announced in the sources checked. FDA has warned companies selling survodutide falsely labeled 'for research purposes' or 'not for human consumption.' 123
