What it is

Gonadorelin is a synthetic version of gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH), a 10-amino-acid hormone from the hypothalamus that tells the pituitary gland to release LH and, to a lesser degree, FSH, which drive the testes and ovaries. The body releases GnRH in pulses, and the pulse pattern is what keeps the system working; gonadorelin lasts only minutes in the blood. In medicine it has been used to test pituitary function and, given in pulses by a pump, to start ovulation or sperm production in people who make too little GnRH.

Limited evidence

Pump-delivered pulses restore sperm production in men with congenital GnRH deficiency about as well as gonadotropin injections, in mostly observational studies; we found no studies of the way most men use it, alongside testosterone therapy.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Sperm production in men with congenital GnRH deficiency when given in pulses by a pump, about as often as with gonadotropin injections and about 5 months sooner, with larger testes, in mostly observational studies of 420 men 6

  • Limited evidence

    Sperm induced in 82% of 56 men with congenital GnRH deficiency treated with pulsed GnRH, versus 76% of 99 treated with hCG and hMG, in a single-center study 7

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    Allergic reactions, including hives and skin hardening at the injection site with repeated use; trouble breathing, ongoing flushing or a racing heart needs urgent care 16

  • Not known

    Brief flushing, headache, lightheadedness, nausea or stomach discomfort 1

  • In theory

    It acts only briefly and the body's response depends on pulse timing, so it is unclear whether occasional injections reproduce the effects seen with pump therapy 2

  • Rare

    In people with a rare pituitary tumor (described in drug information as a gonadotropin-releasing hormone adenoma), use may cause pituitary problems and sudden blindness 1

  • In theory

    Repeated use may raise sex hormones and worsen hormone-dependent tumors 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Hormone-dependent cancer, such as prostate or breast cancer (it may worsen)
  • Known or suspected pituitary tumor
  • Breastfeeding (not adequately studied)
  • Known allergy to gonadorelin
  • Male athletes subject to anti-doping rules (banned by WADA)

Interactions

  • Dopamine-blocking medicines such as haloperidol, chlorpromazine and risperidone are listed as interacting

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • LH and FSH
  • Total testosterone (men) or estradiol (women)
  • Semen analysis if fertility is the goal

While taking it

  • LH, FSH and testosterone or estradiol
  • Semen analysis when treating infertility

No evidence-based monitoring exists for off-label use; in fertility treatment, studies track these hormones and sperm production to judge response.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis2021420 men with congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism in 7 comparative studies

    Spermatogenesis of Male Patients with Congenital Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism Receiving Pulsatile Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone Therapy Versus Gonadotropin Therapy: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

    Pulsed GnRH and gonadotropin therapy produced sperm in similar shares of men, but GnRH did so about 5 months sooner and with larger testes. Allergic reactions occurred mostly with GnRH; breast growth and acne were more common with gonadotropins. The authors call for randomized trials. 6

  2. Cohort2025155 men with congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (56 on pulsed GnRH, 99 on hCG/hMG)

    Comparison of outcomes between pulsatile gonadotropin releasing hormone and combined gonadotropin therapy of spermatogenesis in patients with congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism

    Sperm induction succeeded in 82.1% with pulsed GnRH and 75.8% with hCG/hMG, not a significant difference; GnRH worked about 2 months faster and produced larger testes, and its users reported no significant side effects. 7

Questions for your clinician

  • Why is my testosterone or fertility low, and which approved fertility treatments fit my situation?
  • If I am on testosterone, is there any evidence that gonadorelin injections keep my testes working?
  • Which hormone tests and semen analyses should we track, and how often?

Status in the US

Compounded by prescription No US product; compounding appears to be allowed. Its US brand, Factrel, has been discontinued, but FDA identifies gonadorelin acetate as a component of an FDA-approved drug, and federal rules appear to let licensed pharmacies compound with such ingredients on a prescription (an inference from FDA's listing, not an FDA statement about gonadorelin); for outsourcing facilities it is in Category 1 (under evaluation). WADA bans it for male athletes. 1345
