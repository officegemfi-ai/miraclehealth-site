What it is

KPV is a tiny peptide made of three amino acids (lysine, proline and valine) that matches the tail end of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (alpha-MSH). In cell and rodent studies it dampened inflammatory signaling, possibly by blocking a pathway called NF-kB, but FDA says its molecular targets are still unknown. It has never been studied in humans, and in lab tests it crossed intact human skin poorly unless the skin barrier was broken.

Animal and lab studies

FDA's 2026 review found only cell and rodent studies and no human data of any kind.

What it may do

  • Animal and lab studies

    Reduced inflammatory signaling in human gut-lining cells and immune cells in lab studies 1

  • Animal and lab studies

    Anti-inflammatory and wound-healing effects in rodent models, including corneal wounds 1

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    No human safety data for any route, and no animal toxicity studies were found 12

  • In theory

    Peptides can clump and trigger immune reactions (immunogenicity); FDA found too little data to rule this out 1

  • Not known

    Available material is poorly characterized, with impurities and microbial testing not documented 1

Talk to a clinician first if you are

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Using it in place of proven treatment for a diagnosed condition such as inflammatory bowel disease, psoriasis, eczema or a chronic wound

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • General health check (blood count and metabolic panel)

While taking it

None routinely needed.

No evidence-based monitoring exists because KPV has never been studied in people.

Questions for your clinician

  • Should my inflammation be measured (for example CRP or fecal calprotectin) before I consider anything experimental?
  • What are the risks of using an unregulated product that has never been tested in people?

Status in the US

Not approved Not approved; FDA compounding decision pending. FDA took KPV off its Category 2 safety-risk list in April 2026 after the nomination was withdrawn. FDA staff advised against allowing it in compounding, but its advisory committee voted 8 to 6 (1 abstention) in favor on July 23, 2026; until FDA finishes rulemaking it is not on the list pharmacies may compound from, and an interim access policy has been under White House review since October 14, 2026. 123456
