What it is

Afamelanotide, also known as Melanotan I, is a lab-made peptide of 13 amino acids modeled on alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone (alpha-MSH). It activates the melanocortin 1 receptor in the skin, which raises production of the dark pigment eumelanin regardless of sun or UV exposure. As the prescription implant Scenesse, it helps adults with erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP), a rare disorder that causes painful reactions to light. Unlicensed products sold as Melanotan I for tanning are untested and unregulated.

Strong evidence

Strong only for its FDA-approved use (preventing light-triggered pain in adults with EPP), from two randomized placebo-controlled trials in 168 patients. It is not approved for cosmetic tanning, and unlicensed 'Melanotan I' tanning products have only small early trials and case reports.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    More pain-free time in direct sunlight for people with EPP: a median of 69.4 versus 40.8 hours over 6 months in a US trial of 94 patients, and 6.0 versus 0.8 hours over 9 months in a European trial of 74 5

  • Moderate evidence

    Fewer painful light reactions than placebo (77 versus 146) in the European trial 5

  • Moderate evidence

    Better quality of life than placebo in both trials 5

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Implant-site reactions (21% versus 10% on placebo), most often discoloration, and nausea (19% versus 14%) 1

  • Uncommon

    Darker skin and darkening of existing moles and freckles; new moles were reported in 4% versus 2% on placebo, and the label advises full-body skin exams twice a year 1

  • Uncommon

    Sore throat, cough, fatigue and dizziness, each in 4% to 7%, slightly more often than with placebo 1

  • Not known

    With unlicensed Melanotan I and II used for tanning: flushing, nausea and tiredness in small trials, plus case reports of enlarging or darkening moles, new atypical moles and melanoma (cause not proven) 46

  • Not known

    Unlicensed products have not been tested for quality, and regulators have raised concerns about contamination and infections from shared needles 4

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no human data)
  • Under 18 (safety and effectiveness not established)
  • History of melanoma or many unusual moles, without a dermatologist's advice

Interactions

  • None well documented (no drug interaction studies have been done)

While taking it

  • Full-body skin exam twice a year

The Scenesse label recommends twice-yearly full-body skin exams to watch existing and new pigmented spots; no blood tests are required.

Key studies

  1. RCT2015168 patients with EPP in two trials (94 in the US, 74 in Europe)

    Afamelanotide for Erythropoietic Protoporphyria

    Afamelanotide implants increased pain-free time in direct sunlight and improved quality of life versus placebo; side effects were mostly mild and serious events were not judged drug-related. 5

  2. Label2019244 patients with EPP in placebo-controlled trials (125 on afamelanotide)

    SCENESSE (afamelanotide) implant prescribing information

    Implant-site reactions, nausea, sore throat, cough, fatigue, skin darkening and new moles were more common than with placebo; twice-yearly full-body skin exams are recommended. 1

  3. Review2017Case reports of unregulated melanotan users

    Risks of unregulated use of alpha-melanocyte-stimulating hormone analogues: A review

    Unregulated melanotan I and II use was linked in case reports to mole changes, new dysplastic moles and four melanomas; the authors note that cause is not proven and contrast this with the tested, approved afamelanotide. 6

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved implant for a rare light disorder (EPP). Scenesse was approved in October 2019 to increase pain-free light exposure in adults with EPP and must be implanted by a trained health care professional. It is not approved for tanning; products sold as Melanotan I for that purpose are unlicensed and untested, and afamelanotide is not on FDA's list of substances nominated for pharmacy compounding. 1234
