What it is

NMN (nicotinamide mononucleotide) is a form of vitamin B3 that cells can turn into NAD+, a coenzyme used in the chemical reactions that release energy from food. NAD+ levels fall with age in mammals, and in mice NMN improved glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity, which led to its promotion as an anti-aging supplement. Some NMN taken by mouth is converted to nicotinamide before it reaches the bloodstream, and how it enters cells is still debated.

Limited evidence

Eight small randomized trials in 342 mostly healthy adults, lasting up to 12 weeks, found NMN raised blood NAD+ in most trials but did not improve blood sugar, cholesterol, BMI or blood pressure.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Raised blood NAD+ (a lab marker, not a health effect) in 5 of 8 small randomized trials in adults; whether it also raises NAD+ inside cells and tissues is unclear 34

  • Limited evidence

    No benefit shown for fasting blood sugar, insulin, HbA1c, blood fats, BMI or blood pressure versus placebo across 8 trials of 14 days to 12 weeks in 342 adults 3

  • Limited evidence

    Better muscle insulin sensitivity in one small trial of women with prediabetes 3

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    Long-term safety is uncertain, especially at higher amounts and in older adults; the longest trials lasted 12 weeks 3

  • Not known

    No serious side effects were reported in trials, but adverse events tended to be more frequent in the higher-dose groups of two trials 3

  • In theory

    Theoretical concerns raised by reviewers: higher NAD+ might feed tumor growth or harmful signals from aging cells, and its by-product nicotinamide has a possible link with heart disease risk 3

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (not studied)
  • Current or past cancer (clinician first, given a theoretical concern about tumor growth)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

None routinely needed.

While taking it

None routinely needed.

No routine tests are needed and no evidence-based monitoring exists; trials found no changes in liver enzymes, BMI or blood pressure.

Key studies

  1. Meta-analysis2025342 adults in 8 randomized trials in Japan, India and the US, mostly healthy and middle-aged or older

    Effects of Nicotinamide Mononucleotide on Glucose and Lipid Metabolism in Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis of Randomised Controlled Trials

    NMN did not improve fasting glucose, insulin, HbA1c, blood fats, BMI or blood pressure; a borderline drop in insulin resistance disappeared when one trial was removed. The authors say the results do not support NMN for the general population. 3

  2. Review2023Human trials of NR, NMN and related compounds in healthy midlife and older adults and some patient groups

    Dietary Supplementation With NAD+-Boosting Compounds in Humans: Current Knowledge and Future Directions

    These supplements appear safe and can raise NAD+ in several tissues, but evidence that they improve how the body functions is unclear, and small trials limit conclusions. 4

Questions for your clinician

  • Is there any reason, such as a past cancer, that I should avoid NAD+ boosters?
  • Since trials found no change in blood sugar or cholesterol, how would we tell whether NMN is doing anything for me?
  • Which proven steps should come first for my metabolic or healthy-aging goals?

Status in the US

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement since FDA's September 2025 reversal. FDA said in late 2022 that NMN could not be a dietary supplement because it had first been investigated as a drug, then reversed that position in letters dated September 29 and December 2, 2025. NMN is still a new dietary ingredient, so a premarket notification to FDA is required. 12
