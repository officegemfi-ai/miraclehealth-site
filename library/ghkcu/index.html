What it is

GHK-Cu is the tripeptide GHK (glycine, histidine and lysine) bound to copper. GHK occurs naturally in human plasma, saliva and urine, and its plasma level falls with age. In laboratory and animal research it increases collagen and elastin production and speeds wound repair. It is used in cosmetic skin products and is not an FDA-approved drug.

Limited evidence

Skin benefits come from small cosmetic studies of copper-peptide creams in women, mostly presented at meetings by researchers tied to the developer; healing benefits come from animal studies, and FDA says human data on injections are limited.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Fewer fine lines, shallower wrinkles and thicker, denser skin with copper-peptide face and eye creams over 12 weeks in small studies of 41 to 71 women; most were presented at meetings rather than peer-reviewed 6

  • Limited evidence

    More collagen production in women using a copper-peptide thigh cream for one month than with vitamin C or retinoic acid creams, in a small pilot study 6

  • Limited evidence

    Faster skin recovery after irritation or tape stripping in human test models, using copper-peptide mixtures rather than GHK-Cu alone 6

  • Animal and lab studies

    Faster wound healing with more new blood vessels and collagen in rabbits, rats, mice, pigs and dogs, including diabetic wounds in rats 6

  • Animal and lab studies

    Raises collagen and elastin production and acts on many genes and pathways, according to reviews of mostly laboratory research 7

Risks and side effects

  • In theory

    Possible immune reactions to injected GHK-Cu, because the peptide can clump and carry peptide-related impurities, according to FDA 1

  • Not known

    Limited human safety data, especially for injections; reviews calling it very safe come from researchers linked to its developer and give no formal safety data 16

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no safety data)
  • Wilson disease or another copper-handling disorder (it carries copper; ask a clinician first)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Key studies

  1. Review2015Summary of small cosmetic studies in 41 to 71 women each, human skin-irritation models and animal studies

    GHK Peptide as a Natural Modulator of Multiple Cellular Pathways in Skin Regeneration

    Copper-peptide creams improved wrinkles, skin density and thickness over 12 weeks, and GHK-Cu sped wound healing in several animal species. The authors, from the developer's company, call it very safe but report no formal safety data. 6

  2. Review2018Human, animal and cell studies

    Regenerative and Protective Actions of the GHK-Cu Peptide in the Light of the New Gene Data

    GHK increases collagen, elastin and glycosaminoglycan production and improves tissue repair; gene data suggest it acts on many biochemical pathways at once. 7

Questions for your clinician

  • Is there a proven treatment for my skin, hair or healing goal that I should try first?
  • What are the risks of injecting a peptide that FDA says may cause immune reactions?
  • Could the copper in it matter for any condition I have?

Status in the US

Under FDA review Non-injectable compounding allowed; injectable under review. FDA lists GHK-Cu for non-injectable use in Category 1 (under evaluation), which its interim policy lets licensed pharmacies compound under set conditions. Injectable GHK-Cu came off the Category 2 safety-risk list in April 2026 after its nomination was withdrawn, which did not make it eligible for compounding; FDA's advisers are expected to review it before the end of February 2027, with no date set. 12345
