What it is

Dihexa (PNB-0408) is a small, lab-made molecule derived from angiotensin IV, a peptide fragment that has shown memory effects in animal research. In animal studies it works when taken by mouth and crosses into the brain. It binds hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) and strengthens signaling through HGF's receptor, c-Met, which in lab studies led to new connections (synapses) between nerve cells.

Animal and lab studies

Its memory effects come from rodent and lab studies by the group that developed it; FDA found no human exposure data.

What it may do

  • Animal and lab studies

    Better spatial learning in a water-maze memory test in rodents, an effect blocked by a drug that blocks HGF 5

  • Animal and lab studies

    Growth of new dendritic spines and synapses in hippocampal nerve cells in lab studies 5

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    No human safety data: FDA found no human exposure data and lacks information on whether it would cause harm in people 1

  • In theory

    It strengthens the HGF/c-Met growth-factor system; the effects of long-term stimulation in people are unknown 51

Talk to a clinician first if you are

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Current or past cancer, until more is known (it stimulates a growth-factor pathway)

Interactions

  • None well documented

While taking it

None routinely needed.

No evidence-based monitoring exists because dihexa has not been studied in people.

Key studies

  1. Animal2014Rodents and nerve-cell cultures

    The Procognitive and Synaptogenic Effects of Angiotensin IV-Derived Peptides Are Dependent on Activation of the Hepatocyte Growth Factor/c-Met System

    Dihexa bound HGF, boosted c-Met signaling, increased new synapses in hippocampal neurons and improved spatial learning; blocking HGF or c-Met removed these effects. Several authors co-founded a company developing HGF mimetics. 5

Questions for your clinician

  • Are there approved or well-studied options for my memory or focus concerns?
  • Could a compound that boosts a growth-factor pathway be risky given my personal or family cancer history?
  • How could we track any changes in my thinking or mood objectively?

Status in the US

Under FDA review Not approved; compounding review pending. As of April 2026 FDA lists dihexa acetate as 'nominated but withdrawn' rather than in Category 2, noting it found no human exposure data; it is not in Category 1, so pharmacies may not compound it. FDA's compounding advisory committee is expected to review it before the end of February 2027, with no date set. 1234
