Dapagliflozin

Also called Farxiga, SGLT2 inhibitor

A prescription pill, sold as Farxiga, that makes the kidneys pass extra sugar into the urine; it treats type 2 diabetes and lowers kidney and heart failure risks.

What it is

Dapagliflozin is an SGLT2 inhibitor. It blocks SGLT2 in the kidney tubules, the protein that takes back most filtered sugar, so more sugar leaves in the urine and blood sugar falls. Besides type 2 diabetes, it is approved for chronic kidney disease and heart failure, and those uses do not require diabetes. Its half-life is about 13 hours.

Strong evidence

FDA approval for each main use, based on large randomized trials including DECLARE-TIMI 58 (17,160 adults with type 2 diabetes), DAPA-HF (4,744 adults with heart failure) and DAPA-CKD (4,304 adults with kidney disease).

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Slower kidney decline and fewer deaths in adults with chronic kidney disease, with or without type 2 diabetes: the main outcome (at least a 50% fall in eGFR, kidney failure, or kidney or cardiovascular death) occurred in 9.2% versus 14.5% with placebo over about 2.4 years, and death from any cause in 4.7% versus 6.8% 31

  • Strong evidence

    Lower risk of cardiovascular death, heart failure hospitalization and urgent heart failure visits in adults with heart failure 14

  • Strong evidence

    Fewer heart failure hospitalizations in adults with type 2 diabetes and heart disease or several heart risk factors 1

  • Strong evidence

    Lower blood sugar in adults and children 10 and older with type 2 diabetes 12

Risks and side effects

  • Uncommon

    Genital yeast infections: about 7% to 8% of women versus 1.5% on placebo 1

  • Uncommon

    Urinary tract infections: 4.3% to 5.7% versus 3.7% on placebo 1

  • Rare

    Serious genital and urinary infections, including kidney and blood infections and Fournier's gangrene (a rare tissue-destroying infection around the genitals and anus), have led to hospital stays, repeated surgeries and deaths 12

  • Rare

    Ketoacidosis, which can occur with near-normal blood sugar: 27 cases versus 12 on placebo among about 17,000 adults with type 2 diabetes in DECLARE; risk is much higher in type 1 diabetes 12

  • Uncommon

    Dehydration, low blood pressure and acute kidney injury: volume-depletion events in 2.5% versus 2.4% on placebo in DECLARE, more likely in older adults, people with kidney impairment or those taking loop diuretics 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Type 1 diabetes (not recommended for blood sugar control because of ketoacidosis risk)
  • Past serious allergic reaction to dapagliflozin
  • Dialysis or an eGFR below 25 (not enrolled in its trials)
  • Polycystic kidney disease, or recent or current immune-suppressing treatment for kidney disease (not expected to help the kidneys)
  • Second or third trimester of pregnancy, or breastfeeding (not recommended)

Interactions

  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Loop diuretics: higher risk of dehydration
  • Lithium: levels can fall, so lithium needs closer monitoring
  • Urine glucose tests and the 1,5-AG blood test: give misleading results while taking it

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Kidney function (creatinine, eGFR)
  • Blood pressure and fluid status, especially in older adults, with kidney impairment or with diuretics
  • HbA1c or fasting glucose if you have diabetes

While taking it

  • Kidney function as needed (a small dip in eGFR in the first two weeks is expected, then stabilizes)
  • Blood pressure, watching for low readings
  • Ketones if ketoacidosis is suspected, and considered in type 1 diabetes
  • Lithium levels if you take lithium

The label calls for checking kidney function and fluid status before starting and as needed afterward, watching for low blood pressure, and considering ketone checks in people at risk; kidney changes that don't follow the expected early dip should be checked for acute kidney injury.

Key studies

  1. RCT20204,304 adults with chronic kidney disease (eGFR 25 to 75 with raised urine albumin), with or without type 2 diabetes

    Dapagliflozin in patients with chronic kidney disease

    Over a median of about 2.4 years, the main outcome occurred in 9.2% versus 14.5% with placebo (hazard ratio 0.61, 95% CI 0.51 to 0.72), and death from any cause in 4.7% versus 6.8% (hazard ratio 0.69); effects were similar with and without diabetes. 3

  2. Label202617,160 adults with type 2 diabetes in DECLARE-TIMI 58 (median 4.2 years), plus pooled placebo-controlled trials

    FARXIGA (dapagliflozin) tablets, for oral use: Prescribing Information

    In DECLARE, ketoacidosis occurred in 27 versus 12 people and severe low blood sugar in 0.7% versus 1.0%. In pooled trials, genital yeast infections affected 6.9% to 8.4% of women versus 1.5%. 1

Questions for your clinician

  • Should I pause it before surgery or a procedure that requires fasting?
  • Which signs of a genital or urinary infection, or of ketoacidosis, should make me call you right away?
  • Does my diuretic or lithium need extra monitoring while I take it?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for kidney disease, heart failure, diabetes. Approved as Farxiga to slow kidney decline and lower cardiovascular death and heart failure hospitalization in adults with chronic kidney disease at risk of progression, to lower cardiovascular death, heart failure hospitalization and urgent heart failure visits in adults with heart failure, to lower heart failure hospitalization in adults with type 2 diabetes and heart disease or several risk factors, and to improve blood sugar in adults and children 10 and older with type 2 diabetes. It has no boxed warning; in June 2026 FDA expanded its warning on genital and urinary infections, including Fournier's gangrene. 12
