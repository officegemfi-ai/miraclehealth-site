What it is

Ipamorelin is a synthetic peptide of five amino acids, some of them not found in nature. It switches on the ghrelin receptor, the target of a natural gut hormone, which prompts the pituitary gland to release growth hormone and stimulates stomach and gut movement. It was developed as a drug and tested in hospital patients recovering from bowel surgery, but development was discontinued and it is not an approved medicine.

Limited evidence

Human data are one pharmacology study in 48 healthy men and one 117-patient surgery trial that missed its main goal; there are no human studies of the under-the-skin use common today.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Raised growth hormone in proportion to blood levels in a placebo-controlled study of 48 healthy men; levels fell back to very low within 6 hours 6

  • Limited evidence

    Possibly faster return of bowel function after open abdominal surgery, seen only in a subgroup; the trial's main result (time to first tolerated meal) was not significant 6

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    In the 117-patient surgery trial, two deaths and serious complications occurred after treatment in the ipamorelin group; FDA notes causality was not reported 16

  • Not known

    More low potassium, trouble sleeping and high blood sugar at discharge than with placebo in the surgery trial 6

  • Not known

    No human data for injection under the skin, and no animal toxicity, cancer or reproductive studies, so FDA says it cannot rule out safety concerns 16

  • Not known

    Possible immune reactions from aggregation and impurities; FDA found ipamorelin acetate 'not well characterized' 16

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no reproductive studies)
  • Active cancer
  • Diabetes or prediabetes (clinician first: higher blood sugar was seen in a trial)
  • Competitive athletes (prohibited by WADA at all times)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Not an approved medicine. These are checks a clinician or a clinical trial would use, not a plan for taking it on your own.

Before starting

  • General health check with a clinician
  • IGF-1
  • Fasting glucose or HbA1c

While taking it

  • IGF-1
  • Fasting glucose or HbA1c

No evidence-based monitoring exists for ipamorelin; a review of growth hormone secretagogues advises tracking IGF-1 and blood sugar, and high blood sugar was seen in the surgery trial.

  1. Review2024FDA review of a 48-man pharmacology study, a 117-patient surgery trial and animal data

    The only treatment trial (bowel recovery after surgery) did not meet its main goal, there were no data for under-the-skin use, and FDA found insufficient data to conclude it would not raise safety concerns. 6

Questions for your clinician

  • Since the only treatment trial failed and there are no human data for injection under the skin, what would make this worth the risk for me?
  • How would we watch my blood sugar and IGF-1?
  • Is there an approved treatment for my actual goal or diagnosis?

Status in the US

FDA safety-risk list Not approved; on FDA's safety-risk list (503B). FDA keeps ipamorelin acetate in Category 2 (significant safety risks) for outsourcing facilities; for ordinary compounding pharmacies the nomination was withdrawn, and FDA's advisory committee voted 12-0 (one abstention) in October 2024 against adding it to the list they may compound.
