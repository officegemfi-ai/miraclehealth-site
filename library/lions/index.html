What it is

Lion's mane (Hericium erinaceus) is an edible mushroom native to North America, Europe and Asia, with deep roots in traditional Chinese medicine. It contains compounds called hericenones and, in its mycelium (the root-like threads), erinacines, which stimulated nerve growth factor in lab studies. It is eaten as food and taken in supplements; trials have used capsules, powders and baked goods containing it.

Limited evidence

A 2025 systematic review found 9 small human studies (about 325 people) with mixed effects on memory and some improvement in mood scores; most support for brain benefits comes from lab and animal work.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Mixed effects on memory and thinking: a small randomized trial in older adults with mild cognitive impairment (30 people) and a year-long pilot trial in mild Alzheimer's disease (41 people) reported better cognitive screening scores, but trials in healthy adults found no lasting gains 12

  • Limited evidence

    Lower depression and anxiety scores in two small randomized trials 1

  • Limited evidence

    Faster on one attention task an hour after a single dose, and lower self-rated stress after 28 days, in 41 young adults; other measures, including a standard stress questionnaire, did not improve and one memory measure favored placebo 2

  • Animal and lab studies

    Compounds from the mushroom stimulated nerve growth factor in lab studies, with signs of nerve protection in animals 1

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    Stomach discomfort, diarrhea, nausea, headache or skin rash in some trials; most trials reported no side effects, but reporting was often incomplete 1

  • Rare

    Possible allergic reactions: a case report described a severe lung reaction (acute respiratory distress syndrome) in a man who had taken it for a long time; the cause was not proven 1

  • Not known

    Long-term safety is unknown; trials were small and lasted from weeks to about a year 13

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Mushroom allergy
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (not studied)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Key studies

  1. Systematic review2025About 325 people in 9 human studies (5 randomized trials), plus lab studies

    Benefits, side effects, and uses of Hericium erinaceus as a supplement: a systematic review

    Cognitive results were mixed, depression and anxiety scores improved in two trials, and side effects were mostly mild; the evidence is small and varied, and some numbers in the review are inconsistent. 1

  2. RCT202341 healthy adults aged 18 to 45

    The Acute and Chronic Effects of Lion’s Mane Mushroom Supplementation on Cognitive Function, Stress and Mood in Young Adults: A Double-Blind, Parallel Groups, Pilot Study

    A single dose sped up one attention task, and after 28 days self-rated stress was somewhat lower, but other cognitive and mood measures did not improve; the authors call the findings tentative. 2

  3. Trial202610 healthy adults aged 18 to 55, no control group

    A Pilot Proof-of-Concept Study to Evaluate the Physiological and Cognitive Impacts of Hericium erinaceus on Healthy Adults

    No significant changes in executive function or the blood marker BDNF after 28 days, and no side effects reported; some authors had supplement-industry ties. 3
