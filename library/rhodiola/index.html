What it is

Rhodiola (Rhodiola rosea), also called golden root, rose root or arctic root, is a plant that grows in cold regions and at high altitudes in Europe, Asia and North America. It has a long history of traditional use in Russia, Scandinavia and other parts of Europe for work performance, endurance, fatigue, depression and altitude sickness. Today it is promoted for athletic performance, mood, thinking, energy and stress.

Limited evidence

A review of 11 controlled trials found contradictory results for fatigue, all at high risk of bias or poorly reported, and NIH calls most human research low to moderate quality.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Less mental fatigue in 3 of 5 randomized trials and less physical fatigue in 2 of 6 trials in healthy people; every study had a high risk of bias or reporting flaws 2

  • Limited evidence

    Mixed results for depression: a 2025 review of 3 studies reported lower symptom scores, but in a 12-week placebo-controlled trial of 57 adults with mild to moderate major depression, neither rhodiola nor the antidepressant sertraline improved scores significantly more than placebo; the trial was too small to settle the question (sertraline is an approved antidepressant) 34

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    Dizziness, headache, trouble sleeping, and dry mouth or excess saliva 1

  • Common

    Side effects were reported by 30% of people on rhodiola versus 17% on placebo in a 12-week depression trial 4

  • Not known

    Interactions with losartan, a blood pressure medicine, have been reported (NIH gives no details) 1

  • Not known

    Possibly safe for up to 12 weeks; longer-term safety, and safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding, are unknown 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (little is known)
  • Taking losartan or other blood pressure medicines (clinician first)
  • Depression, or taking an antidepressant (clinician first; don't stop prescribed treatment on your own)

Interactions

  • Losartan (blood pressure medicine): interactions have been reported
  • Other medicines: NIH advises talking with a health care provider before using any herbal product with medicines

Key studies

  1. Systematic review201211 controlled trials (10 randomized) in healthy people and patients

    Rhodiola rosea for physical and mental fatigue: a systematic review

    Results were contradictory: 2 of 6 physical-fatigue trials and 3 of 5 mental-fatigue trials reported benefit, and all studies had a high risk of bias or reporting flaws. 2

  2. RCT201557 adults with mild to moderate major depression

    Rhodiola rosea versus sertraline for major depressive disorder: A randomized placebo-controlled trial

    Over 12 weeks, neither rhodiola nor sertraline lowered depression scores significantly more than placebo. Side effects were reported by 30% on rhodiola, 63% on sertraline and 17% on placebo. 4

  3. Systematic review20253 studies in people with mild to moderate depression

    Rhodiola rosea L. reduces the depression symptoms – A systematic review

    The authors conclude the extract reduced symptom severity; participant numbers and study quality were not reported in the abstract. 3

Questions for your clinician

  • Could rhodiola interact with my blood pressure medicine, antidepressant or anything else I take?
  • Should my fatigue or low mood be checked for an underlying cause first?
  • How would we judge whether it is helping?

Status in the US

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement. Sold as a dietary supplement. NIH notes that supplements are not approved by FDA before they are sold and that manufacturers are responsible for their safety and labeling. 1
