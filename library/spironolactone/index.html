What it is

Spironolactone, sold as Aldactone, is a prescription diuretic (water pill) that can raise potassium levels. It is approved for heart failure, high blood pressure, fluid buildup from liver cirrhosis or kidney disease (nephrotic syndrome), and primary hyperaldosteronism. Because it also has anti-androgen (male-hormone-blocking) effects, dermatologists prescribe it off-label for acne and female pattern hair loss.

Moderate evidence

For acne, a well-run trial in 410 women found modest benefits; for female pattern hair loss the evidence is small, mostly uncontrolled studies; its heart and fluid uses are FDA-approved.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Improves survival and reduces hospital stays in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, and lowers blood pressure as an add-on drug (approved uses) 1

  • Moderate evidence

    In 410 women with persistent acne, acne symptom scores improved slightly more than with placebo at 12 weeks, and at 24 weeks 82% versus 63% said their skin had improved (off-label) 2

  • Limited evidence

    In female pattern hair loss (off-label), about 81% of patients taking spironolactone tablets improved on photos across four mostly small studies; results were too varied to pool, and the reviewers called for better, controlled trials 3

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    High potassium (hyperkalemia), which can be dangerous; the risk rises with other drugs or supplements that raise potassium 1

  • Not known

    Breast enlargement in men (gynecomastia), the most common side effect on the label, usually reversible 1

  • Common

    Headache (20% versus 12% on placebo) in women treated for acne; dizziness or lightheadedness was also more frequent (19% versus 12%), though not significantly 2

  • Uncommon

    Low blood pressure, dehydration and worsening kidney function from too much fluid loss; in a study of 185 women treated for hair loss, 5 developed low blood pressure 14

  • Uncommon

    Menstrual irregularities (about 8%) and dizziness or headache (about 6.5%) in hair-loss studies 3

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • High potassium, Addison's disease, or taking eplerenone (contraindicated)
  • Pregnant or could become pregnant (it feminized male fetuses in rats, and the label advises avoiding it in pregnancy)
  • Kidney problems, or taking potassium supplements, potassium-containing salt substitutes, ACE inhibitors or ARBs (clinician first because of potassium risk)
  • Athletes in tested sports (WADA bans it at all times as a diuretic)

Interactions

  • ACE inhibitors, ARBs, potassium supplements, salt substitutes containing potassium, NSAIDs, heparin and trimethoprim: risk of severe high potassium
  • Lithium: slower lithium clearance and risk of toxicity
  • Digoxin: can falsely raise digoxin blood test results
  • Cholestyramine: high-potassium metabolic acidosis has been reported
  • Aspirin and other NSAIDs: may weaken its effect

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Potassium
  • Kidney function (creatinine, eGFR)

While taking it

  • Potassium within a week of starting or changing the dose, and regularly after
  • Kidney function, electrolytes, uric acid and blood glucose periodically

The heart-failure and blood-pressure label calls for these checks; for healthy young women taking it for acne, the SAFA trial team advised a baseline test and judged ongoing monitoring unnecessary for most. Your prescriber decides which tests you need.

Key studies

  1. RCT2023410 women aged 18 and over with persistent acne

    Effectiveness of spironolactone for women with acne vulgaris (SAFA) in England and Wales: pragmatic, multicentre, phase 3, double blind, randomised controlled trial

    Acne symptom scores improved slightly more than with placebo at 12 weeks (adjusted difference 1.27), and at 24 weeks 82% versus 63% reported improvement. Headache was more common on spironolactone (20% versus 12%), with no serious related reactions. 2

  2. Systematic review20237 studies, 618 people with pattern hair loss (553 women)

    The Efficacy and Safety of Oral and Topical Spironolactone in Androgenetic Alopecia Treatment: A Systematic Review

    Most patients on oral spironolactone improved on photos, but study designs varied too much for a meta-analysis; about 3% stopped because of side effects. 3

  3. Cohort2025185 women prescribed spironolactone for hair loss (retrospective)

    Low Risk of Hypotension in Women Treated with Spironolactone for Hair Loss

    Five developed low blood pressure; the retrospective design and missing symptom data limit the findings. 4

Questions for your clinician

  • Do I need potassium or kidney tests, given my health and other medicines?
  • What birth control should I use while taking it?
  • How long until we can judge whether it is helping my acne or hair?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for heart and fluid problems; acne off-label. Aldactone is approved for heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, as an add-on for high blood pressure, for fluid buildup from cirrhosis or nephrotic syndrome, and for primary hyperaldosteronism. It is not approved for acne or hair loss; doctors have prescribed it off-label for acne for many years. 12
