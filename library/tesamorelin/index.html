What it is

Tesamorelin is a synthetic version of growth hormone-releasing factor (GRF): the full 44-amino-acid hormone with a small chemical group (hexenoyl) attached. It signals the pituitary gland to release the body's own growth hormone, which raises IGF-1 and reduces deep abdominal (visceral) fat. It is sold as Egrifta SV and Egrifta WR and has been FDA-approved since 2010.

Strong evidence

Two randomized, placebo-controlled trials in 816 adults with HIV supported its approval for reducing visceral fat; other uses (fatty liver, thinking skills) rest on single smaller trials.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Cut visceral (deep belly) fat by roughly 15% over 26 weeks versus placebo in adults with HIV and excess abdominal fat; the fat came back in people who stopped 13

  • Moderate evidence

    Lowered triglycerides and improved the cholesterol ratio in adults with HIV and excess abdominal fat 3

  • Moderate evidence

    Reduced liver fat over 12 months in 61 adults with HIV and fatty liver disease 4

  • Moderate evidence

    Improved executive function (planning and attention) over 20 weeks in 152 older adults with or without mild cognitive impairment 5

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Injection-site reactions such as redness, itching or pain (17% vs 6% on placebo) 1

  • Common

    Fluid-retention effects: joint pain, swelling of the limbs, tingling or numbness, and occasionally carpal tunnel syndrome 1

  • Common

    Very high IGF-1: about a third had IGF-1 more than 3 standard deviations above normal at 26 weeks; long-term effects unknown 1

  • Uncommon

    Higher blood sugar: 5% vs 1% reached a diabetes-range HbA1c at 26 weeks 1

  • Uncommon

    Allergic reactions such as itching, flushing, hives or rash (4% in trials) 1

  • Not known

    More deaths have been reported with growth hormone-type drugs during acute critical illness (after heart or abdominal surgery, major trauma, or acute respiratory failure); the label says to consider stopping it in critically ill patients 1

Talk to a clinician first if you are

  • Pregnancy (contraindicated)
  • Active cancer
  • Damage to the hypothalamic-pituitary axis (pituitary tumor or surgery, head radiation, major head trauma)
  • Known allergy to tesamorelin or its ingredients
  • Competitive athletes (prohibited by WADA)

Interactions

  • Glucocorticoid replacement for adrenal insufficiency: higher maintenance or stress doses may be needed
  • Drugs cleared by liver CYP450 enzymes (e.g., corticosteroids, sex steroids, anticonvulsants, cyclosporine): the label advises monitoring when combined

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • Fasting glucose and HbA1c
  • IGF-1

While taking it

  • IGF-1 (the label suggests considering stopping for persistent large elevations)
  • Glucose or HbA1c periodically
  • Eye exams for retinopathy in people with diabetes

The label calls for checking blood sugar before and during treatment and monitoring IGF-1, because tesamorelin raises IGF-1 and can cause glucose intolerance.

Key studies

  1. RCT2007412 adults with HIV and excess abdominal fat

    Metabolic Effects of a Growth Hormone-Releasing Factor in Patients with HIV

    Over 26 weeks, visceral fat fell 15.2% with tesamorelin and rose 5.0% with placebo, and triglycerides and cholesterol ratios improved, with no significant change in fasting glucose. About half of treated patients developed antibodies to the drug. 3

  2. RCT201961 adults with HIV and fatty liver

    Effects of tesamorelin on non-alcoholic fatty liver disease in HIV: a randomised, double-blind, multicentre trial

    Over 12 months, liver fat fell 37% from baseline relative to placebo, and 35% vs 4% reached normal liver fat; blood sugar did not differ between groups. 4

  3. RCT2012152 adults aged 55 to 87, 66 with mild cognitive impairment

    Effects of growth hormone-releasing hormone on cognitive function in adults with mild cognitive impairment and healthy older adults: results of a controlled trial

    After 20 weeks, tesamorelin improved executive function versus placebo in both groups and reduced body fat by 7.4%. Mild side effects were more common than on placebo (68% vs 36%). 5

  4. Label2025816 adults with HIV in two 26-week randomized trials, plus extensions

    EGRIFTA WR (tesamorelin) for injection: prescribing information (revised March 2025)

    Visceral fat fell 14-18% versus little change on placebo; people switched to placebo for another 26 weeks regained it. 1

Questions for your clinician

  • Has a scan or waist measurement confirmed excess visceral fat, and what result would show it is working?
  • How will we track my blood sugar and IGF-1, and at what point would we stop?
  • What happens to the fat I lose if I stop treatment?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved (Egrifta) for HIV-related belly fat. Approved since 2010 to reduce excess abdominal fat in adults with HIV who have lipodystrophy; the label says it is not a weight-loss drug and its long-term heart safety is not established. WADA bans it in sport. 12
