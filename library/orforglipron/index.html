Orforglipron

Also called LY3502970, Oral GLP-1

A GLP-1 medicine taken as a pill, sold as Foundayo, approved in April 2026 for weight management in adults with obesity or overweight with a related health problem.

What it is

Orforglipron is a small-molecule drug, not a peptide, that switches on the GLP-1 receptor. It lowers food intake, likely by reducing appetite, and slows stomach emptying. It is taken by mouth, and its half-life is about 29 to 49 hours. Eli Lilly has also tested it in type 2 diabetes, but so far it is approved only for weight management.

Strong evidence

FDA approval for weight management based on two 72-week randomized trials in 4,740 adults, 1,613 of them with type 2 diabetes.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Average weight loss of about 7% to 11% over 72 weeks, depending on dose, versus about 2% with placebo, in 3,127 adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes; 60% to 72% lost at least 5% of body weight versus 27% 2

  • Strong evidence

    Average weight loss of about 5% to 10% over 72 weeks versus 2.5% with placebo in 1,613 adults with type 2 diabetes and obesity or overweight 2

  • Moderate evidence

    Lower A1C in adults with type 2 diabetes: up to 2.2 points versus 1.4 with oral semaglutide over 52 weeks, and up to 1.7 versus 0.8 with dapagliflozin over 40 weeks (company-reported; not an approved use) 4

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Nausea, constipation, diarrhea, vomiting and indigestion: nausea affected 26% to 35% versus 10% on placebo; severe digestive reactions occurred in about 3% versus 1%, and 8% stopped because of side effects versus 3% 2

  • Uncommon

    Faster heart rate: an average rise of about 4 to 5 beats per minute, and a racing heartbeat (tachycardia) in 3% versus 0.9% on placebo 2

  • Uncommon

    Hair loss in 4% to 5% versus 2% on placebo 23

  • Uncommon

    Gallstones (1% versus 0.7% on placebo) and gallbladder inflammation (0.4% versus 0.3%) 2

  • Rare

    Acute pancreatitis (0.14 versus 0.04 cases per 100 patient-years) and acute kidney injury (0.2% versus 0.05%); breathing stomach contents into the lungs under anesthesia or deep sedation is also a labeled warning 2

  • In theory

    Boxed warning: thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents with GLP-1 drugs that act in rodents; orforglipron does not act in rats or mice, so rodent studies could not show this risk, and whether it applies to people has not been determined 2

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • Past serious allergic reaction to orforglipron
  • Pregnancy (the label says to stop when pregnancy is recognized) or breastfeeding (not recommended)
  • Severe gastroparesis (not recommended)
  • Type 2 diabetes with diabetic retinopathy (needs closer eye monitoring)

Interactions

  • Birth control pills: may work less well after starting and after each dose increase; the label advises a non-oral method or an added barrier method for a time
  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (such as ritonavir) or strong CYP3A4 inducers: can change orforglipron levels; the label limits or avoids some combinations
  • Simvastatin: orforglipron roughly doubles exposure to its active form, so the label caps the statin dose
  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Other GLP-1 drugs: using them together is not recommended

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

  • HbA1c or fasting glucose
  • Kidney function (creatinine, eGFR)
  • Resting heart rate
  • Eye exam if you have diabetic retinopathy

While taking it

  • Blood glucose if you have diabetes, especially with insulin or a sulfonylurea
  • Kidney function if vomiting or diarrhea is severe
  • Resting heart rate at regular visits
  • Eye checks if you have diabetic retinopathy

The label calls for monitoring blood sugar in people with diabetes, kidney function when digestive side effects could cause dehydration, and diabetic eye disease; it reports heart-rate rises without a specific monitoring rule and says routine calcitonin or thyroid ultrasound checks are of uncertain value.

Key studies

  1. Label20263,127 adults with obesity or overweight, without diabetes (72-week placebo-controlled trial)

    FOUNDAYO (orforglipron) tablets, for oral use: Prescribing Information

    Average weight change was -7.4% to -11.1% across three doses versus -2.1% with placebo; 59.6% to 71.5% lost at least 5% of body weight versus 26.8%. 2

  2. Label20261,613 adults with type 2 diabetes and obesity or overweight (72-week placebo-controlled trial)

    FOUNDAYO (orforglipron) tablets, for oral use: Prescribing Information

    Average weight change was -5.1% to -9.6% versus -2.5% with placebo; 47.4% to 67% lost at least 5% of body weight versus 26.8%. 2

  3. RCT2026Adults with type 2 diabetes in the ACHIEVE-2, ACHIEVE-3 and ACHIEVE-5 trials

    Lilly's oral GLP-1 Foundayo (orforglipron) delivered superior A1C control and weight loss in three pivotal type 2 diabetes trials

    A1C fell up to 2.2 points versus 1.4 with oral semaglutide (52 weeks) and up to 1.7 versus 0.8 with dapagliflozin (40 weeks). In those two head-to-head trials, 8.7% to 12.4% stopped because of side effects versus 1.2% to 4.9% on the comparators. Company-reported. 4

Questions for your clinician

  • Do I need to change my birth control when starting or increasing orforglipron?
  • Do any of my other medicines, such as statins or HIV drugs, interact with it?
  • Which side effects, such as severe stomach pain or a racing heart, mean I should call you?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for weight management (April 2026). Approved April 1, 2026 as Foundayo for adults with obesity, or overweight with at least one weight-related condition; it was the first new molecular entity approved under FDA's Commissioner's National Priority Voucher pilot. It is not approved for type 2 diabetes: Lilly said in June 2026 it planned to file for that use by the end of the second quarter, and no FDA decision was found. 1234
