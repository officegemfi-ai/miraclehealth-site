What it is

Ovagen is one of the short 'bioregulator' peptides associated with Vladimir Khavinson's research group in Russia. An online peptide catalog lists it as a chain of three amino acids, glutamic acid, aspartic acid and leucine (Glu-Asp-Leu, or EDL), but we found no journal article or official register that uses the name Ovagen. A patent from Khavinson's group claims the Glu-Asp-Leu peptide helps the liver regenerate, and the group's lab work suggests short peptides like this act by changing which genes are switched on.

Animal and lab studies

Rat, tissue-culture and cell studies of the Glu-Asp-Leu peptide; the only data in people are one small study described in the developer's patent, and none of these sources uses the name Ovagen.

What it may do

  • Animal and lab studies

    About 75% more dividing liver cells after part of the liver was removed, in rats given the Glu-Asp-Leu peptide 4

  • Animal and lab studies

    Liver chemistry and stored liver sugar (glycogen) moved back toward normal in rats with chemically induced cirrhosis 4

  • Limited evidence

    Less weakness and pain reported in a small study of 34 people with chronic hepatitis, described only in a patent 4

  • Animal and lab studies

    Faster growth of rat liver tissue in lab culture 4

  • Animal and lab studies

    Changed activity of p53, a gene that controls cell death, in aging kidney cell cultures 5

Risks and side effects

  • Not known

    What products sold as Ovagen contain is unverified: the Glu-Asp-Leu identity comes from an online catalog, and no FDA-approved or compounded version exists 613

  • Not known

    Human safety has not been tested independently; the developers report no toxic effects in mice, rats and guinea pigs 4

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (never studied)
  • Liver disease such as hepatitis or cirrhosis, unless a clinician is involved
  • Under 18 (never studied)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Not an approved medicine. These are checks a clinician or a clinical trial would use, not a plan for taking it on your own.

Before starting

  • General health check, including a liver panel (ALT, AST, bilirubin)

While taking it

None routinely needed.

No evidence-based monitoring exists for Ovagen; a liver panel is a sensible baseline because liver support is its claimed use.

Key studies

  1. Animal2008Rats; the same patent also describes 34 people with chronic hepatitis

    Peptide stimulating regeneration of lever [sic] tissue, pharmaceutical composition based thereon and use thereof

    In rats, the Glu-Asp-Leu peptide increased liver cell division after partial liver removal and moved liver markers toward normal in cirrhosis. In the human study, most treated patients reported less weakness; the methods are not fully described. 4

  2. Review2021Cell cultures and DNA in test tubes

    Peptide Regulation of Gene Expression: A Systematic Review

    A review by the developers of evidence that short peptides bind DNA and change gene activity; the Glu-Asp-Leu peptide changed p53 activity in aging kidney cell cultures. 5

Status in the US

Not approved Not FDA-approved; not on FDA compounding lists. Ovagen is not an FDA-approved drug and appears on none of FDA's compounding lists (nominated substances, updated May 2026; safety-risk and withdrawn nominations, April 2026). FDA allows pharmacy compounding only from ingredients that are in an approved drug, meet a USP standard, or are on or under review for its bulk list. 123
