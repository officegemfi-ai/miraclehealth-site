What it is

Armodafinil, sold as Nuvigil, is a prescription drug that promotes wakefulness. Modafinil is a 50-50 mix of two mirror-image forms of the same molecule; armodafinil is one of them, the R-form. It is approved for adults with excessive sleepiness from obstructive sleep apnea, narcolepsy or shift work disorder, and it is a Schedule IV controlled substance.

Limited evidence

It is approved for sleepiness from three sleep disorders; for focus in rested healthy people, the evidence comes from studies of modafinil, which found only small effects on a few kinds of thinking tests.

What it may do

  • Strong evidence

    Improves wakefulness in adults with excessive sleepiness from obstructive sleep apnea, narcolepsy or shift work disorder (approved use) 1

  • Limited evidence

    Evidence for focus in rested people comes from modafinil, its parent mixture: across 19 placebo-controlled trials the overall effect on thinking tests was small, and another set of meta-analyses found a clear gain only in memory updating 23

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Headache (17% versus 9% on placebo), nausea (7% versus 3%), dizziness (5% versus 2%) and insomnia (5% versus 1%) in trials 1

  • Rare

    Serious rash, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome, has required hospital care; in children given modafinil, rash led to stopping in about 8 in 1,000; any new rash needs prompt medical review 1

  • Not known

    Allergic and multi-organ reactions, including angioedema, anaphylaxis and DRESS (a drug reaction with rash, fever and organ involvement) 1

  • Not known

    Psychiatric effects: anxiety, agitation and irritability led some trial patients to stop, and mania, delusions, hallucinations, suicidal thoughts and aggression have been reported 1

  • Not known

    Small rises in blood pressure and pulse; the label advises against use with a history of left ventricular hypertrophy, or with mitral valve prolapse that caused symptoms on stimulants before 1

  • Not known

    Misuse, physical dependence and tolerance have been reported 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or planning pregnancy (animal data suggest harm, and fetal growth restriction and miscarriage have been reported)
  • Allergy to armodafinil or modafinil
  • Heart disease, left ventricular hypertrophy, or mitral valve prolapse that caused symptoms on stimulants (needs a clinician first)
  • History of psychosis, depression or mania (needs a clinician first)
  • Athletes in tested sports: WADA bans modafinil in competition and covers all optical isomers of stimulants, and armodafinil is one of the two mirror-image forms of modafinil

Interactions

  • Hormonal birth control: may be less effective during use and for one month after stopping
  • Cyclosporine: blood levels may drop
  • Drugs cleared by the liver enzyme CYP2C19: they may stay in the body longer
  • Drugs cleared by the liver enzymes CYP3A4/5: they may be cleared faster and work less well
  • Warfarin: more frequent INR checks may be needed; MAO inhibitors: use with caution

Blood markers to watch

Before starting

None routinely needed.

While taking it

None routinely needed.

The sources reviewed set no routine blood tests; the label says closer monitoring of heart rate and blood pressure may be appropriate.

Key studies

  1. Label2025Adults with obstructive sleep apnea, narcolepsy or shift work disorder (645 on armodafinil in pooled placebo-controlled trials)

    NUVIGIL (armodafinil) tablets: prescribing information

    Approved to improve wakefulness; headache was the most common side effect (17% versus 9%), and the label warns about serious rash, psychiatric symptoms and reduced birth-control effectiveness. 1

  2. Meta-analysis201919 placebo-controlled trials, 767 adults who were not sleep-deprived (modafinil, not armodafinil)

    The Efficacy of Modafinil as a Cognitive Enhancer: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

    Modafinil had a small overall effect on cognition (g = 0.10), mainly on executive function and processing speed; the authors saw only limited potential as a cognitive enhancer in rested people. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • Is my sleepiness from a sleep disorder that needs diagnosis and treatment first?
  • Will armodafinil weaken my birth control or change the levels of my other medicines?
  • Do my heart or mental-health history make it unsafe for me?

Status in the US

FDA-approved FDA-approved for sleep disorders; Schedule IV. Nuvigil is approved to improve wakefulness in adults with excessive sleepiness from obstructive sleep apnea (it treats the sleepiness, not the airway obstruction), narcolepsy or shift work disorder. It is a Schedule IV controlled substance, it is not approved for children, and use for focus is off-label. 1
