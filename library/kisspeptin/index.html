What it is

Kisspeptin is a hormone made in the hypothalamus, coded by the KISS1 gene, and a key trigger for the release of GnRH, which drives the reproductive hormone chain. It acts on a receptor called KISS1R (GPR54), and this signaling is needed for normal puberty. Kisspeptin-10 is a 10-amino-acid piece of the longer kisspeptin-54 and is the smallest fragment with full activity. In men it quickly raises LH, the pituitary hormone that drives testosterone.

Limited evidence

Short human physiology studies, often with 4 to 6 people per group, show it raises LH and testosterone; there are no treatment trials, and FDA's advisers found no convincing efficacy data.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Rapid, dose-related rise in LH in healthy men 56

  • Limited evidence

    Higher testosterone and more frequent LH pulses during continuous infusion in small groups of healthy men 5

  • Limited evidence

    LH rise in women around ovulation, but not earlier in the menstrual cycle 6

Risks and side effects

  • In theory

    Possible immune reactions to the peptide or its impurities 1

  • Not known

    FDA has little or no safety information for the routes people use and lists it as a significant safety risk 1

  • Not known

    Human safety data cover only hours of use in a handful of people; no adverse events were seen in 6 healthy men 5

  • In theory

    It raises testosterone and other reproductive hormones, which could matter in hormone-sensitive conditions 5

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Hormone-sensitive cancer, such as prostate or breast cancer, now or in the past
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no safety data)
  • Competitive athletes, since kisspeptin is on the 2026 prohibited list for males

Interactions

  • None well documented

Blood markers to watch

Not an approved medicine. These are checks a clinician or a clinical trial would use, not a plan for taking it on your own.

Before starting

  • LH and FSH
  • Total testosterone (men) or estradiol (women)

While taking it

  • LH and testosterone or estradiol, if a clinician is supervising use

No evidence-based monitoring exists for kisspeptin-10; these are the hormones it acts on in studies.

Key studies

  1. RCT2012Healthy men and women, 4 to 5 per group (conference abstract)

    Contrasting effects of kisspeptin-10 between men and women reveal sexual dimorphism in the hypothalamic regulation of human reproduction

    In a randomized, double-blind study, kisspeptin-10 raised LH in men and in women near ovulation, but not in women earlier in the cycle. 6

  2. Trial201114 healthy men (6 in a dose-response study, 4 in each of two infusion studies)

    Kisspeptin-10 is a potent stimulator of LH and increases pulse frequency in men

    Single doses rapidly raised LH; hours-long exposure raised testosterone and LH pulse frequency. No adverse events were reported in the dose-response study. 5

Questions for your clinician

  • What is causing my low testosterone or low libido, and which approved treatments fit?
  • Could raising my sex hormones affect any cancer risk or other condition I have?
  • Which hormone tests should I have before and during any treatment?

Status in the US

FDA safety-risk list FDA Category 2: pharmacies may not compound it. FDA placed kisspeptin-10 in Category 2 in September 2023 over immunogenicity and limited safety data, and it was still there on FDA's May 2026 list; in October 2024 FDA's advisers voted 11-0 against adding it to the compounding list. Kisspeptin is also banned for male athletes on the 2026 World Anti-Doping list. 1234
