What it is

Mazdutide (IBI362) is a lab-made drug that switches on the receptors for two hormones, GLP-1 and glucagon; its developer, Innovent Biologics, describes it as an analog of the hormone oxyntomodulin. Innovent develops it in China under a license from Eli Lilly. China approved it in 2025, but it is not approved in the US.

Moderate evidence

One published phase 3 randomized trial in 610 Chinese adults with obesity or overweight, plus company-reported phase 3 diabetes trials in China; long-term safety is not known.

What it may do

  • Moderate evidence

    Average weight loss of 11.0% and 14.0% on two doses at 48 weeks versus 0.3% with placebo in 610 Chinese adults with obesity or overweight; 35.7% and 49.5% lost at least 15% of body weight versus 2.0% 4

  • Moderate evidence

    Lower A1C in Chinese adults with type 2 diabetes: about 1.4 to 2.0 points more than placebo over 24 weeks, with 5.6% to 7.8% weight loss versus 1.3% (company-reported) 2

  • Moderate evidence

    Smaller waist and lower blood pressure, blood fats, uric acid, liver enzymes and liver fat than with placebo in the same 610-person trial (company-reported) 5

Risks and side effects

  • Common

    Nausea, diarrhea and vomiting were the most common side effects, mostly mild to moderate; 0.5% to 1.5% stopped because of side effects versus 1.0% with placebo 45

  • Not known

    Long-term safety is not known, and the published and company-reported trials were in Chinese adults 42

  • Not known

    Mazdutide sold in the US as a 'research' product is unapproved and illegally marketed, according to FDA warnings 3

  • In theory

    Approved GLP-1 drugs carry a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents; whether this applies to mazdutide was not reported in the sources checked 6

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Not for use in the US outside a clinical trial: it is not approved here, and "research" versions sold online are unapproved and unregulated
  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2 (approved GLP-1 drugs carry this contraindication)
  • Pregnant, breastfeeding or planning pregnancy

Interactions

  • Not well studied. Approved GLP-1 drugs raise the risk of low blood sugar with insulin or sulfonylureas and slow stomach emptying, which can change how oral medicines are absorbed

Blood markers to watch

Not an approved medicine. These are checks a clinician or a clinical trial would use, not a plan for taking it on your own.

Before starting

  • General health check: weight, blood pressure, HbA1c or fasting glucose, kidney and liver tests

While taking it

  • Same general checks at regular visits
  • Resting heart rate

No evidence-based monitoring exists in the US because it is not approved here; these are general health checks, with heart rate added because the label of an approved GLP-1 drug (semaglutide) calls for it.

Key studies

  1. RCT2025610 Chinese adults with obesity or overweight (GLORY-1)

    Once-Weekly Mazdutide in Chinese Adults with Obesity or Overweight

    At 32 weeks, weight changed by -10.1% and -12.6% on two doses versus +0.45% with placebo; at 48 weeks, by -11.0% and -14.0% versus -0.3%. Stopping because of side effects was 0.5% to 1.5% versus 1.0%. Funded by Innovent. 4

  2. RCT2025Chinese adults with type 2 diabetes (DREAMS-1 and DREAMS-2)

    Innovent Announces Mazdutide Received Approval from China's NMPA for Glycemic Control in Adults with Type 2 Diabetes

    A1C fell about 1.4 to 2.0 points more than with placebo at 24 weeks, and slightly more than with dulaglutide at 28 weeks, with greater weight loss. Company-reported. 2

Questions for your clinician

  • Would a clinical trial be an option for me?
  • How do approved options, such as semaglutide or tirzepatide, compare for me?
  • What are the risks of products sold as 'research' mazdutide?

Status in the US

In clinical trials Not approved in the US; approved in China in 2025. Innovent announced Chinese approval for chronic weight management in June 2025 and for blood sugar control in type 2 diabetes in September 2025. It has no US approval, and FDA has warned companies selling mazdutide falsely labeled 'for research purposes' or 'not for human consumption.' 123
