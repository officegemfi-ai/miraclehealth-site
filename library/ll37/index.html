What it is

LL-37 is the only human member of the cathelicidin family of antimicrobial peptides: a 37-amino-acid peptide cut from a precursor protein called hCAP18. White blood cells and skin cells release it, especially after injury, inflammation or infection, and in lab studies it kills bacteria, some viruses and fungi while also steering inflammation. A lab-made version has been tested as a gel on chronic leg ulcers; it is not an approved drug.

Limited evidence

Human evidence is limited to chronic leg-ulcer trials of a topical gel; the larger, 144-patient trial found no benefit across all patients, only in larger wounds, according to the developer. Immune benefits come from lab and animal work.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Better healing of large chronic venous leg ulcers with a topical gel than with placebo in a 144-patient trial, but no difference across all wound sizes; reported by the developer in a press release 4

  • Animal and lab studies

    Kills bacteria, enveloped viruses and fungi and recruits immune cells in laboratory studies 5

  • Animal and lab studies

    Promotes the movement and growth of skin cells involved in wound repair, in laboratory studies 5

Risks and side effects

  • In theory

    Possible immune reactions, which FDA flags for some routes of use 1

  • In theory

    Laboratory and animal findings suggest it may harm male reproduction and promote tumors in some tissues, according to FDA 16

  • In theory

    LL-37 is part of the immune attack in psoriasis and lupus, where LL-37 bound to the body's own DNA switches on inflammatory immune cells; adding more could in theory worsen these diseases 5

  • Not known

    FDA says it lacks enough safety information to know whether it would cause harm in people; we found no human studies of injected LL-37 1

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Psoriasis, lupus, psoriatic or rheumatoid arthritis, or another autoimmune disease (LL-37 is involved in the inflammation)
  • Current or past cancer (laboratory findings suggest it may promote some tumors)
  • Men trying to conceive (animal and laboratory findings suggest harm to male reproduction)
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no safety data)

Interactions

  • None well documented

Key studies

  1. RCT2020144 adults with chronic venous leg ulcers

    Promore Pharma announces positive results from Phase IIb study of ropocamptide in the treatment of venous leg ulcers

    After 13 weeks of a topical LL-37 gel, healing did not differ from placebo across all patients; in wounds of at least 10 square centimeters, complete healing and wound shrinkage were better. No serious adverse events were judged drug-related. 4

  2. Review2020Patient, animal and cell studies

    Immunomodulatory Role of the Antimicrobial LL-37 Peptide in Autoimmune Diseases and Viral Infections

    LL-37 kills microbes and steers immune responses; in psoriasis and lupus, LL-37 bound to self-DNA activates immune cells, and patients can develop T cells or antibodies against LL-37. 5

  3. Review2020Laboratory and animal studies

    Significance of LL-37 on Immunomodulation and Disease Outcome

    Depending on the tissue and setting, LL-37 can boost inflammation and fight tumors, or dampen inflammation and promote cancer. 6

Questions for your clinician

  • Do I have an autoimmune or inflammatory condition, such as psoriasis or lupus, that LL-37 could worsen?
  • Given laboratory signals for tumor growth and harm to male fertility, what is my personal risk?
  • Which proven treatments fit my wound or infection problem?

Status in the US

Under FDA review Not approved; compounding review pending. FDA took LL-37 off its Category 2 safety-risk list in April 2026 after the nomination was withdrawn, which did not make it eligible for compounding. FDA's advisers are expected to review it before the end of February 2027, with no date set. 123
