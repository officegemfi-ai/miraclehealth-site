What it is

Selank is a synthetic seven-amino-acid peptide built from tuftsin, a natural four-amino-acid fragment of human antibodies (immunoglobulin G), with three amino acids added so it lasts longer in the body. Researchers propose that it calms anxiety by acting on the brain's GABA system, the target of benzodiazepines, but this rests mainly on rat studies. It is not an FDA-approved drug.

Limited evidence

The only human evidence we found is one Russian comparative study of 62 patients, in which selank eased anxiety about as much as the drug medazepam; other data come from rat studies.

What it may do

  • Limited evidence

    Eased anxiety about as much as the anti-anxiety drug medazepam in a study of 62 adults with generalized anxiety disorder or neurasthenia, with added anti-fatigue and mildly stimulating effects 5

  • Animal and lab studies

    Changed the activity of GABA receptor genes in the rat brain within hours of a single dose, which researchers suggest could explain calming effects 6

Risks and side effects

  • In theory

    Possible immune reactions, because the peptide can clump and carry peptide-related impurities, according to FDA 1

  • Not known

    Human safety is essentially unstudied: FDA says important safety information is lacking, and the clinical study abstract reports no side-effect data 15

  • In theory

    Researchers describe marked effects on the immune system, with unknown consequences in people 6

Who should avoid it or check with a clinician first

  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no safety data)
  • Taking benzodiazepines or other sedatives, unless a clinician advises (it is thought to act on the same GABA system)
  • Autoimmune disease or a weakened immune system, unless a clinician advises (it may affect immune function)

Interactions

  • Benzodiazepines and other sedatives: it is thought to act on the same GABA system, so check with a clinician

Key studies

  1. Trial200862 adults with generalized anxiety disorder or neurasthenia (30 on selank, 32 on medazepam)

    Efficacy and possible mechanisms of action of a new peptide anxiolytic selank in the therapy of generalized anxiety disorders and neurasthenia

    Both drugs reduced anxiety to a similar degree; selank also had anti-fatigue and stimulating effects, and blood enkephalin activity rose during treatment. The abstract does not report blinding, duration or side effects. 5

  2. Animal201630 rats

    Selank Administration Affects the Expression of Some Genes Involved in GABAergic Neurotransmission

    A single nasal dose changed the activity of dozens of brain genes within 1 to 3 hours, including GABA receptor genes. These are gene-activity measurements in pooled tissue, not evidence of an effect on behavior. 6

Questions for your clinician

  • Which proven treatments for anxiety fit my situation, and how does their evidence compare?
  • Could it interact with sedatives, sleep aids or other medicines I take?
  • What are the risks of using an unapproved peptide whose purity is not verified?

Status in the US

Not approved Not approved; not eligible for compounding. Its compounding nomination was withdrawn, moving it off FDA's Category 2 safety-risk list, but FDA still says it may provoke immune reactions and that important information on its safety in people is lacking. It is not on FDA's May 2026 list of nominated substances or among the peptides set for advisory review in 2026 and early 2027. 1234
