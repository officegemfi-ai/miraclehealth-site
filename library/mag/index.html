Magnesium glycinate

Also called Magnesium, Magnesium bisglycinate

What it isevidence librarian

Magnesium is a mineral that acts as a cofactor in more than 300 enzyme systems. It is involved in energy production, bone structure, making DNA and RNA, and moving calcium and potassium across cell membranes. Magnesium glycinate, also called bisglycinate, is a chelated form that combines magnesium with glycine. It may be slightly better absorbed than inorganic forms, but direct comparisons are limited. Glycine itself has been studied for sleep, with inconclusive results.

Limited evidence

The sleep evidence for the glycinate form rests on one four-week trial with a small effect. Other magnesium trials show marginal lowering of blood pressure and mixed results for migraine and blood sugar.

What it may doevidence librarian

  • Limited evidence

    Slightly lower insomnia severity than placebo after 4 weeks in a trial of 155 adults with self-reported poor sleep; the difference was below the usual threshold for a meaningful change 2

  • Limited evidence

    Marginally lower blood pressure in clinical trials of magnesium supplements 1

  • Limited evidence

    Possibly fewer migraine attacks: NIH's complementary health center calls it possibly effective, and the American Academy of Neurology and American Headache Society rate it probably effective for prevention 31

  • Limited evidence

    May improve insulin sensitivity in people at high risk of diabetes; effects on blood sugar control in people with diabetes are uncertain 41

Risks and side effectssafety agent

  • Uncommon

    Diarrhea, nausea and stomach cramps, mainly at high doses; NIH notes these most often with the carbonate, chloride, gluconate and oxide forms, and in the glycinate trial side effects were no more frequent than with placebo 132

  • Rare

    Magnesium toxicity from very high doses: low blood pressure, nausea, vomiting, flushing and lethargy that can progress to muscle weakness, trouble breathing, irregular heartbeat and cardiac arrest; a few deaths have been reported 1

  • Not known

    Higher risk of magnesium toxicity when kidney function is impaired 1

Who should skip it, or ask a doctor first

  • Kidney disease or reduced kidney function
  • Anyone hoping to prevent migraines with it (NIH advises doing so under a health care provider's supervision)
  • Taking osteoporosis drugs (bisphosphonates), tetracycline or quinolone antibiotics, or diuretics (check with a doctor first)

What it can clash with

  • Bisphosphonates (osteoporosis drugs): magnesium-rich products can reduce their absorption
  • Tetracycline and quinolone antibiotics: magnesium can form insoluble complexes with them
  • Diuretics: loop and thiazide types increase magnesium loss in urine, while potassium-sparing types reduce it
  • Proton pump inhibitors: long-term use, typically over a year, can cause low magnesium levels

Blood tests to watchmarkers agent

Before starting

  • Serum magnesium if you are starting long-term proton pump inhibitor treatment (FDA advises checking)

While taking it

  • Periodic serum magnesium during long-term proton pump inhibitor use

Most people need no tests; serum magnesium is the usual test but does not reflect total body magnesium well, and no single method is considered satisfactory.

The main studiesevidence librarian

  1. RCT2025155 adults aged 18 to 65 with self-reported poor sleep

    Magnesium Bisglycinate Supplementation in Healthy Adults Reporting Poor Sleep: A Randomized, Placebo-Controlled Trial

    Over 4 weeks, insomnia scores fell more with magnesium bisglycinate than with placebo (small effect), but fatigue, daytime sleepiness, mood and stress measures did not differ; most participants reported no side effects. 2

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Is my kidney function normal enough for a magnesium supplement?
  • Do any of my medicines, such as antibiotics, osteoporosis drugs, diuretics or acid reducers, interact with magnesium?
  • Could something other than low magnesium be behind my sleep problems?

Where it stands in the USsafety agent

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement. Sold as a dietary supplement; magnesium is also an ingredient in some laxatives and heartburn remedies. FDA allows a qualified health claim linking magnesium to lower risk of high blood pressure but has concluded the evidence is inconsistent and inconclusive. 1
