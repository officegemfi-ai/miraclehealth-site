Method

How the numbers are made.

Updated Nov 4, 2026

Every answer on Miracle Health draws on two sources, kept apart and always named: the studies, in the Library’s fact sheets, and the community, in the Community Signal: what members report first-hand and what people say on public forums. This page explains how each number is made, what it can and can’t tell you, and what we never do.

The short version

  • Studies come first whenever there are studies in people for what you asked. Where there aren’t, what people report comes first, always said to be reports, not evidence.
  • Reports are weighted, never people: a report its author confirmed, a longer one and one followed up weeks later count more. Nobody gets a credibility score.
  • Ratings come with a likely range, and start from what members report for that use everywhere, so a handful of glowing reports can’t outrank many good ones.
  • Next to what people report, we show how many people improve anyway, on a placebo or with time, from published trials.
  • A side effect reported unusually often for one compound is flagged, and serious reactions point to medical help and the FDA’s MedWatch.
  • No doses, no sellers, no diagnosis. Sig keeps nothing you write.

The studies

Each fact sheet is checked claim by claim against sources we opened: FDA labels and documents, NIH fact sheets and published trials. The evidence for each use is graded: strong (several good trials in people, or approved for that use), moderate (at least one good trial in people), limited (small or short studies in people), animal and lab studies only, or anecdotal (user reports only). Each sheet shows the date it was checked.

How a report is read

Members post in their own words. Sig reads each post: is it about their own use; how it went compared with before (better, about the same or worse); for how long; what they took it for; any side effects, under one name each (so “nauseous” and “mild nausea” count together); and whether a reaction sounds serious. The author then sees that read in one line, “Did we read you right?”, and confirms it or fixes any part with a tap. If something important is missing, usually how long, it asks that one question. Their word always wins over the read.

Two, six and twelve weeks later, the author is asked “How is it now?”: better, about the same or worse; still taking it or not; and if not, why (it worked, it didn’t help, side effects, cost or hassle). The latest answer is the one that counts, and why people stop is counted too.

  • Each account counts once per compound: its latest report.

The Community Signal

One number from 0 to 100 per compound, blend and stack. It has four parts:

  • 65% how reports went (better 1, about the same ½, worse 0)
  • 15% how much they agree
  • 10% how many independent voices there are
  • 10% how much there is to go on

Every Signal starts from Miracle Health’s reading of public forums, which counts as 50 reports; every first-hand report here shifts the balance toward members’ own experience.

Each Signal earns a level for how far along that line it is:

  • Forum data
  • Early signal
  • Community-backed (25% from members, 10+ reports)
  • Community-verified (50%, 25+ reports from 15+ members)
  • Signal Certified (75%, 50+ reports, 30+ members)

Every change is kept as a new version.

Ratings by use, with a likely range

A compound gets its own rating for a use (sleep, joint pain, injury healing…) once three reports name that use. The rating starts from what members report for that use across every compound, worth eight reports, and moves toward the compound’s own reports as they come in: so three glowing reports pull a rating up only so far, and two hundred good ones carry it.

Each rating shows its likely range (90%), worked out from the effective number of reports so that weights never make it look surer than the reports allow, and the share of its reports that went better, with a 95% interval.

Sig lists what the community reports most for a use only from ratings with 10 or more reports from 5 or more people, in order of the low end of their range, and never as a recommendation.

Does the Signal agree with the studies?

One check we run in the open. If the Signal measures something real, compounds with good trials in people should, on the whole, be reported as working more often than compounds studied only in animals. Below, from the live numbers: the share of members’ reports that went better, for every compound with a fact sheet and at least 10 reports, grouped by how strong its studies are. It’s a rough check, not proof: people take strongly studied medicines for what they were studied for, and other compounds for many things.

This fills in by itself as reports come in: it needs at least three compounds with 10 or more reports in a group to compare.

The side-effect watch

For each compound we compare how often each side effect is reported with how often it’s reported for every other compound. A side effect is flagged when all four of these hold:

  • at least three reports mention it
  • it’s reported at least twice as often (a proportional reporting ratio of 2 or more, with half a report added to each count)
  • it’s unlikely to be chance (a Yates chi-square of 3.84 or more)
  • there are at least 20 reports on other compounds to compare with

A flag isn’t proof that the compound causes it; it’s a signal worth knowing.

Reactions Sig reads as serious, in a report, a question or a comment, are counted, moderators see them all, and their writers are pointed to medical help and to MedWatch, where anyone in the US can report a serious reaction to the FDA.

What we read on the forums

Each Signal starts from Miracle Health’s reading of public forums, podcasts and videos: what people describe most, and how often they mention each use. We show counted forum data (such as “412 posts read, 61% positive”) only from sources whose terms allow counting and showing it; Reddit, for one, doesn’t allow commercial use of its data without an agreement, so none is counted from there. We never quote or name anyone.

How Sig uses all this

For each compound and question, Sig starts with the studies when there are studies in people for it, and with what people report when there aren’t, saying so in the same breath. Safety comes before either: for anything on FDA’s list of substances with significant safety risks, the status and the studies lead; in pregnancy, what people report is left out.

The numbers it gives come from these same files: the fact sheets and the community’s hourly snapshot, worked into one reading per compound and use that the fact sheets show too. A check flags any number in an answer that isn’t in what Sig was given, and every part of every answer is checked by a Safety agent before you see it.

A test set of about two hundred questions, with what a good answer must and must not do, is graded automatically each time Sig changes.

What we never do

  • No doses, amounts, schedules or how to take or inject anything, for any compound.
  • No sellers, brands, prices, pharmacies or where to get something or have it tested.
  • No diagnosis, and never start, stop or change a prescribed medicine: that’s for your doctor.
  • No health records: Sig keeps nothing you write, and the community holds only what members choose to post.
