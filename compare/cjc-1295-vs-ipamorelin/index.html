Side by side

CJC-1295 with DAC vs Ipamorelin

From each fact sheet · checked Oct 28, 2026

What each one is, its status, what the studies show, its risks and what people who took it report, from their fact sheets, each claim checked against its source. It isn’t a recommendation: which one fits, if either, is for you and your doctor.

CJC-1295 with DACAlso called CJC-1295 DAC, CJC DAC, DAC:GRF
IpamorelinAlso called Ipa, Ipam

What it is

CJC-1295 with DAC

A long-acting, lab-made version of the hormone that triggers the release of growth hormone. Taken to raise growth hormone and IGF-1. The data in people come from short studies in healthy adults.

Ipamorelin

A small lab-made peptide taken to raise growth hormone, acting on the ghrelin receptor. Its only treatment trial, for bowel recovery after surgery, missed its main goal.

Status in the US

CJC-1295 with DAC

Not approved Not approved; FDA advisers voted against compounding.

Ipamorelin

FDA safety-risk list Not approved; on FDA's safety-risk list (503B).

Evidence overall

CJC-1295 with DAC

Limited evidence

Two small 2006 studies in healthy adults showed higher growth hormone and IGF-1 for days after an injection. FDA found no data on whether it works for any health outcome.

Ipamorelin

Limited evidence

The human data are one pharmacology study in 48 healthy men and one surgery trial in 117 patients that missed its main goal. There are no human studies of the under-the-skin use that is common today.

CJC-1295 with DAC

Ipamorelin

Hormones

What it may do

CJC-1295 with DAC
  • Limited evidenceRaised growth hormone and IGF-1 above baseline for days after a single injection in healthy adults
  • Limited evidenceRaised baseline growth hormone between pulses (not peak levels) and lifted IGF-1 about 40% a week after one dose in healthy adults
Ipamorelin
  • Limited evidenceRaised growth hormone in proportion to blood levels in a placebo-controlled study of 48 healthy men; levels fell back to very low within 6 hours
  • Limited evidencePossibly faster return of bowel function after open abdominal surgery, seen only in a subgroup; the trial's main result (time to first tolerated meal) was not significant

Risks and side effects

CJC-1295 with DAC
  • CommonSide effects in 94% of volunteers after one injection in the first human study, mostly injection-site reactions (about 70%)
  • Not knownFlushing and body-wide vasodilatory reactions, headache, nausea, dizziness and low blood pressure after repeat injections; increased heart rate in a second study
  • RareOne death from a heart attack was reported in a halted, unpublished HIV trial; the treating physician blamed existing heart disease, and causality was not established
  • Not knownAnimal studies found lower hemoglobin, higher cholesterol, injection-site tissue damage and DNA damage in pituitary cells; no cancer studies exist
Ipamorelin
  • Not knownIn the 117-patient surgery trial, two deaths and serious complications occurred after treatment in the ipamorelin group; FDA notes causality was not reported
  • Not knownMore low potassium, trouble sleeping and high blood sugar at discharge than with placebo in the surgery trial
  • Not knownNo human data for injection under the skin, and no animal toxicity, cancer or reproductive studies, so FDA says it cannot rule out safety concerns
  • Not knownPossible immune reactions from aggregation and impurities; FDA found ipamorelin acetate 'not well characterized'

Skip it, or ask a doctor first, if

CJC-1295 with DAC
  • Active cancer or a history of pituitary tumors
  • Heart disease or heart rhythm problems
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no full reproductive studies)
  • Diabetes or prediabetes (clinician first)
Ipamorelin
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (no reproductive studies)
  • Active cancer
  • Diabetes or prediabetes (clinician first: higher blood sugar was seen in a trial)
  • Competitive athletes (prohibited by WADA at all times)

Interactions

CJC-1295 with DAC
  • None well documented
Ipamorelin
  • None well documented
