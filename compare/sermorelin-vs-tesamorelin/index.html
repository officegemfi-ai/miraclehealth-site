Side by side

Sermorelin vs Tesamorelin.

From each fact sheet · checked Oct 28, 2026

What each one is, its status, what the studies show, its risks and what people who took it report, from their fact sheets, each claim checked against its source. It isn’t a recommendation: which one fits, if either, is for you and your doctor.

SermorelinAlso called GRF 1-29, Geref
TesamorelinAlso called Egrifta, Tesa

What it is

Sermorelin

A shortened copy of the brain hormone that tells the pituitary gland to release growth hormone. Once an approved drug. Now made by compounding pharmacies and used to raise growth hormone and IGF-1.

Tesamorelin

A lab-made version of the brain hormone that triggers the release of growth hormone. FDA-approved to shrink excess deep belly fat in adults with HIV.

Status in the US

Sermorelin

Compounded by prescription Compounded by prescription; former approved drug.

Tesamorelin

FDA-approved FDA-approved (Egrifta) for HIV-related belly fat.

Evidence overall

Sermorelin

Limited evidence

It was an approved drug for children with growth hormone deficiency. For the adult uses people seek today (body composition, aging), the evidence found here is limited to a small retrospective study and reviews.

Tesamorelin

Strong evidence

Two randomized, placebo-controlled trials in 816 adults with HIV supported its approval for reducing visceral fat. Other uses (fatty liver, thinking skills) rest on single smaller trials.

Mostly taken for

Sermorelin

Hormones

Tesamorelin

Weight

What it may do

Sermorelin
  • Strong evidenceTriggers growth hormone release from the pituitary; FDA approved it as a test of the pituitary's ability to make growth hormone
  • Moderate evidenceTreated growth failure in children with idiopathic growth hormone deficiency (its approved use, 1997-2009), though a 2012 review called it largely ineffective as a growth promoter
  • Limited evidenceRaised IGF-1 in 14 men on testosterone therapy who took it together with GHRP-2 and GHRP-6
Tesamorelin
  • Strong evidenceCut visceral (deep belly) fat by roughly 15% over 26 weeks versus placebo in adults with HIV and excess abdominal fat; the fat came back in people who stopped
  • Moderate evidenceLowered triglycerides and improved the cholesterol ratio in adults with HIV and excess abdominal fat
  • Moderate evidenceReduced liver fat over 12 months in 61 adults with HIV and fatty liver disease
  • Moderate evidenceImproved executive function (planning and attention) over 20 weeks in 152 older adults with or without mild cognitive impairment

Risks and side effects

Sermorelin
  • Not knownCompounded versions are not FDA-approved, and FDA does not check their safety, quality or strength before sale
  • In theoryFluid retention, joint pain, injection-site reactions and higher blood sugar are label warnings for tesamorelin, a drug that works the same way
  • Not knownLong-term safety, including any cancer risk from raising growth hormone and IGF-1, has not been studied
Tesamorelin
  • CommonInjection-site reactions such as redness, itching or pain (17% vs 6% on placebo)
  • CommonFluid-retention effects: joint pain, swelling of the limbs, tingling or numbness, and occasionally carpal tunnel syndrome
  • CommonVery high IGF-1: about a third had IGF-1 more than 3 standard deviations above normal at 26 weeks; long-term effects unknown
  • UncommonHigher blood sugar: 5% vs 1% reached a diabetes-range HbA1c at 26 weeks

Skip it, or ask a doctor first, if

Sermorelin
  • Active cancer
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Diabetes or prediabetes (doctor first: drugs that raise growth hormone can raise blood sugar)
  • Competitive athletes (prohibited by WADA at all times)
Tesamorelin
  • Pregnancy (contraindicated)
  • Active cancer
  • Damage to the hypothalamic-pituitary axis (pituitary tumor or surgery, head radiation, major head trauma)
  • Known allergy to tesamorelin or its ingredients

Interactions

Sermorelin
  • Glucocorticoid replacement (e.g., for adrenal insufficiency): raising growth hormone can increase steroid needs, per the tesamorelin label
  • Drugs cleared by liver CYP450 enzymes (e.g., anticonvulsants, cyclosporine, sex steroids): growth hormone can change their clearance, per the tesamorelin label
Tesamorelin
  • Glucocorticoid replacement for adrenal insufficiency: higher maintenance or stress doses may be needed
  • Drugs cleared by liver CYP450 enzymes (e.g., corticosteroids, sex steroids, anticonvulsants, cyclosporine): the label advises monitoring when combined
