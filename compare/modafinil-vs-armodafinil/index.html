Side by side

Modafinil vs Armodafinil.

From each fact sheet · checked Oct 28, 2026

What each one is, its status, what the studies show, its risks and what people who took it report, from their fact sheets, each claim checked against its source. It isn’t a recommendation: which one fits, if either, is for you and your doctor.

ModafinilAlso called Provigil
ArmodafinilAlso called Nuvigil

What it is

Modafinil

A prescription drug that keeps people awake, for narcolepsy, sleep apnea and shift-work sleepiness. Also taken for focus, which it isn't approved for. In rested, healthy people its effects are small.

Armodafinil

One of the two mirror-image forms of modafinil, a prescription drug that keeps people with sleep disorders awake. Also taken for focus, an unapproved use with little direct evidence in healthy people.

Status in the US

Modafinil

FDA-approved FDA-approved for sleep disorders; Schedule IV.

Armodafinil

FDA-approved FDA-approved for sleep disorders; Schedule IV.

Evidence overall

Modafinil

Moderate evidence

It is approved for sleepiness from three sleep disorders. For focus in rested, healthy people, meta-analyses of small placebo-controlled trials found only small effects, on a few kinds of thinking tests.

Armodafinil

Limited evidence

It is approved for sleepiness from three sleep disorders. For focus in rested, healthy people, the evidence comes from studies of modafinil. Those found only small effects, on a few kinds of thinking tests.

Mostly taken for

Modafinil

Focus & mood

Armodafinil

Focus & mood

What it may do

Modafinil
  • Strong evidenceImproves wakefulness in adults with excessive sleepiness from narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea or shift work disorder (approved use)
  • Moderate evidenceA small overall gain on thinking tests in adults who were not sleep-deprived (average effect size 0.10 across 19 placebo-controlled trials with 767 people), mainly in executive function and processing speed; attention and memory did not improve
  • Moderate evidenceA separate set of meta-analyses of 14 studies in healthy rested adults, using different methods, found a clear gain only in memory updating (a working-memory task), and the authors said the evidence does not support users' belief that it is an effective cognitive enhancer
Armodafinil
  • Strong evidenceImproves wakefulness in adults with excessive sleepiness from obstructive sleep apnea, narcolepsy or shift work disorder (approved use)
  • Limited evidenceEvidence for focus in rested people comes from modafinil, its parent mixture: across 19 placebo-controlled trials the overall effect on thinking tests was small, and another set of meta-analyses found a clear gain only in memory updating

Risks and side effects

Modafinil
  • CommonHeadache, the most common side effect, in about a third of patients versus about a quarter on placebo; nausea, nervousness, anxiety, insomnia and dizziness are among the other frequent ones
  • RareSerious skin reactions, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis, are rare but can be life-threatening; any new rash needs prompt medical review
  • Not knownAllergic reactions: swelling of the face and throat (angioedema), anaphylaxis and multi-organ reactions, including at least one death reported after approval
  • Not knownPsychiatric symptoms; the label urges caution in people with a history of psychosis, depression or mania
Armodafinil
  • CommonHeadache (17% versus 9% on placebo), nausea (7% versus 3%), dizziness (5% versus 2%) and insomnia (5% versus 1%) in trials
  • RareSerious rash, including Stevens-Johnson syndrome, has required hospital care; in children given modafinil, rash led to stopping in about 8 in 1,000; any new rash needs prompt medical review
  • Not knownAllergic and multi-organ reactions, including angioedema, anaphylaxis and DRESS (a drug reaction with rash, fever and organ involvement)
  • Not knownPsychiatric effects: anxiety, agitation and irritability led some trial patients to stop, and mania, delusions, hallucinations, suicidal thoughts and aggression have been reported

Skip it, or ask a doctor first, if

Modafinil
  • Pregnant or planning pregnancy (animal data suggest harm, and fetal growth restriction and miscarriage have been reported)
  • Allergy to modafinil or armodafinil
  • History of left ventricular hypertrophy or other heart disease (needs a doctor first)
  • History of psychosis, depression or mania (needs a doctor first)
Armodafinil
  • Pregnant or planning pregnancy (animal data suggest harm, and fetal growth restriction and miscarriage have been reported)
  • Allergy to armodafinil or modafinil
  • Heart disease, left ventricular hypertrophy, or mitral valve prolapse that caused symptoms on stimulants (needs a doctor first)
  • History of psychosis, depression or mania (needs a doctor first)

Interactions

Modafinil
  • Hormonal birth control: may fail during use and for one month after stopping; the label advises another or an additional method
  • Cyclosporine: blood levels may drop
  • Omeprazole, phenytoin, diazepam, propranolol, clomipramine and other drugs cleared by the CYP2C19 enzyme: levels may rise
  • Warfarin: more frequent INR checks may be needed
Armodafinil
  • Hormonal birth control: may be less effective during use and for one month after stopping
  • Cyclosporine: blood levels may drop
  • Drugs cleared by the liver enzyme CYP2C19: they may stay in the body longer
  • Drugs cleared by the liver enzymes CYP3A4/5: they may be cleared faster and work less well
