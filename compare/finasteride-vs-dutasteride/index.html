Side by side

Finasteride vs Dutasteride

From each fact sheet · checked Oct 28, 2026

What each one is, its status, what the studies show, its risks and what people who took it report, from their fact sheets, each claim checked against its source. It isn’t a recommendation: which one fits, if either, is for you and your doctor.

FinasterideAlso called Propecia, Proscar
DutasterideAlso called Avodart

What it is

Finasteride

A prescription pill that stops testosterone turning into the hormone DHT. Approved for male pattern hair loss and an enlarged prostate. Sexual and mood side effects are a known concern.

Dutasteride

A prescription capsule that stops the body making the hormone DHT. Approved in the US for an enlarged prostate. Also used for male pattern hair loss, which it isn't approved for.

Status in the US

Finasteride

FDA-approved FDA-approved for hair loss and enlarged prostate.

Dutasteride

FDA-approved FDA-approved for enlarged prostate; off-label for hair.

Evidence overall

Finasteride

Strong evidence

It is FDA-approved for male pattern hair loss and an enlarged prostate, based on large randomized trials. The hair-loss trials had 1,879 men, and each of two prostate trials had about 3,000 men.

Dutasteride

Strong evidence

It is FDA-approved for an enlarged prostate that causes symptoms. For hair loss (off-label in the US), a meta-analysis of 3 short randomized trials in 576 men found more hair growth than with finasteride.

Mostly taken for

Finasteride

Skin & hair

Dutasteride

Skin & hair

What it may do

Finasteride
  • Strong evidenceGrew hair in men aged 18 to 41 with mild to moderate male pattern hair loss: on photographs, 48% improved at one year versus 7% on placebo, and 66% versus 7% at two years; gains reversed within 12 months of stopping
  • Strong evidenceIn men with an enlarged prostate, cut BPH surgery from 10.1% to 4.6% and acute urinary retention from 6.6% to 2.8% over 4 years
  • Strong evidenceCombined with doxazosin, lowered the risk of BPH getting worse by 67% versus placebo (3,047 men)
Dutasteride
  • Strong evidenceImproves symptoms of an enlarged prostate and lowers the risk of acute urinary retention and BPH surgery
  • Moderate evidenceIncreased hair counts more than finasteride, with better photo ratings, in men with male pattern hair loss (3 randomized trials, 576 men, 24 weeks; off-label)

Risks and side effects

Finasteride
  • UncommonLower libido, erection problems and ejaculation problems: each in about 1% to 2% of men in the hair-loss trials (versus about 1% on placebo), and more often in older men treated for an enlarged prostate; guidelines say men should be told about these before starting
  • Not knownSexual problems that continue after stopping, and infertility or poor semen quality, reported after approval
  • Not knownDepression and suicidal thoughts: reported after approval; in 2025 EU regulators confirmed suicidal thoughts as a side effect of unknown frequency, mostly in men taking it for hair loss
  • UncommonMore high-grade prostate cancer: Gleason 8 to 10 cancer in 1.8% versus 1.1% on placebo over 7 years (18,882 men aged 55 and older)
Dutasteride
  • UncommonSexual side effects in the first 6 months: impotence (4.7% versus 1.7% on placebo), lower libido (3.0% versus 1.4%) and ejaculation problems (1.4% versus 0.5%); rates generally fell after the first year, some may persist after stopping, and guidelines say men should be told about them before starting
  • Not knownBreast enlargement or tenderness; reports after approval of male breast cancer, depressed mood, and testicular pain or swelling
  • UncommonMore high-grade prostate cancer: Gleason 8 to 10 cancer in 1.0% versus 0.5% on placebo in the REDUCE trial
  • Not knownLower semen volume (26%), total sperm count (23%) and sperm motility (18%) after a year; total sperm count had not recovered after 24 weeks of follow-up, and the effect on fertility is unknown

Skip it, or ask a doctor first, if

Finasteride
  • Women who are or may be pregnant: must not take it or handle crushed or broken tablets (risk to a male fetus)
  • Women and children (not approved for them)
  • History of depression or suicidal thoughts (discuss the mood risks first)
  • Past allergic reaction to finasteride
Dutasteride
  • Women who are or may be pregnant: must not take it or handle leaking capsules, since it is absorbed through the skin and can harm a male fetus
  • Women and children (not approved for them)
  • Past serious allergic reaction to dutasteride or another 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor
  • Blood donors: no donating until at least 6 months after the last dose

Interactions

Finasteride
  • None well documented (the label found no clinically important drug interactions)
Dutasteride
  • Strong, long-term CYP3A4 inhibitors such as ritonavir or ketoconazole: can raise dutasteride levels
