Side by side

Orforglipron vs Semaglutide

From each fact sheet · checked Oct 28, 2026

What each one is, its status, what the studies show, its risks and what people who took it report, from their fact sheets, each claim checked against its source. It isn’t a recommendation: which one fits, if either, is for you and your doctor.

OrforglipronAlso called LY3502970, Oral GLP-1
SemaglutideAlso called Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus

What it is

Orforglipron

A GLP-1 medicine you take as a pill, sold as Foundayo. Approved in April 2026 for weight management in adults with obesity, or with overweight and a related health problem.

Semaglutide

The prescription GLP-1 medicine sold as Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy. Used to lower blood sugar in type 2 diabetes and to lose weight.

Status in the US

Orforglipron

FDA-approved FDA-approved for weight management (April 2026).

Semaglutide

FDA-approved FDA-approved for diabetes, weight, heart, kidney, MASH.

Evidence overall

Orforglipron

Strong evidence

FDA approved it for weight management, based on two 72-week randomized trials in 4,740 adults, 1,613 of them with type 2 diabetes.

Semaglutide

Strong evidence

It has been tested in large randomized trials such as STEP 1 (1,961 adults) and SELECT (17,604 adults). FDA has approved it for each of its main uses.

Mostly taken for

Orforglipron

Weight

Semaglutide

Weight

What it may do

Orforglipron
  • Strong evidenceAverage weight loss of about 7% to 11% over 72 weeks, depending on dose, versus about 2% with placebo, in 3,127 adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes; 60% to 72% lost at least 5% of body weight versus 27%
  • Strong evidenceAverage weight loss of about 5% to 10% over 72 weeks versus 2.5% with placebo in 1,613 adults with type 2 diabetes and obesity or overweight
  • Moderate evidenceLower A1C in adults with type 2 diabetes: up to 2.2 points versus 1.4 with oral semaglutide over 52 weeks, and up to 1.7 versus 0.8 with dapagliflozin over 40 weeks (company-reported; not an approved use)
Semaglutide
  • Strong evidenceAbout 15% average body-weight loss over 68 weeks in adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes, versus about 2% with placebo
  • Strong evidenceLower blood sugar in adults with type 2 diabetes
  • Strong evidence20% fewer heart attacks, strokes and cardiovascular deaths over about 40 months in adults with heart disease and overweight or obesity but no diabetes
  • Strong evidenceLower risk of kidney function decline, kidney failure and cardiovascular death in adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease

Risks and side effects

Orforglipron
  • CommonNausea, constipation, diarrhea, vomiting and indigestion: nausea affected 26% to 35% versus 10% on placebo; severe digestive reactions occurred in about 3% versus 1%, and 8% stopped because of side effects versus 3%
  • UncommonFaster heart rate: an average rise of about 4 to 5 beats per minute, and a racing heartbeat (tachycardia) in 3% versus 0.9% on placebo
  • UncommonHair loss in 4% to 5% versus 2% on placebo
  • UncommonGallstones (1% versus 0.7% on placebo) and gallbladder inflammation (0.4% versus 0.3%)
Semaglutide
  • CommonNausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation and stomach pain; in STEP 1, 74% had digestive side effects versus 48% on placebo
  • CommonMost lost weight tends to come back after stopping: about two-thirds was regained within a year in STEP 1
  • UncommonGallstones and gallbladder inflammation (gallstones in 1.6% versus 0.7% on placebo)
  • RareAcute pancreatitis

Skip it, or ask a doctor first, if

Orforglipron
  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • Past serious allergic reaction to orforglipron
  • Pregnancy (the label says to stop when pregnancy is recognized) or breastfeeding (not recommended)
  • Severe gastroparesis (not recommended)
Semaglutide
  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • Past serious allergic reaction to semaglutide
  • Pregnancy, or planning a pregnancy within about 2 months
  • Severe gastroparesis (very slow stomach emptying)

Interactions

Orforglipron
  • Birth control pills: may work less well after starting and after each dose increase; the label advises a non-oral method or an added barrier method for a time
  • Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (such as ritonavir) or strong CYP3A4 inducers: can change orforglipron levels; the label limits or avoids some combinations
  • Simvastatin: orforglipron roughly doubles exposure to its active form, so the label caps the statin dose
  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
Semaglutide
  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Oral medicines with a narrow safety margin, such as levothyroxine: slower stomach emptying can change how much is absorbed
  • Other semaglutide products or other GLP-1 drugs: using them together is not recommended
