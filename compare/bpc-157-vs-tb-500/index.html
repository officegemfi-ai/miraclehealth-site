Side by side

BPC-157 vs TB-500

From each fact sheet · checked Oct 28, 2026

What each one is, its status, what the studies show, its risks and what people who took it report, from their fact sheets, each claim checked against its source. It isn’t a recommendation: which one fits, if either, is for you and your doctor.

BPC-157Also called Body Protection Compound, PL 14736, BPC
TB-500Also called Thymosin beta-4 fragment, TB4 fragment, TB 500

What it is

BPC-157

A small piece of a protein first found in human stomach juice. People take it to heal tendons, muscles and the gut. Most of the evidence comes from rodents.

TB-500

A lab-made piece of the protein thymosin beta-4, promoted for injury and wound recovery. FDA found no studies of it in people.

Status in the US

BPC-157

Under FDA review Not approved; compounding not yet allowed.

TB-500

Under FDA review Not approved; compounding not yet allowed.

Evidence overall

BPC-157

Limited evidence

Most data come from rodent studies; the handful of human studies are tiny, uncontrolled or published only as meeting abstracts, and FDA judged them insufficient to establish safety or effectiveness. The tendon, muscle and other tissue benefits come only from animal studies.

TB-500

Animal and lab studies

There are no human studies; the evidence is animal and lab work, mostly on the full thymosin beta-4 protein or related peptides, and a lab test of TB-500 itself found no wound-healing effect.

Mostly taken for

BPC-157

Recovery, Gut

TB-500

Recovery

What it may do

BPC-157
  • Limited evidenceIn one placebo-controlled trial in 53 people with mild to moderate ulcerative colitis, BPC-157 enemas lowered a disease activity score more than placebo; it was reported only as a meeting abstract, and FDA judged the data inadequate
  • Animal and lab studiesFaster healing of cut tendons in rodents
  • Animal and lab studiesHealing of surgically created colon fistulas and protection against stomach and intestinal damage from NSAID painkillers in rats
  • Animal and lab studiesProtection in rodent models of stroke, encephalopathy (brain dysfunction) and spinal cord injury
TB-500
  • Animal and lab studiesFaster skin-wound closure and more collagen in aged mice treated with thymosin beta-4 or a non-acetylated peptide with the same LKKTETQ sequence
  • Animal and lab studiesThe LKKTETQ sequence has been reported to stimulate new blood vessel growth in lab studies
  • Animal and lab studiesOne TB-500 breakdown product caused a small but significant wound closure in a lab-dish test, although TB-500 itself did not

Risks and side effects

BPC-157
  • Not knownToo little human safety information to describe its side effects: the human studies were too small to show them
  • In theoryImmune reactions, which FDA called a possibly significant risk that peptide clumping could make worse
  • In theoryUnknown cancer risk: no cancer studies exist, and it may promote new blood vessel growth
  • Not knownChanged blood-clotting tests in rats and dogs, plus higher liver enzyme (ALT), glucose and triglyceride levels in animal toxicity studies
TB-500
  • Not knownNo human safety data: FDA found no clinical studies, case reports or other human exposure data by any route
  • In theoryImmune reactions to the injected peptide, possibly worsened by clumping and impurities
  • Not knownNo animal toxicology or cancer studies to judge longer-term safety
  • Not knownA report to FDA of skin and gum darkening, which returned on re-use, after injections of a product blending TB-500 with BPC-157; cause not established

Skip it, or ask a doctor first, if

BPC-157
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (not studied in people)
  • Current or past cancer, unless a clinician advises otherwise (possible blood vessel growth effects; no cancer studies)
  • Bleeding or clotting disorders, or taking blood thinners (clotting tests changed in animal studies)
  • Athletes subject to anti-doping rules (BPC-157 is banned by WADA)
TB-500
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (not studied in people)
  • Current or past cancer, unless a clinician advises otherwise (linked to blood vessel growth in lab work; no cancer studies)
  • Athletes subject to anti-doping rules (TB-500 is banned by WADA)

Interactions

BPC-157
  • None well documented
TB-500
  • None well documented
