Semaglutide vs Liraglutide

From each fact sheet · checked Oct 28, 2026

What each one is, its status, what the studies show, its risks and what people who took it report, from their fact sheets, each claim checked against its source. It isn’t a recommendation: which one fits, if either, is for you and your doctor.

SemaglutideAlso called Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus
LiraglutideAlso called Victoza, Saxenda, Lira

What it is

Semaglutide

The prescription GLP-1 medicine sold as Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy. Used to lower blood sugar in type 2 diabetes and to lose weight.

Liraglutide

A prescription GLP-1 medicine that lowers blood sugar and appetite. Sold as Victoza for type 2 diabetes and as Saxenda for weight management.

Status in the US

Semaglutide

FDA-approved FDA-approved for diabetes, weight, heart, kidney, MASH.

Liraglutide

FDA-approved FDA-approved for diabetes, heart risk and weight.

Evidence overall

Semaglutide

Strong evidence

Large randomized trials such as STEP 1 (1,961 adults) and SELECT (17,604 adults), plus FDA approval for each of its main uses.

Liraglutide

Strong evidence

Large randomized trials, including SCALE (3,731 adults) for weight and LEADER (9,340 adults) for heart outcomes, plus FDA approval for each main use.

Mostly taken for

Semaglutide

Weight

Liraglutide

Weight

What it may do

Semaglutide
  • Strong evidenceAbout 15% average body-weight loss over 68 weeks in adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes, versus about 2% with placebo
  • Strong evidenceLower blood sugar in adults with type 2 diabetes
  • Strong evidence20% fewer heart attacks, strokes and cardiovascular deaths over about 40 months in adults with heart disease and overweight or obesity but no diabetes
  • Strong evidenceLower risk of kidney function decline, kidney failure and cardiovascular death in adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease
Liraglutide
  • Strong evidenceLower blood sugar in adults and children 10 and older with type 2 diabetes; A1C fell about 0.9 to 1.4 points more than placebo when added to other diabetes drugs
  • Strong evidenceAverage weight loss of 8.4 kg versus 2.8 kg with placebo over 56 weeks in 3,731 adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes; 63% lost at least 5% of body weight versus 27%
  • Strong evidenceFewer heart attacks, strokes and cardiovascular deaths in adults with type 2 diabetes at high heart risk, most with heart disease: 13.0% versus 14.9% over a median of 3.5 years (hazard ratio 0.87)
  • Strong evidenceApproved for weight management in children 12 and older with obesity

Risks and side effects

Semaglutide
  • CommonNausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation and stomach pain; in STEP 1, 74% had digestive side effects versus 48% on placebo
  • CommonMost lost weight tends to come back after stopping: about two-thirds was regained within a year in STEP 1
  • UncommonGallstones and gallbladder inflammation (gallstones in 1.6% versus 0.7% on placebo)
  • RareAcute pancreatitis
Liraglutide
  • CommonNausea (39% versus 14% on placebo), diarrhea, constipation and vomiting in weight-management trials
  • CommonFaster resting heart rate: on average 2 to 3 beats per minute higher, and 34% versus 19% on placebo had rises of more than 10 beats per minute at two visits in a row
  • UncommonGallstones (2.2% versus 0.8% on placebo) and gallbladder inflammation (0.8% versus 0.4%)
  • RareAcute pancreatitis (0.3% versus 0.1% on placebo)

Skip it, or ask a doctor first, if

Semaglutide
  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • Past serious allergic reaction to semaglutide
  • Pregnancy, or planning a pregnancy within about 2 months
  • Severe gastroparesis (very slow stomach emptying)
Liraglutide
  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • Past serious allergic reaction to liraglutide
  • Pregnant or planning pregnancy (discuss with a clinician first)

Interactions

Semaglutide
  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Oral medicines with a narrow safety margin, such as levothyroxine: slower stomach emptying can change how much is absorbed
  • Other semaglutide products or other GLP-1 drugs: using them together is not recommended
Liraglutide
  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Oral medicines: slower stomach emptying can change how they are absorbed
  • Other liraglutide products or other GLP-1 drugs: using them together is not recommended
