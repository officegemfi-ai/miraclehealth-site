Tirzepatide vs Retatrutide

From each fact sheet · checked Oct 28, 2026

What each one is, its status, what the studies show, its risks and what people who took it report, from their fact sheets, each claim checked against its source. It isn’t a recommendation: which one fits, if either, is for you and your clinician.

TirzepatideAlso called Mounjaro, Zepbound, Tirz
RetatrutideAlso called Reta, LY3437943, Triple agonist

What it is

Tirzepatide

A prescription injection, sold as Mounjaro and Zepbound, that activates two hormone receptors (GIP and GLP-1) to lower blood sugar and body weight.

Retatrutide

An experimental Eli Lilly drug that activates three hormone receptors (GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon); it is in late-stage obesity trials and is not FDA-approved.

Status in the US

Tirzepatide

FDA-approved FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes, weight and sleep apnea.

Retatrutide

In clinical trials Investigational; not approved, not legal to compound.

Evidence overall

Tirzepatide

Strong evidence

Large randomized trials such as SURMOUNT-1 (2,539 adults), plus FDA approval for type 2 diabetes, weight management and obstructive sleep apnea.

Retatrutide

Moderate evidence

One published phase 2 randomized trial (338 adults) plus large phase 3 trials whose results so far come only from Lilly's press release; long-term safety is not yet known.

Mostly taken for

Tirzepatide

Weight

Retatrutide

Weight

What it may do

Tirzepatide
  • Strong evidenceAbout 15% to 21% average body-weight loss over 72 weeks, depending on dose, in adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes, versus about 3% with placebo
  • Strong evidenceApproved as Mounjaro to improve blood sugar control in adults with type 2 diabetes; in adults with type 2 diabetes and excess weight, about 13% to 15% weight loss over 72 weeks versus about 3% with placebo
  • Strong evidenceFewer breathing pauses during sleep in adults with obesity and moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea: the apnea-hypopnea index fell by about 51% to 59% over 52 weeks, versus about 3% with placebo
Retatrutide
  • Moderate evidenceAverage weight loss of 21.6% to 22.6% at 80 weeks versus 3.2% with placebo in 1,949 adults with severe obesity and heart disease (phase 3 results reported by Lilly, not yet peer-reviewed)
  • Moderate evidenceAverage weight loss of up to 24.2% at 48 weeks versus 2.1% with placebo in a published phase 2 trial of 338 adults with obesity or overweight
  • Moderate evidenceLower blood sugar and weight in adults with type 2 diabetes and excess weight: A1C fell 1.4 to 1.6 points versus 0.2 with placebo, and weight fell up to 20.8% versus 4.0%, over 80 weeks (company-reported)

Risks and side effects

Tirzepatide
  • CommonNausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation and stomach pain; nausea affected about 1 in 4 people versus 8% on placebo, and severe digestive reactions about 2% to 3% versus 1%
  • CommonMost lost weight tends to return after stopping: people switched to placebo after 36 weeks of treatment regained about 14% of body weight over the next year
  • UncommonGallbladder problems: gallstones (1.1% versus 1% on placebo) and gallbladder inflammation (0.7% versus 0.2%)
  • RareAcute pancreatitis: a labeled warning, though rates matched placebo (0.2%) in the weight-loss trials
Retatrutide
  • CommonDiarrhea, nausea, constipation, less appetite and vomiting, more often at higher doses; in one phase 3 trial diarrhea affected 27% to 34% versus 13% on placebo, and up to 13.5% stopped because of side effects versus about 5% on placebo
  • UncommonUnpleasant or abnormal skin sensations (dysesthesia), mostly mild to moderate: 4.5% to 7.3% versus 0.7% on placebo
  • Not knownFaster resting heart rate, rising with dose and peaking around 24 weeks in the phase 2 trial before easing
  • Not knownLong-term safety is not known; the drug is still in trials and not approved

Avoid it, or check with a clinician first, if

Tirzepatide
  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2
  • Past serious allergic reaction to tirzepatide
  • Pregnancy (the label says to stop when pregnancy is recognized)
  • Severe gastroparesis (very slow stomach emptying)
Retatrutide
  • Anyone outside a clinical trial: it is not an approved medicine
  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2 (approved drugs in this class carry this as a contraindication)
  • Pregnant, breastfeeding or planning pregnancy

Interactions

Tirzepatide
  • Insulin or sulfonylureas: higher risk of low blood sugar
  • Birth control pills: can work less well after starting tirzepatide and after each dose increase; the label advises a non-oral method or adding a barrier method during those periods
  • Oral medicines with a narrow safety margin, such as warfarin: slower stomach emptying can change absorption, so levels need monitoring
  • Other tirzepatide products or any GLP-1 drug: using them together is not recommended
Retatrutide
  • Not yet studied. A related approved drug, tirzepatide, can raise the risk of low blood sugar with insulin or sulfonylureas, can change how oral medicines are absorbed, and can make birth-control pills less effective
