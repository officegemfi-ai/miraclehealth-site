Side by side

NAD+ vs NMN

From each fact sheet · checked Oct 28, 2026

What each one is, its status, what the studies show, its risks and what people who took it report, from their fact sheets, each claim checked against its source. It isn’t a recommendation: which one fits, if either, is for you and your doctor.

NAD+Also called NAD, Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, NAD+ injections
NMNAlso called Nicotinamide mononucleotide

What it is

NAD+

A molecule your cells use to make energy. Given by IV drip or injection, or taken by mouth, by people hoping for more energy and slower aging. The evidence in people is minimal.

NMN

A building block your body uses to make NAD+ (full name: nicotinamide mononucleotide). Taken in hopes of slowing aging and boosting metabolism. Benefits in people are unproven.

Status in the US

NAD+

Compounded by prescription Not FDA-approved; pharmacies may compound it.

NMN

Dietary supplement Dietary supplement since FDA's September 2025 reversal.

Evidence overall

NAD+

Limited evidence

The human data on NAD+ itself are a pilot infusion study of 11 men and a small clinic chart review with no health outcomes. Trials of oral precursors show higher NAD+ levels but unclear benefits.

NMN

Limited evidence

Eight small randomized trials in 342 mostly healthy adults, lasting up to 12 weeks, found that NMN raised blood NAD+ in most trials. It did not improve blood sugar, cholesterol, BMI or blood pressure.

Mostly taken for

NAD+

Energy, Aging

NMN

Aging

What it may do

NAD+
  • Limited evidenceRaised blood NAD+ during a 6-hour IV infusion in healthy men, but only after about 2 hours, because infused NAD+ was at first rapidly and completely removed from the blood (8 men on NAD+, 3 on saline)
  • Limited evidenceOral precursors such as NR and NMN can raise NAD+ in several human tissues and appear safe in trials so far, but whether this improves physical function is unclear
NMN
  • Limited evidenceRaised blood NAD+ (a lab marker, not a health effect) in 5 of 8 small randomized trials in adults; whether it also raises NAD+ inside cells and tissues is unclear
  • Limited evidenceNo benefit shown for fasting blood sugar, insulin, HbA1c, blood fats, BMI or blood pressure versus placebo across 8 trials of 14 days to 12 weeks in 342 adults
  • Limited evidenceBetter muscle insulin sensitivity in one small trial of women with prediabetes

Risks and side effects

NAD+
  • CommonDiscomfort during IV infusion: all 6 clinic clients given IV NAD+ in a small chart review had moderate to severe cramping, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, fast heart rate or chest pressure, which stopped when the infusion ended; a 2019 pilot in 8 men reported no adverse events
  • Not knownLittle safety information beyond the short term: human studies are small and brief
NMN
  • Not knownLong-term safety is uncertain, especially at higher amounts and in older adults; the longest trials lasted 12 weeks
  • Not knownNo serious side effects were reported in trials, but adverse events tended to be more frequent in the higher-dose groups of two trials
  • In theoryTheoretical concerns raised by reviewers: higher NAD+ might feed tumor growth or harmful signals from aging cells, and its by-product nicotinamide has a possible link with heart disease risk

Skip it, or ask a doctor first, if

NAD+
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (not studied)
  • Liver or kidney disease (excluded from the infusion study; ask a doctor)
NMN
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding (not studied)
  • Current or past cancer (doctor first, given a theoretical concern about tumor growth)

Interactions

NAD+
  • None well documented
NMN
  • None well documented
