Studied for muscle & body composition

Supplement · Muscle & performance Creatine monohydrate A compound the body makes and gets from animal foods, taken as a supplement to add strength and power for short, intense exercise. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Hormone · Growth-hormone axis Somatropin (HGH) Lab-made human growth hormone, prescribed for growth hormone deficiency and certain growth disorders; distributing it for anti-aging or bodybuilding is a federal crime. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Medication · Growth-hormone axis MK-677 An experimental oral drug that mimics the hormone ghrelin to raise growth hormone and IGF-1, taken for muscle, sleep and recovery; FDA bars compounding over heart-failure risk. Moderate evidenceFDA safety-risk list Supplement · Muscle & performance Turkesterone A plant steroid from Ajuga turkestanica, promoted for building muscle; the few small human studies found no effect, and products often contain less than labeled. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Muscle & performance Follistatin 344 A natural protein that blocks myostatin, a brake on muscle growth; taken to build muscle, it has been tested in people as a gene therapy for muscle diseases. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis IGF-1 DES A shortened, more potent form of IGF-1 missing its first three amino acids, sold as a research chemical for muscle growth; evidence is limited to lab studies. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis IGF-1 LR3 A lab-made, more potent form of IGF-1 that escapes the body's IGF-1 binding proteins, sold as a research chemical for muscle growth; evidence is limited to lab studies. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Muscle & performance MGF A lab-made peptide based on a variant of the IGF-1 gene, used for muscle growth and banned in sport; its role in the body is disputed. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Muscle & performance PEG-MGF A lab-made, pegylated version of a short peptide from the IGF-1 gene, promoted for muscle growth and repair but never studied in people. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Hormone · Hormone balance Testosterone A male sex hormone (androgen) prescribed to men with low testosterone from testicular, pituitary or hypothalamic problems; a Schedule III controlled substance in the US. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis CJC-1295 with DAC A long-acting, lab-made version of the hormone that triggers growth hormone release, taken to raise growth hormone and IGF-1; human data come from short studies in healthy adults. Limited evidenceNot approved Supplement · Skin & hair Collagen peptides Collagen broken down into small protein fragments, taken by mouth mainly to improve skin hydration, elasticity and wrinkles, and sometimes for joint symptoms. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Energy & mitochondria CoQ10 A substance the body makes that helps cells produce energy, taken for heart health, statin-related muscle aches, migraine and energy. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Hormone · Hormone balance DHEA A hormone made by the adrenal glands that the body converts to testosterone and estrogen; sold as a supplement for libido, well-being and aging, with weak evidence. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Growth-hormone axis GHRP-2 A lab-made peptide that releases growth hormone and increases appetite; used in Japan as a diagnostic test and sold elsewhere as a research chemical. Limited evidenceFDA safety-risk list Peptide · Growth-hormone axis GHRP-6 An early lab-made peptide that releases growth hormone through the ghrelin receptor, sold as a research chemical to raise growth hormone; human studies are small and short. Limited evidenceFDA safety-risk list Peptide · Growth-hormone axis Hexarelin A lab-made relative of GHRP-6 that releases growth hormone through the ghrelin receptor, sold as a research chemical; human data come from a few small studies. Limited evidenceNot approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis Ipamorelin A small lab-made peptide taken to raise growth hormone through the ghrelin receptor; its only treatment trial, for bowel recovery after surgery, missed its main goal. Limited evidenceFDA safety-risk list Supplement · Longevity NMN Nicotinamide mononucleotide, a building block the body uses to make NAD+, taken in hopes of slowing aging and boosting metabolism; human benefits are unproven. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Longevity NR (nicotinamide riboside) A form of vitamin B3 that raises NAD+, a molecule cells use for energy, taken in hopes of healthier aging; trials show few clear health effects. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Medication · Longevity Rapamycin An immune-suppressing transplant drug (sirolimus) that extends lifespan in mice and is being studied in people as a possible way to slow aging. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Mood & stress Rhodiola rosea A cold-climate herb (golden root) taken to fight fatigue and stress and lift mood; trials are small and conflicting, and NIH says the evidence is insufficient. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Growth-hormone axis Sermorelin A shortened copy of the brain hormone that tells the pituitary to release growth hormone; once an approved drug, now compounded and used to raise growth hormone and IGF-1. Limited evidenceCompounded by prescription Peptide · Longevity SS-31 A lab-made peptide that targets mitochondria, FDA-approved in 2025 as Forzinity for Barth syndrome and taken off-label for aging and energy based mostly on animal studies. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Hormone balance Tongkat ali Root extract of the Southeast Asian shrubby tree Eurycoma longifolia, long used as an aphrodisiac and taken mainly to raise testosterone; evidence comes from a few small trials. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Longevity MOTS-c A small peptide encoded in mitochondrial DNA, taken in hopes of improving metabolism, exercise capacity and aging; its effects have only been tested in rodents and cells. Animal and lab studiesUnder FDA review Medication · Metabolic & weight SLU-PP-332 An experimental compound that switches on estrogen-related receptors (ERRs) and mimics some effects of exercise in mice; no human studies have been published.
