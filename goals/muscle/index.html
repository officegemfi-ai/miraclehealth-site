What the studies show for each compound people take for muscle & body composition, strongest evidence first, next to its Community Signal: what people who took it report. It isn’t a ranking of what to take: the evidence grade is for the compound as a whole, and each fact sheet says what it was studied for.

Studied for muscle & body composition

Supplement · Muscle & performance Creatine monohydrate A compound your body makes and also gets from animal foods. Taken as a supplement to add strength and power for short, intense exercise. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Hormone · Growth-hormone axis Somatropin (HGH) Lab-made human growth hormone. Prescribed for growth hormone deficiency and certain growth disorders. Distributing it for anti-aging or bodybuilding is a federal crime. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Medication · Growth-hormone axis MK-677 An experimental drug taken by mouth that acts like the hormone ghrelin to raise growth hormone and IGF-1. Taken for muscle, sleep and recovery. FDA bars pharmacies from making it over a heart-failure risk. Moderate evidenceFDA safety-risk list Supplement · Muscle & performance Turkesterone A plant steroid from Ajuga turkestanica, promoted for building muscle. The few small studies in people found no effect, and products often contain less than the label says. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Muscle & performance Follistatin 344 A natural protein that blocks myostatin, a brake on muscle growth. People take it to build muscle. In people, it has been tested as a gene therapy for muscle diseases. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis IGF-1 DES A shortened, stronger form of IGF-1, missing its first three amino acids. Sold as a 'research chemical' for muscle growth. The evidence is limited to lab studies. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis IGF-1 LR3 A lab-made, stronger form of IGF-1 that escapes the proteins that normally hold IGF-1 in check. Sold as a 'research chemical' for muscle growth. The evidence is limited to lab studies. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Muscle & performance MGF A lab-made peptide based on a variant of the IGF-1 gene. Used for muscle growth, and banned in sport. Its role in the body is disputed. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Muscle & performance PEG-MGF A lab-made version of a short peptide from the IGF-1 gene, with a PEG chain attached. Promoted for muscle growth and repair. Never studied in people. Animal and lab studiesUnder FDA review

Sometimes taken for it

Studied mainly for something else, with some evidence or interest for muscle & body composition.

Supplement · Vitamins & minerals Iron An essential mineral the body needs to make hemoglobin. Taken to prevent or treat iron deficiency and the anemia and tiredness it can cause. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Hormone · Hormone balance Testosterone A male sex hormone. Prescribed to men whose testosterone is low because of a problem with the testicles, the pituitary gland or the hypothalamus. A Schedule III controlled substance in the US. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis CJC-1295 with DAC A long-acting, lab-made version of the hormone that triggers the release of growth hormone. Taken to raise growth hormone and IGF-1. The data in people come from short studies in healthy adults. Limited evidenceNot approved Supplement · Skin & hair Collagen peptides Collagen broken down into small pieces of protein. Taken by mouth, mainly for skin hydration, elasticity and wrinkles, and sometimes for joint symptoms. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Energy & mitochondria CoQ10 A substance your body makes that helps cells produce energy. Taken for heart health, muscle aches from statins, migraine and energy. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Hormone · Hormone balance DHEA A hormone your adrenal glands make, which the body turns into testosterone and estrogen. Sold as a supplement for libido, well-being and aging. The evidence is weak. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Growth-hormone axis GHRP-2 A lab-made peptide that makes the body release growth hormone and raises appetite. Used in Japan as a diagnostic test, and sold elsewhere as a 'research chemical'. Limited evidenceFDA safety-risk list Peptide · Growth-hormone axis GHRP-6 An early lab-made peptide that makes the body release growth hormone by acting on the ghrelin receptor. Sold as a 'research chemical'. Studies in people are small and short. Limited evidenceFDA safety-risk list Peptide · Growth-hormone axis Hexarelin A lab-made relative of GHRP-6 that makes the body release growth hormone by acting on the ghrelin receptor. Sold as a 'research chemical'. The data in people come from a few small studies. Limited evidenceNot approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis Ipamorelin A small lab-made peptide taken to raise growth hormone, acting on the ghrelin receptor. Its only treatment trial, for bowel recovery after surgery, missed its main goal. Limited evidenceFDA safety-risk list Supplement · Longevity NMN A building block your body uses to make NAD+ (full name: nicotinamide mononucleotide). Taken in hopes of slowing aging and boosting metabolism. Benefits in people are unproven. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Longevity NR (nicotinamide riboside) A form of vitamin B3 that raises NAD+, a molecule cells use for energy. Taken in hopes of healthier aging. Trials show few clear health effects. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Medication · Longevity Rapamycin A transplant drug (sirolimus) that holds back the immune system. It extends lifespan in mice, and is being studied in people as a possible way to slow aging. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Mood & stress Rhodiola rosea A cold-climate herb (golden root) taken to fight fatigue and stress and to lift mood. Trials are small and conflicting, and NIH says there isn't enough evidence. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Growth-hormone axis Sermorelin A shortened copy of the brain hormone that tells the pituitary gland to release growth hormone. Once an approved drug. Now made by compounding pharmacies and used to raise growth hormone and IGF-1. Limited evidenceCompounded by prescription Peptide · Longevity SS-31 A lab-made peptide that targets mitochondria, where cells make energy. FDA-approved in 2025 as Forzinity, for Barth syndrome only. People also take it for aging and energy, based mostly on animal studies. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Hormone balance Tongkat ali Root extract of a Southeast Asian tree (Eurycoma longifolia), long used as an aphrodisiac and taken mainly to raise testosterone. The evidence comes from a few small trials. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Longevity MOTS-c A small peptide coded for in the DNA of mitochondria. People take it hoping to improve metabolism, exercise capacity and aging. Its effects have only been tested in rodents and cells. Animal and lab studiesUnder FDA review Medication · Metabolic & weight SLU-PP-332 An experimental compound that copies some effects of exercise in mice by switching on estrogen-related receptors (ERRs). No studies in people have been published. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis CJC-1295 no DAC (Mod GRF 1-29) A modified copy of the hormone that triggers the release of growth hormone. Sold as a 'research chemical' to raise growth hormone. The only evidence comes from related peptides. AnecdotalNot approved
