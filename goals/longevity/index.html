Goals

Healthy aging.

14 compounds studied for it · 3 with strong or moderate evidence

What the studies show for each compound people take for healthy aging, strongest evidence first, next to its Community Signal: what people who took it report. It isn’t a ranking of what to take: the evidence grade is for the compound as a whole, and each fact sheet says what it was studied for.

Studied for healthy aging

Medication · Longevity Metformin A prescription pill that lowers blood sugar in type 2 diabetes. Some people take it hoping to slow aging. It isn't approved for that, and it's unproven. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Heart & circulation Omega-3 fish oil Oils from fish, krill or algae that supply the omega-3 fats EPA and DHA. Taken mainly for heart health and to lower triglycerides. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Vitamins & minerals Vitamin D3 The form of vitamin D your skin makes in sunlight. Taken to keep bones healthy and to correct or prevent low vitamin D levels. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Energy & mitochondria CoQ10 A substance your body makes that helps cells produce energy. Taken for heart health, muscle aches from statins, migraine and energy. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Energy & mitochondria NAD+ A molecule your cells use to make energy. Given by IV drip or injection, or taken by mouth, by people hoping for more energy and slower aging. The evidence in people is minimal. Limited evidenceCompounded by prescription Supplement · Longevity NMN A building block your body uses to make NAD+ (full name: nicotinamide mononucleotide). Taken in hopes of slowing aging and boosting metabolism. Benefits in people are unproven. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Longevity NR (nicotinamide riboside) A form of vitamin B3 that raises NAD+, a molecule cells use for energy. Taken in hopes of healthier aging. Trials show few clear health effects. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Medication · Longevity Rapamycin A transplant drug (sirolimus) that holds back the immune system. It extends lifespan in mice, and is being studied in people as a possible way to slow aging. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Longevity SS-31 A lab-made peptide that targets mitochondria, where cells make energy. FDA-approved in 2025 as Forzinity, for Barth syndrome only. People also take it for aging and energy, based mostly on animal studies. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Longevity Taurine An amino acid found in energy drinks and supplements, taken for energy and healthy aging. The lifespan gains seen in animals haven't been shown in people. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Medication · Heart & circulation Telmisartan A prescription drug for blood pressure and heart protection. In lab studies it also partly turns on a metabolic switch (PPAR-gamma). Benefits for healthy aging are unproven. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Longevity Epitalon A tiny lab-made peptide from Russian aging research. People take it hoping to slow aging and restore their melatonin rhythms. The evidence in people is very thin. Animal and lab studiesUnder FDA review Peptide · Longevity Humanin A small peptide coded for in the DNA of mitochondria. It protects cells in lab and animal studies, and levels fall with age. It has not been tested as a treatment in people. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Longevity MOTS-c A small peptide coded for in the DNA of mitochondria. People take it hoping to improve metabolism, exercise capacity and aging. Its effects have only been tested in rodents and cells. Animal and lab studiesUnder FDA review

Sometimes taken for it

Studied mainly for something else, with some evidence or interest for healthy aging.

Medication · Longevity Canagliflozin A prescription pill, sold as Invokana, that lowers blood sugar and heart and kidney risks in type 2 diabetes. The anti-aging interest rests on a mouse study. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Muscle & performance Creatine monohydrate A compound your body makes and also gets from animal foods. Taken as a supplement to add strength and power for short, intense exercise. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Medication · Metabolic & weight Dapagliflozin A prescription pill, sold as Farxiga, that makes the kidneys pass extra sugar out in the urine. It treats type 2 diabetes and lowers kidney and heart failure risks. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Metabolic & weight Dulaglutide A prescription GLP-1 injection, sold as Trulicity. It lowers blood sugar in type 2 diabetes and reduces the risk of heart attack and stroke. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Medication · Metabolic & weight Empagliflozin A prescription pill, sold as Jardiance, that makes the kidneys pass extra sugar out in the urine. It treats type 2 diabetes and lowers heart failure and kidney risks. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Hormone · Hormone balance Estradiol The body's strongest estrogen. Prescribed mainly to ease hot flashes and vaginal symptoms of menopause, and to prevent bone loss after it. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Metabolic & weight Liraglutide A prescription GLP-1 medicine that lowers blood sugar and appetite. Sold as Victoza for type 2 diabetes and as Saxenda for weight management. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Medication · Hormone balance Raloxifene A prescription pill (a SERM) that lowers the risk of spine fractures and of breast cancer in women after menopause. Its label says it is not recommended for men. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Metabolic & weight Semaglutide The prescription GLP-1 medicine sold as Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy. Used to lower blood sugar in type 2 diabetes and to lose weight. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Hormone · Growth-hormone axis Somatropin (HGH) Lab-made human growth hormone. Prescribed for growth hormone deficiency and certain growth disorders. Distributing it for anti-aging or bodybuilding is a federal crime. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Vitamins & minerals Vitamin B12 An essential vitamin for nerves, red blood cells and DNA. Taken to prevent or treat deficiency, which is more likely with age, vegan diets and some medicines. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Metabolic & weight Berberine A plant compound from goldenseal, barberry and Oregon grape. Taken mainly to lower blood sugar and for weight loss. Moderate evidenceDietary supplement Medication · Growth-hormone axis MK-677 An experimental drug taken by mouth that acts like the hormone ghrelin to raise growth hormone and IGF-1. Taken for muscle, sleep and recovery. FDA bars pharmacies from making it over a heart-failure risk. Moderate evidenceFDA safety-risk list Peptide · Growth-hormone axis CJC-1295 with DAC A long-acting, lab-made version of the hormone that triggers the release of growth hormone. Taken to raise growth hormone and IGF-1. The data in people come from short studies in healthy adults. Limited evidenceNot approved Supplement · Skin & hair Collagen peptides Collagen broken down into small pieces of protein. Taken by mouth, mainly for skin hydration, elasticity and wrinkles, and sometimes for joint symptoms. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Immune & inflammation Crystagen A tiny peptide from Russian 'bioregulator' research, promoted to support immunity in older people. The evidence is mainly small studies by its own developers. Limited evidenceNot approved Supplement · Immune & inflammation Curcumin The main yellow compound in turmeric, taken mostly for joint pain and inflammation. The evidence is still early, and turmeric supplements have been linked to rare liver injury. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Hormone · Hormone balance DHEA A hormone your adrenal glands make, which the body turns into testosterone and estrogen. Sold as a supplement for libido, well-being and aging. The evidence is weak. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Healing & recovery GHK-Cu A small peptide that binds copper, found naturally in human blood. Used in skin creams and injected for skin, hair and healing. That rests mostly on small cosmetic studies and animal studies. Limited evidenceUnder FDA review Peptide · Liver & detox Glutathione An antioxidant your body makes. Taken by mouth or by IV for liver health, detox and skin lightening. Studies in people are small, and IV use has caused serious reactions. Limited evidenceCompounded by prescription Supplement · Sleep Glycine An amino acid taken mainly to sleep better. The evidence is three small, very short trials in healthy adults. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Growth-hormone axis Ipamorelin A small lab-made peptide taken to raise growth hormone, acting on the ghrelin receptor. Its only treatment trial, for bowel recovery after surgery, missed its main goal. Limited evidenceFDA safety-risk list Supplement · Cognition & focus Lion’s mane An edible mushroom (Hericium erinaceus) taken for memory, focus and mood. Small trials in people show mixed results, and most of the evidence comes from lab and animal studies. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Medication · Energy & mitochondria Methylene blue A blue dye that is also an approved hospital drug for a rare blood disorder. Now promoted for focus and energy. The evidence in people for those uses is thin. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Liver & detox NAC (N-acetylcysteine) A modified form of the amino acid cysteine. Approved as an antidote for acetaminophen overdose and as a mucus thinner, and sold for decades as a dietary supplement. Limited evidenceRestricted Peptide · Metabolic & weight Pancragen A tiny peptide from Russian 'bioregulator' research, promoted for the pancreas and blood sugar. Tested in cells, monkeys and small studies in people. Limited evidenceNot approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis Sermorelin A shortened copy of the brain hormone that tells the pituitary gland to release growth hormone. Once an approved drug. Now made by compounding pharmacies and used to raise growth hormone and IGF-1. Limited evidenceCompounded by prescription Peptide · Hormone balance Testagen A Russian supplement peptide from Khavinson's 'bioregulator' research, promoted for male reproductive health and testosterone. The evidence in people is one small study by its developers. Limited evidenceNot approved Supplement · Vitamins & minerals Zinc An essential mineral the body needs for immune function, wound healing and hundreds of enzymes. Taken mostly to shorten colds and support immunity. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Immune & inflammation Bronchogen A tiny peptide from Russian 'bioregulator' research, promoted for the lungs and airways. So far the evidence is limited to studies in cells and rats. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Cognition & focus Dihexa A lab-made compound based on the peptide angiotensin IV, used to try to boost memory. It improved learning in rodents. FDA found no data on it in people. Animal and lab studiesUnder FDA review Peptide · Liver & detox Ovagen A short lab-made peptide from Russian 'bioregulator' research, promoted for the liver and digestive tract. Exactly what it is, and whether it helps people, are poorly documented. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Hormone balance Prostamax A short lab-made peptide from Russian 'bioregulator' research, promoted for prostate health. The published evidence is limited to lab studies on cells and DNA. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Medication · Metabolic & weight SLU-PP-332 An experimental compound that copies some effects of exercise in mice by switching on estrogen-related receptors (ERRs).
