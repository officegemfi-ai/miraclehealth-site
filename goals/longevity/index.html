Healthy aging

14 compounds studied for it · 3 with strong or moderate evidence

What the studies show for each compound people take for healthy aging, strongest evidence first, next to its Community Signal: what people who took it report. It isn’t a ranking of what to take: the evidence grade is for the compound as a whole, and each fact sheet says what it was studied for.

Studied for healthy aging

Medication · Longevity Metformin A prescription pill that lowers blood sugar in type 2 diabetes; some people take it off-label hoping to slow aging, which is unproven. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Heart & circulation Omega-3 fish oil Oils from fish, krill or algae that supply the omega-3 fats EPA and DHA, taken mainly for heart health and to lower triglycerides. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Vitamins & minerals Vitamin D3 The form of vitamin D the skin makes in sunlight, taken to keep bones healthy and to correct or prevent low vitamin D levels. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Energy & mitochondria CoQ10 A substance the body makes that helps cells produce energy, taken for heart health, statin-related muscle aches, migraine and energy. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Energy & mitochondria NAD+ A molecule cells use to make energy, given by IV drip or injection or taken by mouth by people hoping for more energy and slower aging; human evidence is minimal. Limited evidenceCompounded by prescription Supplement · Longevity NMN Nicotinamide mononucleotide, a building block the body uses to make NAD+, taken in hopes of slowing aging and boosting metabolism; human benefits are unproven. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Longevity NR (nicotinamide riboside) A form of vitamin B3 that raises NAD+, a molecule cells use for energy, taken in hopes of healthier aging; trials show few clear health effects. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Medication · Longevity Rapamycin An immune-suppressing transplant drug (sirolimus) that extends lifespan in mice and is being studied in people as a possible way to slow aging. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Longevity SS-31 A lab-made peptide that targets mitochondria, FDA-approved in 2025 as Forzinity for Barth syndrome and taken off-label for aging and energy based mostly on animal studies. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Longevity Taurine An amino acid found in energy drinks and supplements, taken for energy and healthy aging; lifespan gains seen in animals haven't been shown in people. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Medication · Heart & circulation Telmisartan A prescription blood pressure and heart-protection drug; in lab studies it also partly activates a metabolic switch (PPAR-gamma), but benefits for healthy aging are unproven. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Longevity Epitalon A lab-made four-amino-acid peptide from Russian aging research, taken in hopes of slowing aging and restoring melatonin rhythms; human evidence is very thin. Animal and lab studiesUnder FDA review Peptide · Longevity Humanin A small peptide encoded in mitochondrial DNA that protects cells in lab and animal studies; levels fall with age, but it is untested as a treatment in people. Animal and lab studiesNot approved Peptide · Longevity MOTS-c A small peptide encoded in mitochondrial DNA, taken in hopes of improving metabolism, exercise capacity and aging; its effects have only been tested in rodents and cells. Animal and lab studiesUnder FDA review

Sometimes taken for it
