Immune resilience

7 compounds studied for it · 1 with strong or moderate evidence

What the studies show for each compound people take for immune resilience, strongest evidence first, next to its Community Signal: what people who took it report. It isn’t a ranking of what to take: the evidence grade is for the compound as a whole, and each fact sheet says what it was studied for.

Studied for immune resilience

Supplement · Vitamins & minerals Vitamin D3 The form of vitamin D the skin makes in sunlight, taken to keep bones healthy and to correct or prevent low vitamin D levels. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Immune & inflammation Crystagen A three-amino-acid peptide (Glu-Asp-Pro) from Russian 'bioregulator' research, promoted to support immunity in older people; evidence is mainly the developers' own small studies. Limited evidenceNot approved Peptide · Immune & inflammation LL-37 The body's own germ-fighting and inflammation-steering peptide, made by immune and skin cells, taken for immune support and wound healing; human evidence is limited to leg-ulcer trials. Sometimes taken for it

Studied mainly for something else, with some evidence or interest for immune resilience.

Supplement · Gut & digestion Probiotics Live microorganisms such as Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium and Saccharomyces boulardii, taken mainly to prevent antibiotic-related diarrhea and ease gut symptoms. Moderate evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Immune & inflammation Curcumin The main yellow compound in turmeric, taken mostly for joint pain and inflammation; evidence is preliminary, and turmeric supplements have been linked to rare liver injury. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Liver & detox Glutathione An antioxidant the body makes, taken by mouth or IV for liver health, detox and skin lightening; human studies are small and IV use has caused serious reactions. Limited evidenceCompounded by prescription Supplement · Cognition & focus Lion’s mane An edible mushroom (Hericium erinaceus) taken for memory, focus and mood; small human trials show mixed results, and most evidence comes from lab and animal studies. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Liver & detox NAC (N-acetylcysteine) A modified form of the amino acid cysteine, approved as an antidote for acetaminophen overdose and as a mucus thinner, and sold for decades as a dietary supplement. Limited evidenceRestricted Medication · Longevity Rapamycin An immune-suppressing transplant drug (sirolimus) that extends lifespan in mice and is being studied in people as a possible way to slow aging. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Mood & stress Selank A lab-made seven-amino-acid peptide based on tuftsin, an immune protein fragment, taken for anxiety and focus; human evidence is one small Russian study.
