Immune resilience

7 compounds studied for it · 1 with strong or moderate evidence

What the studies show for each compound people take for immune resilience, strongest evidence first, next to its Community Signal: what people who took it report. It isn’t a ranking of what to take: the evidence grade is for the compound as a whole, and each fact sheet says what it was studied for.

Studied for immune resilience

Supplement · Vitamins & minerals Vitamin D3 The form of vitamin D your skin makes in sunlight. Taken to keep bones healthy and to correct or prevent low vitamin D levels. Strong evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Immune & inflammation Crystagen A tiny peptide from Russian 'bioregulator' research, promoted to support immunity in older people. The evidence is mainly small studies by its own developers. Limited evidenceNot approved Peptide · Immune & inflammation LL-37 A peptide your own immune and skin cells make to fight germs and steer inflammation. Taken for immune support and wound healing. The evidence in people is limited to leg-ulcer trials. Limited evidenceUnder FDA review Medication · Immune & inflammation Low-dose naltrexone (LDN) Naltrexone is an opioid-blocking drug for addiction. In much smaller amounts, people take it for chronic pain and inflammatory conditions, which it isn't approved for. Small trials have given mixed results. Limited evidenceCompounded by prescription Peptide · Immune & inflammation Thymosin alpha-1 A peptide that acts on the immune system. Sold abroad as Zadaxin, mainly for hepatitis B, and taken for immune support. FDA found there wasn't enough evidence for any use it reviewed. Limited evidenceNot approved Supplement · Vitamins & minerals Zinc An essential mineral the body needs for immune function, wound healing and hundreds of enzymes. Taken mostly to shorten colds and support immunity. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Immune & inflammation Bronchogen A tiny peptide from Russian 'bioregulator' research, promoted for the lungs and airways. So far the evidence is limited to studies in cells and rats. Animal and lab studiesNot approved

Sometimes taken for it

Studied mainly for something else, with some evidence or interest for immune resilience.

Supplement · Gut & digestion Probiotics Live microorganisms, such as Lactobacillus, Bifidobacterium and Saccharomyces boulardii. Taken mainly to prevent diarrhea from antibiotics and to ease gut symptoms. Moderate evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Immune & inflammation Curcumin The main yellow compound in turmeric, taken mostly for joint pain and inflammation. The evidence is still early, and turmeric supplements have been linked to rare liver injury. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Liver & detox Glutathione An antioxidant your body makes. Taken by mouth or by IV for liver health, detox and skin lightening. Studies in people are small, and IV use has caused serious reactions. Limited evidenceCompounded by prescription Supplement · Cognition & focus Lion’s mane An edible mushroom (Hericium erinaceus) taken for memory, focus and mood. Small trials in people show mixed results, and most of the evidence comes from lab and animal studies. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Liver & detox NAC (N-acetylcysteine) A modified form of the amino acid cysteine. Approved as an antidote for acetaminophen overdose and as a mucus thinner, and sold for decades as a dietary supplement. Limited evidenceRestricted Medication · Longevity Rapamycin A transplant drug (sirolimus) that holds back the immune system. It extends lifespan in mice, and is being studied in people as a possible way to slow aging. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Mood & stress Selank A small lab-made peptide based on tuftsin, a piece of an immune protein. Taken for anxiety and focus. The evidence in people is one small Russian study. Limited evidenceNot approved
