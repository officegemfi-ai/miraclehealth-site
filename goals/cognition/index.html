Studied for focus, mood & cognition

Medication · Cognition & focus Modafinil A prescription drug that keeps people awake, for narcolepsy, sleep apnea and shift-work sleepiness. Also taken for focus, which it isn't approved for. In rested, healthy people its effects are small. Moderate evidenceFDA-approved Medication · Cognition & focus Armodafinil One of the two mirror-image forms of modafinil, a prescription drug that keeps people with sleep disorders awake. Also taken for focus, an unapproved use with little direct evidence in healthy people. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Supplement · Mood & stress Ashwagandha An herb from Ayurvedic medicine (Withania somnifera). Extracts of its root are taken mainly to ease stress and anxiety and to sleep better. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Cognition & focus Cerebrolysin A mix of small peptides from pig brain. Prescribed abroad for stroke and dementia, and taken for focus and memory. Not approved in the US, and the evidence behind it is weak. Limited evidenceNot approved Supplement · Mood & stress L-theanine An amino acid from tea leaves. Taken to feel calm without getting drowsy, to ease stress, and to improve sleep quality and focus. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Cognition & focus Lion’s mane An edible mushroom (Hericium erinaceus) taken for memory, focus and mood. Small trials in people show mixed results, and most of the evidence comes from lab and animal studies. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Medication · Energy & mitochondria Methylene blue A blue dye that is also an approved hospital drug for a rare blood disorder. Now promoted for focus and energy. The evidence in people for those uses is thin. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Hormone · Mood & stress Oxytocin A hormone that makes the uterus contract. Approved as an injection to start or strengthen labor. Nasal sprays taken for mood or bonding have little evidence behind them. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Hormone · Hormone balance Pregnenolone A steroid the body turns into other hormones, including ones that act on the brain. Sold as a supplement, and studied in small trials for pain, schizophrenia and bipolar depression. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Mood & stress Rhodiola rosea A cold-climate herb (golden root) taken to fight fatigue and stress and to lift mood. Registered in Russia as nasal drops, and taken for focus, memory and stroke recovery. Would it have happened anyway?

Many people improve with time or on a placebo, whatever they take. In published trials:

  • 37%Mood & motivation At least 50% reduction in depression score (HAM-D or MADRS) from baseline Furukawa TA 2016, Walsh BT 2002
  • 39%Stress & anxiety Responder by each trial's definition, in priority order: at least 50% drop on the Hamilton Anxiety scale (HAM-A), otherwise CGI 'much' or 'very much improved' Rutherford BR 2015, Motta LS 2023

It’s context, not a direct comparison: in trials “improved” was measured on strict scales, while a member’s “better” is their own word. More on this.
