Sleep.

6 compounds studied for it · 1 with strong or moderate evidence

What the studies show for each compound people take for sleep, strongest evidence first, next to its Community Signal: what people who took it report. It isn’t a ranking of what to take: the evidence grade is for the compound as a whole, and each fact sheet says what it was studied for.

Studied for sleep

Supplement · Sleep Melatonin A hormone your brain makes in the dark to time sleep. Sold in the US as a supplement, mainly to fall asleep faster or ease jet lag. Moderate evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Mood & stress Ashwagandha An herb from Ayurvedic medicine (Withania somnifera). Extracts of its root are taken mainly to ease stress and anxiety and to sleep better. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Sleep DSIP A small peptide, also called emideltide, first found in rabbit brain blood. People take it hoping to sleep better. The evidence that it works is weak. Limited evidenceNot approved Supplement · Sleep Glycine An amino acid taken mainly to sleep better. The evidence is three small, very short trials in healthy adults. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Mood & stress L-theanine An amino acid from tea leaves. Taken to feel calm without getting drowsy, to ease stress, and to improve sleep quality and focus. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Vitamins & minerals Magnesium glycinate The mineral magnesium bound to the amino acid glycine. Taken mainly for sleep and relaxation, and to top up magnesium intake. Limited evidenceDietary supplement

Sometimes taken for it

Studied mainly for something else, with some evidence or interest for sleep.

Hormone · Hormone balance Estradiol The body's strongest estrogen. Prescribed mainly to ease hot flashes and vaginal symptoms of menopause, and to prevent bone loss after it. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Hormone · Hormone balance Progesterone (micronized) A prescription form of the natural hormone progesterone. Used mainly to protect the lining of the uterus in women taking estrogen after menopause, and to treat periods that have stopped. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Metabolic & weight Tirzepatide The prescription injection sold as Mounjaro and Zepbound. It acts on two hormone receptors (GIP and GLP-1) to lower blood sugar and body weight. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Medication · Growth-hormone axis MK-677 An experimental drug taken by mouth that acts like the hormone ghrelin to raise growth hormone and IGF-1. Taken for muscle, sleep and recovery. FDA bars pharmacies from making it over a heart-failure risk. Moderate evidenceFDA safety-risk list Medication · Cognition & focus Modafinil A prescription drug that keeps people awake, for narcolepsy, sleep apnea and shift-work sleepiness. Also taken for focus, which it isn't approved for. In rested, healthy people its effects are small. Moderate evidenceFDA-approved Medication · Cognition & focus Armodafinil One of the two mirror-image forms of modafinil, a prescription drug that keeps people with sleep disorders awake. Also taken for focus, an unapproved use with little direct evidence in healthy people. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis CJC-1295 with DAC A long-acting, lab-made version of the hormone that triggers the release of growth hormone. Taken to raise growth hormone and IGF-1. The data in people come from short studies in healthy adults. Limited evidenceNot approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis GHRP-6 An early lab-made peptide that makes the body release growth hormone by acting on the ghrelin receptor. Sold as a 'research chemical'. Studies in people are small and short. Limited evidenceFDA safety-risk list Peptide · Growth-hormone axis Ipamorelin A small lab-made peptide taken to raise growth hormone, acting on the ghrelin receptor. Its only treatment trial, for bowel recovery after surgery, missed its main goal. Limited evidenceFDA safety-risk list Supplement · Cognition & focus Lion’s mane An edible mushroom (Hericium erinaceus) taken for memory, focus and mood. Small trials in people show mixed results, and most of the evidence comes from lab and animal studies. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Growth-hormone axis Sermorelin A shortened copy of the brain hormone that tells the pituitary gland to release growth hormone. Once an approved drug. Now made by compounding pharmacies and used to raise growth hormone and IGF-1. Limited evidenceCompounded by prescription Peptide · Longevity Epitalon A tiny lab-made peptide from Russian aging research. People take it hoping to slow aging and restore their melatonin rhythms. The evidence in people is very thin. Animal and lab studiesUnder FDA review Peptide · Growth-hormone axis CJC-1295 no DAC (Mod GRF 1-29) A modified copy of the hormone that triggers the release of growth hormone. Sold as a 'research chemical' to raise growth hormone. The only evidence comes from related peptides. AnecdotalNot approved

Would it have happened anyway?

Many people improve with time or on a placebo, whatever they take. In published trials:

  • 42%Sleep Improvement of at least 6 points on the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) after 3 months on placebo. The low end is the same measure at 1 month. A second drug programme used a 7-point cut-off and found 30–41% across months 1, 3 and 6. Herring WJ 2016, Herring WJ 2019, Roth T 2022, Winkler A 2015

It’s context, not a direct comparison: in trials “improved” was measured on strict scales, while a member’s “better” is their own word. More on this.
