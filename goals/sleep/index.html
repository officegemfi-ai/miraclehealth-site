Sleep

6 compounds studied for it · 1 with strong or moderate evidence

What the studies show for each compound people take for sleep, strongest evidence first, next to its Community Signal: what people who took it report. It isn’t a ranking of what to take: the evidence grade is for the compound as a whole, and each fact sheet says what it was studied for.

Studied for sleep

Supplement · Sleep Melatonin A hormone the brain makes in the dark to time sleep, sold in the US as a supplement mainly to fall asleep faster or ease jet lag. Moderate evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Mood & stress Ashwagandha An Ayurvedic herb (Withania somnifera) whose root extracts are taken mainly to ease stress and anxiety and to improve sleep. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Peptide · Sleep DSIP A nine-amino-acid peptide, also called emideltide, first found in rabbit brain blood and taken in hopes of better sleep; evidence that it works is weak. Limited evidenceNot approved Supplement · Sleep Glycine An amino acid taken mainly to improve sleep; the evidence is three small, very short trials in healthy adults. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Mood & stress L-theanine An amino acid from tea leaves, taken to feel calm without drowsiness, ease stress, and improve sleep quality and focus. Limited evidenceDietary supplement Supplement · Vitamins & minerals Magnesium glycinate A form of the mineral magnesium bound to the amino acid glycine, taken mainly for sleep and relaxation and to top up magnesium intake. Limited evidenceDietary supplement

Sometimes taken for it

Studied mainly for something else, with some evidence or interest for sleep.

Hormone · Hormone balance Estradiol The body's most potent estrogen, prescribed mainly to relieve menopausal hot flashes and vaginal symptoms and to prevent bone loss after menopause. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Hormone · Hormone balance Progesterone (micronized) A prescription form of the natural hormone progesterone, used mainly to protect the uterine lining in postmenopausal women taking estrogen and to treat periods that have stopped. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Metabolic & weight Tirzepatide A prescription injection, sold as Mounjaro and Zepbound, that activates two hormone receptors (GIP and GLP-1) to lower blood sugar and body weight. Strong evidenceFDA-approved Medication · Growth-hormone axis MK-677 An experimental oral drug that mimics the hormone ghrelin to raise growth hormone and IGF-1, taken for muscle, sleep and recovery; FDA bars compounding over heart-failure risk. Moderate evidenceFDA safety-risk list Medication · Cognition & focus Modafinil A prescription wakefulness drug for narcolepsy, sleep apnea and shift work sleepiness, used off-label for focus; in rested healthy people its effects are small. Moderate evidenceFDA-approved Medication · Cognition & focus Armodafinil The R-form of modafinil: a prescription wakefulness drug for sleep disorders, used off-label for focus with little direct evidence in healthy people. Limited evidenceFDA-approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis CJC-1295 with DAC A long-acting, lab-made version of the hormone that triggers growth hormone release, taken to raise growth hormone and IGF-1; human data come from short studies in healthy adults. Limited evidenceNot approved Peptide · Growth-hormone axis GHRP-6 An early lab-made peptide that releases growth hormone through the ghrelin receptor, sold as a research chemical to raise growth hormone; human studies are small and short. Limited evidenceFDA safety-risk list Peptide · Growth-hormone axis Ipamorelin A small lab-made peptide taken to raise growth hormone through the ghrelin receptor; its only treatment trial, for bowel recovery after surgery, missed its main goal. Would it have happened anyway?

Many people improve with time or on a placebo, whatever they take. In published trials:

  • 42%Sleep Improvement of at least 6 points on the Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) after 3 months on placebo. The low end is the same measure at 1 month. A second drug programme used a 7-point cut-off and found 30–41% across months 1, 3 and 6. Herring WJ 2016, Herring WJ 2019, Roth T 2022, Winkler A 2015

It’s context, not a direct comparison: in trials “improved” was measured on strict scales, while a member’s “better” is their own word. More on this.
