Off FDA’s risk list isn’t the same as allowed

Nov 5, 2026

This is the first Signal, Miracle Health’s weekly email: what moved in the Community Signal, new studies, and FDA news on the compounds in the Library. Every fact here links to the fact sheet it comes from, and every fact sheet to its sources. No ads, nothing for sale.

The peptides FDA reviewed this year

In April 2026 FDA took a group of peptides off its Category 2 list of compounding substances with significant safety risks, after their nominations were withdrawn. Among them: BPC-157, TB-500, KPV, semax, epitalon, MOTS-c, LL-37, Melanotan II and injectable GHK-Cu.

That didn’t make them legal to compound. In July 2026 FDA’s compounding advisory committee voted to recommend allowing BPC-157 (8 to 6), TB-500 for wound healing (8 to 6), KPV (8 to 6), semax (8 to 5), epitalon (7 to 4) and MOTS-c (7 to 5), each against FDA staff’s advice. As of mid-October, FDA hadn’t added any of them to the list pharmacies may compound from.

An interim access policy for certain peptides went to the White House for review on October 14. It hadn’t been issued as of mid-October.

Where every compound stands with the FDA

GLP-1s

Orforglipron, a GLP-1 pill, was approved on April 1, 2026 as Foundayo, for weight management. It isn’t approved for type 2 diabetes.

Retatrutide is still in phase 3 trials, and Lilly says it plans to submit it to FDA in the first quarter of 2027. FDA says it can’t be used in compounding under federal law, and has sent warning letters over it.

Novo Nordisk submitted CagriSema (cagrilintide with semaglutide) for weight management in December 2025. No FDA decision had been found as of late October.

The shortages of semaglutide and tirzepatide are over, so pharmacies may no longer routinely copy them, and in May 2026 FDA proposed keeping both off the list outsourcing facilities may compound from.
